# Influencers — Handling replies

### INF-REPLY-01 — Get the audience data brands will ask for

Rule (must): Once a creator engages, ask for their media kit or, failing that, their audience split by age and country, because every brand will ask for it.
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "You'll need details about their audience and age because brands will ask."
Source: lesson video "2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology)" @42:52 — "I'll say brands typically require this data to review any deal we send to them, which is actually the case."

### INF-REPLY-02 — Reassure a wary creator honestly

Rule (must): Many creators have been scammed and get defensive. Explain plainly what you do for them (a specific brand, more partnerships, better-fitting brands), never invent experience, and show the deal carries no risk for them.
Do: Say what you are here for, concretely; Point out there is no risk on their side; Use case studies once you have them
Don't: Lie about experience or clients; Be sneaky about who you are
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "If you don't have experience, don't lie. Just be honest, and they will like it, you can get away by showing they won't have any risks on their end"
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "Lots of people scam influencers or steal their data info, resulting in them being very defensive when you approach them."

### INF-REPLY-03 — "No website / no domain / who do you manage?"

Rule (tip): When a creator doubts you for lacking a website, a domain or a track record, answer calmly and redirect: ask whether that is really a dealbreaker, say what is true (site in progress, onboarding creators for a brand campaign), or back it with a strong guarantee. If they still refuse, move on.
Steps: Ask whether the missing thing is a dealbreaker for them → State only what is true about your setup → Optionally offer a confident guarantee → If it lands, go on and ask for their details; if not, move on
Don't: Give a reason for having no website or domain that is not true (e.g. 'busy finishing a campaign' when there is none); Claim mentors, an incubator or clients the agency does not have
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "Ask them, "If I don't have this, is it a dealbreaker for you?" Would be sad to miss out on a couple thousands $$$ over a domain."
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "Tell them your website is under construction (if it's the case)"

### INF-REPLY-04 — "What do you have in mind?" — study them before answering

Rule (must): Before answering, find their strongest platform and what sells on it. Ask only a few quick questions (where they did most partnerships, their analytics or media kit, past deals, monthly capacity, rates) and research the rest from their channels. Their recent deals and analytics give you the facts to argue the price down. If they stay unconvinced, offer one trial deal.
Steps: Propose the format where they already do most partnerships (their strongest platform) → Name the kind of brands truthfully ('a few brands in mind' when none are signed) → Hand the ball back: before going further, their audience data (media kit or analytics screenshot) → Then a few qualifying questions: deals they can take a month, rates, recent sponsored posts → Keep the exchange short: each answer is information for the offer
Do: Research their socials yourself first; Ask a short, targeted set of questions; Use their own numbers when discussing price; Offer a single trial deal when they hesitate
Don't: Send a long questionnaire; Pitch the platform where they get few views
Source: 2.1. What to do when they respond — "if he does have a million views on YouTube and 300 views on TikTok, the brands won’t be interested in his TikTok account."
Source: 2.1. What to do when they respond — "You don't have to ask all of this. The function of this quick research is to see if he will be a potential influencer for brands to work with or not, so ask him quick questions, and some of the other research do it by analyzing his socials."

### INF-REPLY-05 — What the media kit must show

Rule (must): From the media kit or analytics, confirm three things before pitching: at least one of the four strong-buying countries (US, UK, Canada, Australia) in the top three audience countries, an age range that buys in this niche, and engagement.
Source: 2.5. What are media kits — "To get the influencer's analytics and see: - if the countries are with good purchase power : at least one of 4 good countries within top 3) - age (depend on the niche) - engagement (if you can't already see it on the profile) make sure you got this because brands will ask for it"
Source: 2.3. How do influencers get paid CPM vs Straight — "focus on channels which audiences are usually mature and in first world countries, like the US, Canada, Australia or the UK."

### INF-REPLY-06 — Be selective when they answer

Rule (should): When a creator replies, act like an agency with criteria: ask for analytics and past partnerships before committing, so they feel they have to qualify rather than that you will take anyone.
Source: 2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers — "Create the feeling that you don’t accept anyone here, and that there’s a criteria to pass."
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @8:37 — "I need to qualify you. I need to understand a bit more about you before even going to let's do partnerships"

### INF-REPLY-07 — The creator already worked with that brand

Rule (must): Ask before pitching: should they be left out, or do they want you to try for another deal? If the work was in the past and nothing is active, offer to revive it and pitch higher on their growth since (followers, views, engagement). If they want us to handle it, agree that we run that relationship from now on and that they pass any message from that brand to us. A brand that knows the creator's old price may check with them directly, especially when the price has gone up.
Source: 5.Influencer Already Worked With The Brand — "Always confirm with the influencer before pitching a brand they’ve worked with to avoid stepping on toes."
Source: 5.Influencer Already Worked With The Brand — "Leverage growth metrics (more followers, higher views, better engagement) to justify higher pricing if needed."

### INF-REPLY-08 — A creator says no or goes quiet: find the real reason

Rule (must): Do not assume it is the money. Check what it is — your website, the budget, the brand, the timeline — confirm it is the only issue, then address exactly that.
Source: 5.Effective Objection Handling in Sales — "If an influencer refuses to work with your company, don’t immediately assume it’s about the price."
Source: coaching call "6/2" (2025-02-06) — "try to figure out why, if he said it doesn't fit, how are you going to know? next one is not going to fit as well."

### INF-REPLY-09 — Creator not interested: two options

Rule (must): Accept it calmly, then ask whether they are stopping partnerships altogether or it is just the price that does not work — if they still do partnerships, address the real concern.
Source: 5.Turn a 'No' into 'Yes' with This Simple Technique — "are you planning to stop doing partnerships altogether, or is it just the price that doesn’t work for you?"
Source: coaching call "November 12th: Review/ Roast my email- Ammar" (2025-11-12) — "If they say no, I just keep. Most of the time, they're like, why? Would you explain? Just try to get an answer, but I'm not pushing anymore."

### INF-REPLY-10 — A creator bristles at the analytics request

Rule (should): When a creator answers the analytics ask with 'who are you, answer my question', stay calm and polite: say we are sorry it came across as dodging, answer plainly (the brands we are genuinely looking to pitch that fit them), and say we need their analytics to put them forward. Do not grovel or keep apologizing — that makes them bigger.
Don't: Over-apologize and beg to work together; Answer rudeness with attitude
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @12:57 — "Send him a message very simply, very calmly. You should always handle things calmly."
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @13:13 — "He said, We're currently looking at, didn't lie, didn't say we're working on deals with none of that. He said, We're currently looking at these brands. We would believe this would be a perfect fit for you. However, we just need to vouch you first with your analytics."

### INF-REPLY-11 — 'I already have an agency'

Rule (should): That is fine: say we work as an add-on, only with a few creators we pick, and ask for their minimum fee for the deliverable — the agency makes its margin on top and takes nothing from what they already earn (the discount path, INF-MGR-05).
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @23:39 — "our goal is just to be an add-on. So, we're completely fine with you working with an agency. Just let us know what is your minimal fee and we'll figure out how to make a profit on our end."
Source: coaching call "2/1" (2025-01-02) — "even though he said he was already in shifting with a couple of agency agencies, just take the normal route"

### INF-REPLY-12 — A student's experience: a wary contact may just need a call

Rule (tip): When a creator worries about being scammed, or a brand questions a plain email address, answer honestly (no website or domain yet, if so), offer a short call so they see the person behind the emails, and say you understand if it is a dealbreaker. Being straightforward is what reassures them.
Source: lesson video "6.How Suleyman Made 9.5k in 5 days" @26:55 — "Don't be shy to hop on a call because these guys, all they want from you in a call is just to see that you're not some dodgy guy."

### INF-REPLY-13 — A student's experience: corrected by the creator? Laugh it off and move on

Rule (tip): When a creator corrects an observation in the first message (e.g. 'almost all my videos are sponsored'), acknowledge it lightly with a touch of humour, keep the tone friendly and move to the next step (their analytics). Never argue the point.
Source: lesson video "6.How Abdelmenan Closed 4,500 on his first deal" @31:59 — "So I diffused it by just joking a little bit and making sure we're on good points, you know, good terms."

### INF-REPLY-14 — Weak audience countries in the analytics: raise it before any call

Rule (should): When a creator's analytics show most viewers in low-buying-power countries (e.g. only a few percent from the US), do not rush to a call or a price. Say calmly that these audiences are harder to place with brands and ask how they price around that. It qualifies them and shows the agency knows the market; never phrase it as an insult to a country.
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @2:26 — "Top four countries, India, Turkey, Brazil. There's only 8% that is in the US. All of these are the lowest purchase power countries you can have when working with influencers. So, this is a huge red flag. And what he said, out of neediness and desperation, he just said, Yeah, let's set up a call."
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @3:24 — "But if you go and you say, look, these countries are usually harsh. Harder to sell to brands. How do you structure the pricing around that?"

### INF-REPLY-15 — The creator does not qualify: close it briefly and politely

Rule (should): When the analytics or answers show the creator does not fit (audience, engagement, price), end the conversation in one or two polite lines — we won't be able to take it further for now, thanks for their time. No lecture, no explanation of what is wrong with their channel.
Source: lesson video "2.1. What to do when they respond" @12:19 — "If that doesn't work, by the way, if this is trash, just say unfortunately, we won't be able to assist you further. Thank you for your time."

### INF-REPLY-16 — Respond to what they sent before asking the next thing

Rule (should): When a creator sends analytics or answers, acknowledge it with a line that shows you read it (their main country, what that means for the brands you will pitch), then ask the next question. A bare 'thanks, now what's your price' makes the exchange feel transactional; a friendly human line now and then (their weekend, a shared interest) builds the relationship that makes later negotiation easier.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @32:23 — "I see the metrics. I understand what the metrics um I see that the top audience is US, so we'll be focusing more on brands that are targeting the US. I just need to understand a bit further what would be your sort of rate for integration as well as dedicated videos, for example."
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @31:35 — "But try to build a relationship with them to the point that they are more than happy to speak to you on a daily basis"

### INF-REPLY-17 — 'Which brand?' before they have shown anything: their audience first

Rule (should): When a creator replies by asking which brand it is before sharing anything, answer and qualify in the same reply: name the brands we plan to approach (INF-REPLY-26) and ask for their audience data (countries, age, recent views) so we can confirm the fit; the specifics of a real deal follow once it fits. Honesty: never suggest a specific brand is already waiting when none is (INF-FIRST-04, INF-FIRST-07).
Don't: Imply a specific brand is waiting when none is
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 2" @1:10:47 — "you're going to put that burden of proof onto them. You're going to say, Yeah, no worries. We would love to share all the details with you. But before we waste time going back and forth, let's just make sure that if you can send us over some demographics, you can send over some audience information. We'll see if it makes sense. If it makes sense, I'll give you all the details."

### INF-REPLY-18 — Ask for audience data with a reason, in plain words

Rule (must): Never ask for analytics cold. Say why: to see which brands fit their audience (some brands target the US, others the UK). Ask for 'audience analytics' (top countries, age, gender) rather than a 'media kit', which some creators do not know or think must be a formal PDF. If a reply is short or lukewarm, start with one easy question instead, such as how many sponsored videos they can do a month, and keep to one question per email. Never ask them for their engagement rate, click-through or conversion numbers: work out engagement yourself from their public numbers.
Don't: Tell the creator the exact audience you need before they send their analytics
Source: coaching call "9/6" (2025-06-09) — "one mistake a lot of students do when they ask for the media kit is not giving them a reason to give it."
Source: coaching call "September 15: Q&A - Younes" (2025-09-15) — "So just to be safe and to avoid, you know, the back and forth, you can just ask for audience analytics, like top countries, age, gender, et cetera."

### INF-REPLY-19 — No screenshots? Typed key numbers can be enough

Rule (should): If a creator will not send screenshots, take the key numbers typed out. When the top countries and ages are strong, ask for the rate and move on; ask for screenshots only if a brand wants proof. Without any country or age split we cannot place them with brands: say why it is needed, and if they still decline, wish them well and move on.
Source: coaching call "12/6" (2025-06-12) — "As long as they're good, as long as the percentage is high, then you don't really need to know the rest, to be honest."
Source: coaching call "12/6" (2025-06-12) — "Unless the brand, sometimes the brand specifically want actual screenshots because they think you're just making numbers up."

### INF-REPLY-20 — 'Who are you? Any past deals?': honest, short, no risk to them

Rule (must): Answer doubts about the agency briefly and truthfully. If there are no past deals yet, say so, then say what is true instead: we do the outreach and bring them brand deals at no cost and no risk to them, with no exclusivity, and they accept or decline each deal. Point to the website and, if there is one, the registered company, and send the link without apologising for it. Offer a short call so they see a real person. Never claim past campaigns, clients or brands we do not have (FND-ETH-01). If they keep demanding proof, it is fine to move on.
Source: coaching call "24/7" (2025-07-24) — "if the guy asks for previous partnerships, the best thing is just be honest, you know, I don't have at the moment, but again, no risk on you"
Source: coaching call "24/7" (2025-07-24) — "listen, I'm not planning on managing you exclusively. We can take things step by step, case by case"

### INF-REPLY-21 — How we work and earn: short and true, never the margin

Rule (should): Keep explanations of the agency short: creators understand from context, and long explanations invite demands for proof. Asked how we earn: the brand pays us for finding the right creators and handling the deal; the creator gets the fee we agreed, in full. Never state or hint at our margin, and never claim to earn nothing.
Source: coaching call "24/7" (2025-07-24) — "So simply said, you get the benefit from the brands by pitching multiple influencers, or bringing the perfect fits. Simple."
Source: coaching call "October 8th: Deal Breakdown & Strategy with kush" (2025-10-08) — "why do I need to explain everything? That's the thing. just make them clear. Hey, we agree on a flat fee, and we're going to pay you that."

### INF-REPLY-22 — Fully booked: book the months after

Rule (should): A creator who says they are fully booked is usually busy for real, and busy creators stay busy. Ask whether a later month is free and book it now, or ask when things open up; collect their analytics and rate anyway, because brands book ahead. 'Booked for the next few videos' is sometimes a polite no: their next uploads will show. Never pushy.
Source: coaching call "September 25: Q&A - Younes" (2025-09-25) — "just do a follow-up just ask is it okay if we can book something for november and december just ask him if he says yeah for sure then we can just start discussing medicaid and rates okay okay yeah because otherwise he is just closed right now i know i mean you can get back to him in november but the thing is if he is someone that gets booked a lot when you reach out in november he's gonna say yeah i'm fully booked november and december"
Source: coaching call "December 22nd: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-22) — "either he's actually fully booked, and, you know, and he's honest with you, he's fully booked, that way you just ask him, and that way he can give you back his details, because you might have, want to have sponsors in March and April. Or, it might be a nice way of telling you, I'm not interested, don't email me again."

### INF-REPLY-23 — 'No agencies' or 'we have our own team'

Rule (should): Many creators assume an agency wants to manage them exclusively. Say briefly that we don't: we bring specific brands that fit them, deal by deal, and they decide each time. Ask once, politely, what makes them avoid agencies. If it is still no, thank them and move on; don't argue or over-explain.
Source: coaching call "August 25: Q&A - Younes" (2025-08-25) — "maybe he thinks you're trying to manage him exclusively and doing all the work, but all you're trying to do is find good fits for the brand."
Source: coaching call "24/7" (2025-07-24) — "only thing I'm going to be helpful for you is to bring new brand deals, and it's up to you if you want to accept them or not."

### INF-REPLY-24 — Rude replies: stay calm, or let it go

Rule (should): A hostile no needs no answer. If our email may have come across badly, send one short, calm reply: apologise for how it read and offer a quick call to explain. Never answer in kind or escalate.
Source: coaching call "September 30: Brand Detective Hour - Dylan" (2025-09-30) — "just as long as you, you maintain your head up, you have good a clock, you don't like, you know, you don't escalate the situation"
Source: coaching call "August 27: Roast my Email - Ammar" (2025-08-27) — "it doesn't have to be as it seems like, my apologies if my email sounded like this"

### INF-REPLY-25 — No rate until there is a brand? Give them something concrete to price

Rule (should): When a creator will not quote before there is a brand, give them something concrete: the deliverable they usually do (a 60-second integration) and a likely window (the next month or two), and say we need the ballpark so we don't waste their time or the brand's.
Source: coaching call "December 25th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-25) — "It's going to be 60 second integration. That's what I see you do all the time. And most likely it's going to be in January, February. What would your rate be? Because I need to make sure there is a match because I don't want to waste the brand's time and I don't want to waste your time."

### INF-REPLY-26 — 'Which brands?': answer with real, fitting names

Rule (must): Answer the question; never dodge with categories. Name three or four brands that sponsor similar creators (look at who sponsors the bigger channels in the niche) and fit this audience, checked against the creator's own past sponsors. Present them as brands we will reach out to ('brands like X, Y and Z, and more'), never as clients (INF-FIRST-04). Never name a brand that does not sponsor creators. Then ask for their analytics to confirm the fit (INF-REPLY-17).
Source: coaching call "December 4th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-04) — "Don't give them categories because that doesn't answer their question. The biggest mistake students do is when someone asks you a question, don't avoid it. If they want to know specifically what kind of brands, that's not a secret."
Source: coaching call "December 4th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-04) — "Let's be transparent. I'm not trying to hide anything. These are the brands I'm going to reach out to."

### INF-REPLY-27 — A creator who takes us for the brand: say who we are

Rule (must): Say plainly that we are not the brand: we are onboarding creators for outreach to several brands, and we need a few details before any brief. Call ourselves an agency only once the company, website and domain exist; until then, an independent agent who helps creators get brand deals.
Source: coaching call "All Q/A Calls in November" (2024-11-07) — "So basically, how you go about it is telling him that you don't just represent one pen, for example, that you don't plan on representing just one pen or reaching out to one brand. But you're currently onboarding for, like, a session of outreach to different brands, and you need to get a bit more stuff for, you know, sending over any brief or anything."
Source: coaching call "3/2" (2025-02-03) — "what i recommend is to not say you're an influencer marketing agency unless you have company ready you have website ready and you have domain ready"

### INF-REPLY-28 — Never confirm a creator before the brand does

Rule (must): When a creator sends analytics or a rate for a specific brand, do not call them approved: thank them and say you will check with the brand. If the brand passes, say so with the real reason, and keep them for the next brand that fits.
Source: coaching call "December 10th: Review/Roast my email session-Ammar" (2025-12-10) — "and you don't say, oh, it's amazing, this is what we look for, thanks for sharing, let me check with the brand, and then you go to brand, they say, no, we have to pass, and you go back to influencers and be like, hey, based on your audience analytics, this and that, brands said we have, they have to pass, unfortunately, blah, blah, blah, you know, but still you close the influencer, you can find an expert for him"

### INF-REPLY-29 — We sent the creator something meant for the brand: own it once

Rule (should): If a message meant for the brand (the brand's price, our negotiation) reached the creator, apologise plainly once and offer a way forward: drop the deal if they prefer, or, if it still stands, a better rate for them. If they do not come back, let it go.
Source: coaching call "October 6th: Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-06) — "I totally understand, if you didn't like it, don't want it, if you want, I can just leave this, and, you know, close this"
Source: coaching call "October 6th: Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-06) — "If he doesn't want to work with you, he doesn't want to work with you. You've tried what you need to do"

### INF-REPLY-30 — Auto-replies: carry on as normal

Rule (tip): An automatic reply (out of office, 'we received your message') is not an answer: keep the normal follow-up sequence, and move on if there is still no reply.
Source: coaching call "February 23rd: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2026-02-23) — "just treat it as normal. Do your sequence, follow up sequence. If no response, move on."

### INF-REPLY-31 — Dry one-liners are normal: match them and give the next step

Rule (tip): A one-word 'Yes' or a blunt line is not a bad sign. Match their style (short, no greeting once the thread is going) and give the normal next step: the brands we plan to approach and the request for their analytics. When they are warm, be warm back.
Source: coaching call "March 30th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2026-03-30) — "Just, if he's quick, and easy, and straight to the point, just do the same."
Source: coaching call "March 30th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2026-03-30) — "it's okay not to say hi or hello every time."

### INF-REPLY-32 — Two kinds of exclusivity

Rule (should): When a creator or manager mentions exclusivity, check which kind they mean: management exclusivity (one agency alone represents the creator) or category exclusivity (after promoting a brand, no competitors for a period). Answer the one they mean. Before pitching a creator to a competitor of a brand they recently promoted, ask whether they have an active category exclusivity and when it ends: it usually runs for months (often about six), and it always has an end date.
Source: coaching call "October 22nd: Review/Roast my email- Ammar" (2025-10-22) — "Do you exclusively represent this guy? That means I'm his manager, only me."
Source: coaching call "October 22nd: Review/Roast my email- Ammar" (2025-10-22) — "You can only work with, I don't know, let's say BMW, you cannot work with Porsche"

### INF-REPLY-33 — 'I don't need a manager': we bring brand deals, not management

Rule (should): Creators often read a first email as an offer to manage them and decline on that basis. Clarify plainly that we're not asking to manage or represent them: we work on brands' campaigns, are looking at brands such as X for creators like them, and want to bring them paid deals; they keep running their channel and their other deals.
Source: coaching call "May 14th: Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-05-14) — "You can tell them that you're not reaching out to manage them, just tell them like, hey, we just are kind of looking into XYZ brands, and we want to help them out in their marketing campaigns"
Source: coaching call "June 4th: Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-06-04) — "you are not here to represent him or to manage him you're just here to offer marketing services for brands and we you know we came across your channel or something you know"
