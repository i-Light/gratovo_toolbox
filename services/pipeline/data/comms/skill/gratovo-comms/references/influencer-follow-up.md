# Influencers — Follow-ups

### INF-FU-01 — When to follow up

Rule (must): Follow up one working day after a first email that got no reply, and two working days after a stalled exchange. Tuesday and Thursday answer best. Never on weekends, never daily for a week; if the first email went out on a non-working day, let a working day pass first.
Source: 2.8. Follow-ups — "When to follow up ? Good way 👍 : ✅ After 1 working day with no response (on first email only) ✅ After 2 working days after first exchange ✅ Tuesday and Thursday are usually great responsive days"
Source: lesson video "2.8. Follow-ups" @3:46 — "most of the time your responses will come from follow-ups"

### INF-FU-02 — Six follow-ups that escalate, never copied

Rule (should): Escalate across short follow-ups: a gentle nudge; a slightly more direct check that they got it; a bump to the top of the inbox; a light question with a meme; owning that you have followed up several times; a humorous last touch. Write each one fresh: copied lines, including the course's own follow-up scripts, land in spam. The lesson allows up to six; how many the workflow sends is house choice HC-17.
Steps: 1 gentle reminder → 2 polite, more direct → 3 bump to the top → 4 any questions? (+ meme) → 5 own the repetition → 6 humorous close (+ meme)
Source: 2.8. Follow-ups — "First Follow-Up: This is a gentle reminder, nothing pushy. A simple nudge like: "Hey [Name], just checking if you had the chance to go through my last email?"
Source: 2.8. Follow-ups — "Fifth Follow-Up: Address the elephant in the room. You know you've followed up multiple times, so own it: "Hey [Name], I know this is like the fifth time I'm reaching out, but I'm dying to know what you think!"

### INF-FU-03 — Memes: once or twice, mid-sequence

Rule (tip): A meme or GIF can lift a follow-up, but only one, in the middle of the sequence, with a short professional line around it, and not in every sequence: images can now push an email into spam.
Source: 2.8. Follow-ups — "Stick to one or two in the middle of your follow-up sequence."
Source: coaching call "February 4th: Roast my email (Influencers)- Ammar" (2026-02-04) — "With that, you can end up in spam. So maybe every third, fourth influencer, that's working."

### INF-FU-04 — No answer after the sequence: wait, then a new thread and a new angle

Rule (should): When a sequence ends unanswered, wait a while (coaches say two weeks to two months) and start a fresh thread with a new subject and a different angle (a specific deal instead of a general partnership, or real new opportunities), written as a new first email with fresh personalization; it needn't mention the earlier emails. Then follow it up again: the lesson allows up to seven follow-ups, and how many the workflow sends is house choice HC-17.
Source: 5.Followed up 7 times, no responses, what to do — "Start a New Email Thread with a Different Approach: - Change the Subject Line: - Make it fresh and interesting. - Try a Different Angle: - If you first asked about partnerships, now maybe offer them a specific deal. - Or mention that you manage other influencers and have new opportunities. Follow Up Again: - Send the New Thread and Follow Up Up to 7 Times: - Keep trying different approaches. - Be patient but persistent."
Source: lesson video "2.8. Follow-ups" @13:26 — "I may send a new thread just following up on an actual previous message and being like, Hey, I actually sent you like 20 emails a month ago, but I saw maybe I was in spam, so I'm trying it out my chance again."

### INF-FU-05 — A ghosting creator: lead with real news

Rule (should): Re-engage with something true and concrete: a brand ready to work with them, or deals you recently closed for similar creators, or campaigns you are launching. Never claim a brand or a result that does not exist.
Source: 5.Followed up 7 times, no responses, what to do — "Wave Cash Under Their Nose: - Tell them you have a brand ready to work with them. (if it's the case) - If not, leverage other things you've been doing in the meantime. (closed new influencers, launching a campaign with XYZ brands, etc)."

### INF-FU-06 — A creator who said no months ago: come back with something new

Rule (tip): A no is not forever. When the agency has something new for a creator who declined (a fitting brand, a concrete deal), write again a few months later: remind them briefly of the earlier exchange and ask whether now is different. Only with real news, never the same pitch again.
Source: lesson video "1.1. Introduction to Cold Email - Coach Ammar" @1:23 — "Even if it's no, today's no can be transferred in yes, in a couple of months. Maybe. Let's say you are reaching out to an influencer. He says, No, I'm not interested. I got a bunch of these inquiries, blah, blah, blah. After a couple of months, you have a couple of brands, you have a good offer, and you'll be like, Hey, it's me, something, something. You know, when we spoke, you were not interested, what about now?"
Source: coaching call "November 12th: Review/ Roast my email- Ammar" (2025-11-12) — "You have a brand and now go back to him and mention the brand, the subject line, no personalization."

### INF-FU-07 — A follow-up only points back to the first message

Rule (must): Send follow-ups as replies in the same thread, and give them one job: get the first message read and answered. No new question, no new request (not even the media kit), no extra context. Short, light, sometimes with a touch of humour. The same holds for brands, a little gentler. This is the default of house choice HC-15; the alternative is INF-FU-08.
Don't: Start a new thread for each follow-up; Add a new ask to a follow-up; Say that a tracker shows they opened the email
Source: lesson video "2.8. Follow-ups" @6:51 — "a follow-up is not to ask a new question, it's not to remind them of something else, it's not to send another context. The sole goal of a follow-up is to follow up on the first email, is to tell them, hey, have a look at the first email. That's it. Listen again. The only goal of a follow-up is to make them read and answer the first email."
Source: coaching call "2/1" (2025-01-02) — "Yeah, even though you can see that opened it because they might have forgot, they might have opened and by accident, they might have many reasons. Don't just don't mention that you've seen it."

### INF-FU-08 — Alternative follow-up style: add one small, true piece of value

Rule (tip): Coach Osama's negotiation training asks that every follow-up carry value, never a bare 'did you see my email?': one short, true addition the reader can use, such as a recent result, a brand that fits, or a concrete next step. It still never rewrites the pitch (BRD-FU-06). The lesson's default is a follow-up that only points back to the first message (INF-FU-07); which style the workflow uses is house choice HC-15.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @40:31 — "And when you follow up, you follow up with value. You don't just follow up: hey, did you see my email?"

### INF-FU-09 — Coach Younes: two or three days apart, three or four follow-ups

Rule (tip): Coach Younes finds one day between follow-ups a little short (two or three days is the sweet spot) and stops after three or four follow-ups: most replies come between the first email and the fourth follow-up, and the time is better spent on new creators. The spacing is house choice HC-12, the count HC-17.
Source: coaching call "24/7" (2025-07-24) — "The sweet spot, I would say, is two to three days."
Source: coaching call "September 11: Q&A -Younes" (2025-09-11) — "And I would probably cap it at four follow-ups, although the course says you can do up to six."

### INF-FU-10 — Coach Osama: one day's gap, then daily follow-ups to creators

Rule (tip): Coach Osama leaves a creator one day after the first email and then follows up every day, which he found works better than every other day; agents are left alone at weekends. One of the options of house choice HC-12.
Source: coaching call "9/5" (2025-05-09) — "I've done half of a batch, following up every single every other day, or every three days, I see that it's not as efficient as if I follow up every single day, after having given them one day. We respond, so for example, I send my cold outreach today, tomorrow I don't, I leave them to see if they're going to respond, after that, I follow up every day."

### INF-FU-11 — Urgency only from real dates

Rule (must): A follow-up may carry urgency only from something true: the week we really start pitching brands, or a brand's real deadline ('the brand wants to settle this by Friday'). Never invent slots, deadlines or competing creators to rush them, never threaten, and never tell them to reply faster: it sounds needy and doesn't speed anyone up.
Source: coaching call "August 21: Q&A - Younes" (2025-08-21) — "So I would say follow-up, potentially do like an urgency follow-up, that's what I call it, where you place an urgency, say you're going to start reaching out now in a few days or next week, whatever."
Source: coaching call "30/7" (2025-07-30) — "I like to put some urgency. You know, if you, if you follow-ups or initial email, usually follow-ups, maybe it's better. You can put something like date, you know, it will be good if we can sell this one until the end of the week"

### INF-FU-12 — A brand is waiting and the creator has gone quiet

Rule (should): When a brand is ready and the creator stops answering, follow up daily. If the thread stays silent, start a new one whose subject says a brand wants to do a deal, and reach them on their other channels (an Instagram DM saying it's us and that we emailed). Give a real date after which we offer the spot to another creator, and line up an active replacement meanwhile. If it comes to that, tell the brand plainly that the creator isn't available and present the replacement honestly.
Source: coaching call "September 24: Deal Breakdown & Strategy - kush" (2025-09-24) — "He wasn't being very responsive, so I started a new email thread with, brands wants to do a deal. So, look, this problem-solving capability."
Source: coaching call "October 17th: Advanced Brands (Osama)" (2025-10-17) — "get in touch with the influencer via Instagram, he's got Instagram, send him a DM, say that it's you, and get in touch with him just quickly, or either you can voice note him on Instagram, no problem"

### INF-FU-13 — Quiet after we asked for analytics

Rule (should): Creators often go quiet after we ask for analytics simply because screenshots take effort and get put off. Follow up the next day with a light reminder, then every other day three or four more times, saying exactly what we need and why: the countries and age groups the brands we're approaching look for. Keep at it only with a creator who showed real interest; otherwise move on.
Source: coaching call "June 3rd: Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-06-03) — "If they just are not answering, most likely they're, you know, maybe, you know, be like, I'll do it later. Maybe they just forgot. So follow them up, inshaAllah. I usually go, if we have warm conversation, I usually follow up the day after"
Source: coaching call "September 17th- Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-09-17) — "So if you want to follow up, what you typically do is, uh, just ask for context, we are looking for these kinds of audience in these countries, in this age bracket, just to make it a bit more specific"

### INF-FU-14 — The last follow-up closes kindly and leaves the door open

Rule (should): End an unanswered sequence with a closing follow-up: no pressure, it's fine if now isn't the time, and they can reply whenever it makes sense. It often gets the answer the others didn't. Then stop; any later approach is a fresh email (INF-FU-04).
Source: coaching call "March 30th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2026-03-30) — "But, personally, I try to keep it open-ended. So, I use my last follow-up as my strongest follow-up. I use it as, you know, no pressure, just, okay, cool, no problem. If this wasn't the time, that's fine. Just, you know, if you do change your mind, let me know, because... I really want to book some spots."
Source: coaching call "August 30th - Follow Ups Workshop Influencer Imam" (2026-08-30) — "Then we get to the final follow up, which we send roughly three to five days after the second follow up. And over here is we want to keep the door open. We obviously don't want to be rude."

### INF-FU-15 — Coach Ammar: a new upload is a reason to follow up

Rule (tip): When the creator posts a new video during the sequence, Coach Ammar mentions it in the next follow-up together with the check on his earlier email; it shows the follow-up isn't automated. One of the ways of house choice HC-15 ('blend'); by default a follow-up only points back (INF-FU-07). Only a video the facts actually show.
Source: coaching call "16/7" (2025-07-16) — "if you're following up, let's say, and your influencer made a video. And, and, uh, and. You can mention that video in follow-up. Also, the last video you posted, just checking if you had time to review my email or something like that."
Source: coaching call "September 10: Review/Roast my Email - Ammar" (2025-09-10) — "You just say, I'm following up on my previous email, saw your last video about backflips, you know, we could probably expand or offer you more email, more brands. Let me know what you think about it, something like that."

### INF-FU-16 — Coach Izak: a creator who quoted a rate and went quiet on the analytics

Rule (tip): When a creator sent a rate and then goes quiet on the analytics request, Coach Izak follows up within a day or two and says plainly that we would be interested in working with the channel: creators who see real interest usually send their data straight away. Only when it is true: we would work with them if the audience fits, and nothing is promised before the numbers are in.
Source: coaching call "23/4 - Influencers Q&A" (2026-04-23) — "The second thing is, and this might, this could be strange, but show interest in working with them. Tell them like, we would be interested in collaborating with this channel. When they see this, they would go crazy."
