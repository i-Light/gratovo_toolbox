# Influencers — Closing

### INF-CLOSE-01 — What 'closed' means — and close several

Rule (must): A creator is closed when you hold their audience data and price list, the numbers leave you a profit, and they are ready to work with you (at a fair rate or under exclusivity). No contract is needed for that: an agreement on the rate by email is enough, and the creator's contract comes with a real brand deal (INF-CLOSE-02) or with exclusive management. Never pitch a creator to a brand before you know their price. Close several creators before starting brand outreach, so one falling through (illness, family, being booked, a brand saying no) does not stop the work. How many is house choice HC-16: the lesson says 3-4, Coach Younes 4-5, Coach Osama 5-7.
Source: 2.8. Closed an influencer, now what — "You have their audience information and pricing : This could be their media kit (or audience analytics data), as well as their pricing list for the different options they offer."
Source: 2.8. Closed an influencer, now what — "You just need to close 3-4 more influencers to not be limited to 1 option"

### INF-CLOSE-02 — Sending the creator their contract

Rule (must): Attach the contract, ask them to sign and send it back if all is good, say what follows (approval, tracking link, talking points), invite questions — then wait without rushing them.
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "Then wait. Don't rush them. 6. What happens after you send the contract: 1. Wait until influencer signs the contract 2. then sign the brands contract (Never Before) 3. then service delivery begins"
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "For influencers: Keep it clear, calm, and confident."

### INF-CLOSE-03 — The creator contract: seven must-haves

Rule (must): Every creator contract states: deliverables (exact platform, format, quantity); timeline (script, draft and go-live dates); rate and what starts the payment clock (invoice or go-live); usage rights (ads, and for how long); exclusivity after posting; maximum revision rounds (2-3); the obligation to disclose the sponsorship.
Source: 4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar — "Every creator contract needs these seven elements, no exceptions: - Deliverables. Exact platform, format, and quantity. - Timeline. Script due date, draft due date, and go-live date, all three. - Rate & payment trigger. How much they're paid and what starts the clock, invoice date or go-live date. - Usage rights. Whether the brand can use the content in ads, and for how long. - Exclusivity. How long the creator can't work with direct competitors after posting. - Revision rounds. State the max upfront. 2-3 rounds is the ceiling. - Disclosure. The creator's legal obligation to disclose the sponsorship."
Source: lesson video "4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar" @14:45 — "Always clarify how many rounds of revision."

### INF-CLOSE-04 — Exact wording, and the creator never sees the brand deal

Rule (must): Write deliverables exactly ('1 Instagram reel and 2 story frames'), date the contract the day it is sent, confirm the integration format (dedicated, pre-roll, mid-roll ≈ a third in, post-roll), and price usage rights with a duration. The creator's contract carries only their rate; the agency's deal with the brand stays separate.
Source: 4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar — "Deliverables need exact language, "1 Instagram reel and 2 story frames," not just "Instagram," since vague wording can be used against you."
Source: 4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar — "The rate in the creator contract is theirs alone. Your deal with the brand stays separate and the creator never sees it."

### INF-CLOSE-05 — Everything agreed goes in writing, payment terms included

Rule (must): Whatever was agreed by call, video or message, send the creator a written agreement with every term, deliverable and date, and state the payment terms plainly (when exactly they are paid, e.g. net 15 or 30). A little room for small adjustments is appreciated.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @56:33 — "Always send them written agreement with all terms, deliverables and timelines. This will protect you both not just you both. And then you set clear expectations from the beginning. There's a lot of issues, a lot of problems that come with payments. When am I gonna get paid? When am I exactly going to get paid? Especially payment, right? So clearly state the payment terms. Is it 15 net terms? Is it 30 data? Right?"

### INF-CLOSE-06 — Every term in plain words in the email, not only in the contract

Rule (must): Whatever the creator's contract contains (deliverables, whitelisting days, exclusivity period, usage rights) is also listed plainly in the email that comes with it, in two or three short bullet points, so the creator agrees to terms they have actually read. Never slip a term into the contract hoping it goes unnoticed, and always get the creator's agreement, or pay them, before their video is used in the brand's own ads. Whether the other extras are asked about first or stated as part of the deal is house choice HC-30.
Source: coaching call "March 31st: Advanced Brands Q&A - Osama" (2026-03-31) — "so, hey, influencer, the deliverables for this deal is as follows. This amount of reels, and they'll be whitelisting for 60 to 90 days, and, you know, for further opportunity to work further with them. And then, know, then... They go from there, and then anything else that is within the contract. Just, you know, just two to three bullet points, and that's it, and then just let me know if all is okay, and I'll send you the contract through."
Source: coaching call "8/7" (2025-07-08) — "By usage rights, you obviously, you always get permission from the influencer, or pay for it, basically, if they're going to use it for marketing."

### INF-CLOSE-07 — Coach Osama: state whitelisting and the usual exclusivity as part of the deal

Rule (tip): Coach Osama doesn't ask a creator's permission for whitelisting or for the usual 30-day exclusivity: he lists them plainly among the deliverables and says the contract follows; a creator who objects says so, and only then does it go back to the brand. Longer exclusivity and usage rights are still raised with the creator and paid for. One of the options of house choice HC-30; the default is to ask the creator first (BRD-NEG-26).
Source: coaching call "March 27th: Advanced Brands Q&A - Osama" (2026-03-27) — "I'll just put the whitelisting in the contract, send it to the influencer, let him read it. If he's got any issues, he'll let you know. And then if he's got any issues, then you can raise those issues to the brand."
Source: coaching call "8/7" (2025-07-08) — "you don't need to ask an influencer, no one needs to ask an influencer for exclusivity for 30 days. They should know that, and on the contract, regardless, you will have exclusivity closed for 30 days."

### INF-CLOSE-08 — Contract sent, not signed yet

Rule (should): Most creators sign within their usual reply time. After about two days, check lightly that they've seen it and whether anything in it needs a word. Say why it comes first: signing before filming protects them too. No product ships and no address goes to the brand before the signature. If a brand date is close, give a real deadline after which the spot goes to another creator (INF-FU-12).
Source: coaching call "All Q/A Calls in December" (2024-12-12) — "You can follow up like two days. So I'm probably, you can just check like, hey, it's everything all right with the contract. Have you managed to see it yet? No problem."
Source: coaching call "March 31st: Advanced Brands Q&A - Osama" (2026-03-31) — "So, you know, for your protection, or, you know, to be covered from all aspects. Just make sure that you sign the contract before you go ahead and spend your valuable time on producing the video"

### INF-CLOSE-09 — WhatsApp: ask with a reason, best once a deal is there

Rule (should): Ask for a creator's WhatsApp number with the reason attached (quicker contact about brand deals, the details for the contract), and best once a real deal is on the table; an unexplained request for a phone number from someone with no deal yet looks odd.
Source: coaching call "July 29th: Brands Q&A with Himzo" (2026-07-29) — "I usually get their WhatsApp number once, like, the deal is actually there, and there's a reason. You know, you're sort of just asking them, like, you know, here's, you know, what's your, can you provide me with your WhatsApp number?"
Source: coaching call "June 23rd Brands Q&A (Osama)" (2026-06-23) — "When you ask for a phone number, don't just say, hey, give me your phone number, right? You have to sweeten up the deal. Why do you want that phone number? You know, you want the phone number for faster access, for faster response, et cetera, because you might have a deal to close then and there, right?"
