# Glossary (from the course)

Common Influencer Marketing Terms

# Summary

This guide explains key terms like exclusivity, usage, timeline, production date, and placement in influencer marketing. Each term is detailed with examples, helping you understand how to negotiate effectively with brands.

### Influencer Marketing Terms Explained

#### Exclusivity

Exclusivity can mean two things:

1. Managing an Influencer Exclusively
  - You act as their exclusive agent.
  - Handle both inbound and outbound deals for them.
  - Makes it easier for brands to trust you since you represent the influencer directly, avoiding potential messes like price disputes or delayed deliverables.
  - Example: You tell a brand, "I exclusively manage this influencer, so all deals go through me."
2. Brand-Specific Exclusivity
  - Influencers work exclusively with one brand in a niche for a set period.
  - Example:
    - A beard oil brand wants Mr. Beast to advertise for them but doesn’t want him promoting other skincare or beard oil brands during the agreement.
    - This ensures the audience doesn’t lose trust because he isn’t promoting similar products repeatedly.

#### Usage

Usage defines how the content created by the influencer can be used by the brand.

- Basic Usage: 90-second integration with no strings attached.
- Expanded Usage:
  - Restrictions like no competing brand collaborations for a certain time.
  - Full rights to reuse the video for ads.
- Example:
  - Mr. Beast promotes Honey in his video. The brand then pays him for the ad rights to keep running his endorsement as skippable YouTube ads.

#### Timeline

Timeline refers to all key dates related to content creation and posting.

- Include specifics like:
  - Contract signing and payment deadline.
  - Product shipping date (if needed).
  - Draft submission date for review.
  - Final posting date.
- Example:
  - January 1: Contract signed and payment received.
  - January 5: Product received for video production.
  - January 15: Draft submitted for approval.
  - January 25: Final video goes live.

#### Production Date

- When the video is being created or recorded.
- Make sure all products are shipped in advance so they’re available for filming.
- Example: If the video integrates a physical product, delays in shipping might force influencers to reshoot parts of the video to add the product, which isn’t ideal.

#### Placement

Placement refers to where in the video the brand’s ad or mention appears.

- Options include:
  - Pre-roll: Mention at the very beginning of the video.
  - Mid-roll: Integrated in the middle after a topic is introduced.
  - Post-roll: Near the end of the video.
- Example:
  - For an iPhone 16 review, the influencer might first discuss the phone to build hype, then transition into discussing the sponsored app.
  - Alternatively, the sponsor could appear right at the beginning before any iPhone details are revealed.

### Summary

- Exclusivity: An agreement where either an influencer is exclusively managed by one agent or promotes only one brand in a specific category for a defined period.
- Usage: Defines how a brand can repurpose the influencer’s content, including rights for advertising and limiting collaborations with competing brands.
- Timeline: A detailed plan outlining key dates for signing contracts, submitting drafts, and publishing content.
- Production Date: The date when the content is created, ensuring all necessary products are available beforehand.
- Placement: Specifies where in the video the sponsorship will appear, such as at the start, middle, or end.

These terms help establish clear expectations and smooth collaboration between influencers and brands.

Acronyms Resource List

## The Money Terms:

- CPM = Cost per thousand impressions (yes, M = mille = thousand)
- CPA = Cost per acquisition (how much to get one customer)
- CPC = Cost per click
- CPV = Cost per view
- ROAS = Return on ad spend (if you spend $1, how much comes back)
- CAC = Customer acquisition cost

## The Performance Terms:

- CTR = Click-through rate (clicks ÷ impressions)
- CVR = Conversion rate (purchases ÷ clicks)
- EMV = Earned media value
- ER = Engagement rate (likes + comments ÷ followers)
- AOV = Average order value

## The Deal Terms:

- SOW = Statement of work
- IO = Insertion order (the actual contract)
- Exclusivity = Can't work with competitors
- Usage rights = How long brands can use your content
- Whitelisting = Letting brands run ads from your account
- Net 30/60/90 = When you actually get paid

## Campaign Types:

- Flat fee = Fixed payment regardless of performance
- Performance-based = Payment tied to results
- Hybrid = Flat fee + performance bonus
- Affiliate = Commission on sales only
- Seeding = Free product, no payment

## The Platform Terms:

- UGC = User-generated content
- FTC = Federal Trade Commission (they make you write hashtag#ad)
- MCN = Multi-channel network
- KPI = Key performance indicators (what success looks like)
