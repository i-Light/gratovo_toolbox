# Influencers — First contact

### INF-FIRST-01 — Four parts, always in this order

Rule (must): A first email to a creator has a subject line, a personalization, an offer and a call to action, in that order; a DM is a shorter version of the same structure.
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar @5:07 — "Every influencer email follows exactly four parts in the same order: Subject Line, Personalization, Offer, and CTA."

### INF-FIRST-02 — Personalization proves you watched

Rule (must): Reference one specific moment of a recent video that could not be pasted into an email to anyone else; find it by watching a few minutes, the intro and outro, and the most-replayed part. Never use a generic AI summary of the channel.
Do: Pick one concrete, checkable detail from a real video; Keep it to a sentence that leads into the offer
Don't: Write praise that fits any creator; Open with an AI-style overview of the channel
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar @10:45 — "If you can copy-paste it to any other influencer, it's a template."
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar — "ChatGPT gives generic overviews that influencers spot immediately."

### INF-FIRST-03 — The offer is the point of the email

Rule (must): Everything before the offer only exists to get the creator to it. Frame it around their audience: what the people watching them would buy. When a brand offer for them is really in hand, say so up front: it shortens the process and lifts the close rate (never when there is none, INF-FIRST-04).
Don't: Tell the creator they are worth a lot more or deserve bigger deals: it inflates the rate they ask
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar @16:50 — "The offer is the most important part of the email. Everything before it exists to get the influencer to this moment."
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 1" @38:20 — "Change your outreach a little bit when you're dealing with influencers. Approach it by letting them know you already got offers from brands that are ready to be taken."

### INF-FIRST-04 — No brands yet: never lie, name two that fit

When: The agency has no brand deal in hand for this creator.
Rule (must): Do not pretend to have brands. Look at what similar creators in the niche promoted in their last videos and name two brands that fit this creator's audience but that this creator has not worked with yet.
Steps: Open 5-10 creators in the same niche → List the brands in their last 5 videos → Pick 2 that fit this creator's audience and are not in this creator's own videos
Don't: Claim or imply the agency already works with those brands
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar — "If you have no brands yet, don't lie. Open 5-10 influencer profiles in your niche, list every brand they've worked with across their last 5 videos, then name 2 brands that are a strong fit for the creator you're emailing but that they haven't worked with yet."
Source: lesson video "2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar" @19:16 — "Find two brands in your niche that would be a great fit for that creator in specific."

### INF-FIRST-05 — End on one yes-or-no question

Rule (must): The last line asks one short yes-or-no question whose only job is a reply. Offer 'more details' only when the details really exist.
Do: Alternative close that saves a step: if they are interested, ask them to send their analytics to check the fit
Don't: Ask for anything that takes work in the first email; Offer to send details you do not have; Offer to send a 'brief' when no brand has given one
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar @23:12 — "The CTA is the last line of your email. Its only goal is to get a reply. Keep it short, simple, and framed as a yes-or-no question."
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar — "Avoid "Should I send more details?" unless you actually have details ready to send."

### INF-FIRST-06 — One question per email

Rule (must): Ask exactly one question. A list of questions reads as a survey from a stranger; if you ask something beyond the call to action, make it about their business pain.
Don't: Several questions in one first email
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "A list of questions is a survey, and five questions from a stranger gets you blocked."
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "One question per email, every time."

### INF-FIRST-07 — No brands yet: sell the service, honestly

When: No brand in hand.
Rule (must): An honest offer is either the service ('I find brands that fit your content') or two fitting brands named as brands in their space (INF-FIRST-04) — never a claimed relationship. A vague offer collapses the moment they ask for budget, timeline and deliverables.
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "Don't lie about having brands. They'll ask for budgets, timelines, and deliverables, and you'll have nothing to give them."
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "Sell the service: "I can find brands that fit your content.""

### INF-FIRST-08 — Calls to action that push work onto them are out

Rule (must): Never close with lines that make the creator do the work ('you know where to find me', 'send me the brands you'd like'). The close is a yes-or-no question answerable in a second.
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — ""You know where to find me" and "send me a list of brands you'd like" both push the work onto them."

### INF-FIRST-09 — The six checks before sending

Rule (must): Every first message passes six checks: reads as written by a person (not AI), sounds like a partner not a fan, about six sentences readable in 20 seconds, one question only, a clear and honest offer, a yes-or-no close.
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "Run through all six before you press send: - Did I write this myself, or does it read like AI? - Do I sound like a fan or a partner? - Is it around six sentences, readable in under 20 seconds? - Is there only one question? - Is my offer clear and honest? - Is my CTA a yes or no question?"
Source: lesson video "2.1. Setting expectations" @4:57 — "Influencers have an anger meter. So basically, let's say like water boils at 100 degrees. Well, influencers get mad and stop replying and stop and decide to not work with you at, I don't know, let's just call it five extra questions or three too many questions, right? And how it works is basically if you ask them too many things, or if you do too many claims, or if you make it too hard for them to entertain a conversation with you, they'll just get angry or just stop responding to you."

### INF-FIRST-10 — Write as an agency onboarding creators for real outreach

Rule (should): Be specific about the real opportunity — the brands you are pitching or the outreach you are preparing — and say you are onboarding creators for it. That frames you as offering an opportunity and avoids 'who are you?' back-and-forth. Only describe plans that genuinely exist.
Source: 5.Smart Aikido Trick for Influencer Outreach — "Mention you’re onboarding influencers for upcoming outreach."
Source: 5.Smart Aikido Trick for Influencer Outreach — "Instead of being the one asking for something, you position yourself as offering an opportunity."

### INF-FIRST-11 — Text-style first touch: ask who handles their deals

Rule (tip): One 2026-style option: a two-line note with a specific detail from a recent post, then a quick question about who handles their brand deals — it surfaces managers early and reads like a person.
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "Hey {name}, Saw your last post with {brand}. Loved how clean the transition was. Quick q who handles your brand deals?"

### INF-FIRST-12 — Spark the need with a true observation

When: The facts show the creator's recent videos carry no sponsor
Rule (tip): A first creator message can point to a checked, true gap (their recent videos have no sponsorship) as a plain observation followed by whether they're open to some: 'your last few videos weren't sponsored; open to a couple?'. Never ask why they have none, never frame it as a problem we will fix, and with a large creator assume it is their choice and simply ask whether they're open to a deal. If they say yes, the natural next question is what kept those videos unsponsored; the answer gives the agency its reason to step in.
Do: Every sponsored video was the same brand: ask whether that is loyalty or a lack of brands that interest them; Sponsors that do not fit their content: ask whether better-fitting brands would interest them
Don't: Claim a gap that was not checked in the facts; Make the observation sound like mockery; Ask why they have no sponsors; Name a brand they already worked with, or call a sponsored video unsponsored, to provoke a correction
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @35:19 — "I saw your last five videos didn't have a collaboration. Are you open to partnerships?"
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @34:31 — "Hey, bro, who just show your channels pretty cool. I was just curious, are you open to partnerships? This is my favorite opener when it comes to influencers. It's just simple, it's straightforward."

### INF-FIRST-13 — What creators want — speak to the one the facts show

Rule (should): Creators want, in order of how often: more money; more time on their content and no brand emails or calls; and brands that fit, so they never feel they are selling their audience something wrong. Pick the one their channel suggests (few sponsors, mismatched sponsors, a heavy upload schedule) and make the message about it.
Source: lesson video "1.5. Marketing crash course" @1:43 — "For influencers, the guy may be interested in money, which to be honest is like 99% of the cases"
Source: lesson video "1.5. Marketing crash course" @2:07 — "maybe they find the right brands for their content so that they feel like they're not scamming their audience"

### INF-FIRST-14 — The creator's latest video is about a loss

Rule (tip): If the recent video is about a death or a hardship, keep any condolence short and sincere, never use it as the hook for the sale, and say plainly that this may not be the moment — then ask about later (e.g. next month). Flippant or salesy lines around a loss read as disrespectful.
Source: lesson video "8.Abu Lahya Reviewing Emails" @24:12 — "I'm assuming this isn't the right time, but I'm still sending it out."
Source: lesson video "8.Abu Lahya Reviewing Emails" @25:23 — "Honestly, this whole email just feels disrespectful."

### INF-FIRST-15 — Recruiting creators for a brand that asked the agency

Rule (should): When a brand has asked the agency for creators, do not claim to represent the brand; say the agency works closely with it (true in this case) and is lining up creators for its upcoming campaign. Naming the brand in the subject line draws attention. First check that the creator has not been sponsored by that brand before.
Source: lesson video "8.Abu Lahya Reviewing Emails" @27:53 — "I don't say that I represent the brand. This is this can backfire definitely. Rather, I say I work closely with this brand, right? And also mention the brand in the uh subject line because that creates curiosity, definitely."
Source: lesson video "8.Abu Lahya Reviewing Emails" — "That the influencer hasn't been sponsored previously by the brand. This could backfire."

### INF-FIRST-16 — A bridge line from the personal hook to the offer

Rule (must): Do not jump from the personal opening straight to the offer: the first sentence of the offer picks up one element of the opening and turns it toward what the agency does, so the email reads as one thought.
Source: lesson video "2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar" @21:19 — "Bridgeline is the first sentence of your offer that still kind of is attached to your personalization."
Source: lesson video "2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar" @21:48 — "I just pick one thing from the last sentence that I mentioned and have it in my first offer."

### INF-FIRST-17 — High-demand creators: spark curiosity about how they do integrations

Rule (tip): For a creator who already has plenty of sponsors and ignores standard pitches, an experienced approach comments specifically on one of their recent integrations and asks whether they have considered sponsorships that do not feel like ads, then offers a couple of fitting brand ideas. Only with real insight into their content.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @27:51 — "I was watching X and I saw how you made this integration, right?"
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @28:27 — "have you ever considered partnering with a brand in a way that doesn't feel like a brand deal?"

### INF-FIRST-18 — A shared recent topic can be the hook for a group of creators

Rule (tip): When several creators covered the same recent topic (e.g. a new phone launch), that topic is a genuine, specific hook for each of them. The hook can be shared; the wording of every email must still differ (FND-WRITE-14).
Source: lesson video "2.2. The best way finding influencers organically" @11:56 — "20 YouTubers who post about iPhones and posted about the iPhone 16, for example, I can send them the same email, just changing the name."

### INF-FIRST-19 — Coach Kushtrim: end on an easy statement, not a question

Rule (tip): Coach Kushtrim avoids question CTAs and 'if' clauses and ends on an easy statement that leans toward yes ('Let me know and I'll send the analytics over'). The lesson and most coaches end on one yes-or-no question (INF-FIRST-05); which applies is house choice HC-20.
Source: coaching call "August 13: Review/Roast my email - Dylan" (2025-08-13) — "What I do is, for example, you know, let me know and I'll do this. Let me know, I'll do that. So I make it easy on them to respond."
Source: coaching call "August 27: Roast my Email - Ammar" (2025-08-27) — "I usually don't like to question CTA, you know, like asking things in the CTA."

### INF-FIRST-20 — Greet a solo creator by name

Rule (should): Greet a solo creator by their first name: it is usually in a video intro, their Instagram profile, the comments, or a PayPal or Buy Me a Coffee link. When it can't be found, greet them by the name they go by online (their channel name or alias); creators don't mind it. 'Hi team' suits a channel run by several people. Never guess a name. When the case gives no first name for a solo creator, use the channel name and flag the missing name for the person approving.
Source: coaching call "28/1 - RME (Influencers) w/Ammar" (2026-01-28) — "Hey there. Oh, guys, so if you cannot find their name, yes, but try to do it, inshaAllah. What you can do, go to their YouTube channel. Sometimes he says, hey, my name is Rick, I'm doing this and that. Sometimes you can find it in the comment section. But the best one, what I saw is Instagram. Just go to their Instagram."
Source: coaching call "August 6: Roast my Email - Ammar" (2025-08-06) — "go to his Instagram page, okay, if he has PayPal, usually PayPal or Buy Me Coffee thing, they have the whole name of this guy, also, where we can, yeah, comments, go to comment section"

### INF-FIRST-21 — Say exactly how far along each brand is

Rule (must): Match the words to the facts for every brand named. No contact yet: 'we're looking into X' or 'we're thinking of pairing you with X', never 'we work with X'. In talks, or holding the brand's brief: 'we're planning a campaign for X'. A signed deal: 'we're working with X', and then say it plainly, since a real deal is the strongest reason to reply. Never word a first email as if a deal were ready (a rate, a brief, a slot waiting) when none is.
Don't: A separate sentence or paragraph confessing that nothing is signed: 'we're looking into X' already says it, plainly and honestly
Source: coaching call "June 22nd: Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-06-22) — "So, you need to be, you need to give him a few brand names. Even if you don't work with them, it all depends on the wording. For example, you can tell him, like, we're thinking about XYZ brands. We're looking into XYZ brands. We're thinking to pair you with XYZ brands. You know? You don't say, like, we work with XYZ brands."
Source: coaching call "May 06: Student Success Breakdown - Abu Numaan" (2026-05-06) — "You're planning a campaign, you're planning for this brand. They give you a brief, and you're planning a campaign for them, so you're planning a campaign for them, and you're not lying. So that's the wording I would use. But as soon as you close to deal with them, you can say, we're working with them, or we work with, right?"

### INF-FIRST-22 — Name brands that sponsor creators this size, as brands we plan to approach

Rule (should): Name two or three brands that sponsor creators of a similar size in the niche (the sponsors in similar creators' recent videos), not household giants that rarely sponsor creators (Apple, Samsung) or brands that only pay the biggest names or work on affiliate only: those make a new agency look inexperienced. Check the creator hasn't worked with them, and present them as some of the brands we plan to approach, so a creator who already worked with one simply asks which others we have. Vary the names across emails.
Source: coaching call "October 20th: Influencer Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-20) — "if I tell this influencer, I'm going to reach out to Apple, Samsung, you know, all of these big brands, I'm going to sound like the best agency ever. But realistically, they're just going to look at you, someone who's inexperienced."
Source: coaching call "October 20th: Influencer Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-20) — "So you can just pick one influencer you have in your list, go through his past videos, note down three or four brands that he's worked with and use that when reaching out to other influencers. And most likely those brands are not huge like Logitech and whatever. And if you mention these brands, you're going to sound more experienced, more, uh, what's it called, trustworthy"

### INF-FIRST-23 — Instagram DMs: say what it is first, keep it to two lines, move to email

Rule (should): Email comes first; a DM is for creators whose email can't be found. A message request shows only its first line, so start with what the message is about (for example 'Paid collab:') before the greeting: a bare 'Hey James' looks like one more fan message. Keep the DM to one or two casual lines: one true detail and whether they are open to sponsorships. Don't ask for a media kit or rates in a DM; ask for their email so the details can follow there.
Source: coaching call "August 16th - Influencers Q&A with Imam" (2026-08-16) — "He's only going to see Hey X, which is going to stuff you up because he's just thinking, it's another fan email saying like, oh, thank you or whatever. But what you can do is before Hey X, just add something like this to a paid collab opportunity. And this will basically appear in the first line."
Source: coaching call "May 14th: Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-05-14) — "Just I could mention one thing, know, like, hey, I noticed you worked with NordVPN in your latest video. Would you be open to your sponsorships? That's it. You could keep it simple, short, sweet."

### INF-FIRST-24 — 'You could be a good fit', never 'these brands want you'

Rule (should): Before seeing a creator's numbers, write that they could be a good fit, not that they would be perfect or that a brand is perfect for them: certainty is salesy and gives away our position. Never write that brands are interested in working with them: it is rarely true at first contact, and it tells the creator the brand is the one to talk to. Say that we are looking to pair them with brands such as X (INF-FIRST-21).
Source: coaching call "September 12th- Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-09-12) — "You would be a fit. You might be a good fit. Why? Because you are not sure. If you are sure, you will have some context. But no, if you do call outreach, don't say this. Basically, you're giving up too much power."
Source: coaching call "November 7th 2025: Brands (Advanced)" (2025-11-07) — "Well, if you say, look, this brand is perfect for you, like, it's a bit too salesy, right? Like, could you reach out to them? No, you could be a good fit for this brand."

### INF-FIRST-25 — A live campaign: lead with it

Rule (should): When a brand deal is really in hand, say so in the first lines: the brand, what it needs (for example a 60-90 second integration) and when ('filling a campaign for X in November'), adding 'as well as a few other brands' in case they have already worked with it. Keep one short personal line even then: it shows the message isn't from a bot. A reply-by date only when the brand really has one; no invented urgency. Wording follows INF-FIRST-21 and INF-FIRST-15.
Source: coaching call "July 8th: Brands Q&A with Himzo" (2026-07-08) — "Now your outreach email will be, hey, I'm filling this campaign for this brand in November. Like, let me know sort of thing. So it's straight to the point and that'll definitely increase your response rate."
Source: coaching call "25/4" (2025-04-25) — "I wanted to connect with you, or just outreaching to you, as my agency is currently running a few campaigns for the brand, for this brand. And then, I also like to say, as well as a few other brands"

### INF-FIRST-26 — The personal line: their latest video, one point, never retold

Rule (must): Base the personal line on their latest video (anything from the last 30 days works; the latest is best because they remember it). Make one point, casually: never retell the video to them, and don't open with 'I watched' or 'the way you…'. When the facts give only a video's title, react to what the title promises without claiming to have watched it.
Source: coaching call "August 9th - Influencers Q&A With Imam" (2026-08-09) — "anything that's within the last 30 days, but I prefer to just do the last video. The reason being because, of course, he remembers everything technically in that video if I reference a specific part."
Source: coaching call "June 28th: Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-06-27) — "This is what I do. Intro, outro, maybe one moment, or look for the spike, you have video title, and two or three comments, that's all, exactly TK, don't explain the video back to them"

### INF-FIRST-27 — Our own brief voice, whoever the creator is

Rule (should): Write to every creator in the same brief, friendly, direct voice: don't switch to academic prose for a doctor or slang for a gamer. Everyone is busy and wants the gist in a few seconds. This is the default of house choice HC-28; the alternative is INF-FIRST-28.
Source: coaching call "23/7" (2025-07-23) — "don't make the mistake that if you're writing to a doctor, or if you're writing to like an academic type YouTuber, that you're gonna write a more academic type email, everyone is busy, right"
Source: coaching call "July 15th: Brands Q&A with Himzo" (2026-07-15) — "so if it's like a very initial interaction with influencers, try and be as professional as possible. I've become bros bros with some of my influences, but that's after like a little bit of time. So I've done a deal with them, I've paid them"

### INF-FIRST-28 — Coach Ammar: a casual first email that matches the creator's style

Rule (tip): Coach Ammar keeps the first email very casual and matches the creator's style and language (a light joke for a playful creator), becoming more professional from the follow-up on: a creator who recognises their own style reads the sender as one of their own. One of the options of house choice HC-28; the default is our own brief voice (INF-FIRST-27).
Source: coaching call "September 24th: Review/ Roast my Email- Ammar" (2025-09-24) — "I usually stay casual with influencers. don't make it too professional until the follow-up. I usually stay very casual for the first email to make it easy. And then, yeah. Okay. If you match style, if you match language, they'd be like, oh, this guy, this is familiar. This is my guy."

### INF-FIRST-29 — Real results: one plain line in the first email, details later

Rule (should): Once the agency has real results, one plain line of proof can go in the first email ('we just closed a sponsorship for a creator like you'); it works best with creators similar to the one we delivered for. Keep the details (numbers, names) for when they hesitate or ask, and never inflate them. Without real results, say nothing of the kind (INF-FIRST-04).
Source: coaching call "June 13th: Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-06-13) — "Yeah, yeah, use it in your initial email. Because you have something to offer. You have, you know, case studies are for that one. Like, look at this. It's proof of my work."
