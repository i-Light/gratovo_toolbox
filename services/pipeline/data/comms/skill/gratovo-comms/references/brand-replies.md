# Brands — Handling replies

### BRD-REPLY-01 — Affiliate or ambassador proposals: decline

Rule (should): When a brand counters with an affiliate or ambassador deal, steer back to a paid sponsorship or move on; especially early, sponsorships are what pay.
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "Brands pitching affiliate/ambassador deals. Ignore them, especially when starting. Sponsorships are the quickest path to making money."
Source: lesson video "3.1. Setting Expectations" @21:26 — "you rather spend that time instead of trying to negotiate, affiliate, and do a whole bunch of things. You rather just try and close all the brands that actually got money and focus on them."

### BRD-REPLY-02 — Expect chaos: CCs, ghosting, backdooring

Rule (should): Managers CC others and nobody answers, brands go silent after receiving a creator list, a few try to go around you to the creator. Be precise and valuable enough that working through you is the obvious choice, protect the relationship in writing, and move on when it happens (it is rare).
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "Managers ccing five other people, and no one gets back to you."
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "Make Offers Specific and Clear: Be so precise and valuable that brands see working with you as the only logical choice."

### BRD-REPLY-03 — When they ask about the agency

Rule (must): Keep it short and about expertise, and only state what is true (brands worked with, creators managed, the niche you specialize in). If they do not ask, do not volunteer it — keep the conversation on the creator's strengths and social proof.
Don't: Claim brands, creators or results the agency does not have; Long paragraphs about the agency
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "If they don’t ask about you, don’t volunteer unnecessary details."
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "Always center the conversation around the influencer’s strengths and achievements instead."

### BRD-REPLY-04 — Speak as the creator's side, never as a loose connector

When: Every statement about the relationship with the creator must be true (agreed with the creator).
Rule (must): Do not signal weak control ('these creators are interested'): speak as 'we', reaching out on the creator's behalf, when that is what was agreed. Do not present yourself as someone just connecting dots — brands bypass middlemen. Asked about exclusivity, answer confidently and truthfully: we represent the creator for this opportunity and work closely with them; never claim an exclusive representation we don't have.
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "reveal that you're just connecting dots. If the brand feels like you're a middleman, they may try to bypass you."
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "We represent them for projects like this and collaborate closely to ensure the best results for brands like yours."

### BRD-REPLY-05 — 'Send more information' = sell the creator now

Rule (must): They want audience size, demographics, content examples and ideas for working together. Answer with substance: the scope (e.g. an integration in an upcoming video and why it fits), audience stats, the content track record, and a confident next step.
Do: The creator's name with a link to the channel, the few numbers that matter (subscribers, average views, engagement, audience where known), and two or three sentences on why they fit this brand, then leave them room to think
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "Can you send us more information about your influencer(s)?" What they're really asking: - Audience size. - Demographics. (country, age, habits and preferences) - Content examples. (where you could integrate the brand) - Ideas on how you can work with the brand. IMPORTANT : GIVE MEAT TO YOUR EMAILS, IT'S TIME TO SELL YOUR INFLUENCER !!!"
Source: lesson video "3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do" @25:50 — "make sure you give meat to your emails. It's time to sell your influencers. And with this, I mean stop sending like one-line, just numbers thing."

### BRD-REPLY-06 — 'We only do X' = a precise offer for X

Rule (must): When a brand names one deliverable, answer with exactly that: the format (e.g. a 60-90 s integration), the rate and what it includes (production, posting, reporting); other formats only as an optional extra.
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "We're only looking for [specific deliverable like YouTube sponsorships]." What they're signaling: - Narrow focus-be precise with your offer. - They want clear deliverables and pricing."
Source: lesson video "3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do" @29:13 — "if they're saying we're specifically looking for YouTube sponsorships, oh, amazing, Tom is excellent for YouTube sponsorships."

### BRD-REPLY-07 — 'Pausing campaigns' is a stall, not a no

Rule (must): Do not push. Acknowledge the timing, keep the door open, and ask roughly when a new campaign would make sense so you can prepare — then follow up at that time.
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "We're pausing campaigns for now." What they mean: - Not a rejection but a stall-don't be pushy. - Keep the door open for future opportunities. - You can score big if you keep them engaged and re-engage at the perfect timing."
Source: lesson video "3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do" @30:38 — "And by the way, this is a test. When they're sending you those kind of emails, when they're saying, hey, we're stopping this, we're posing this, we can't spend money, they are actually testing you. They're testing how you're going to respond to these. By the way, it's subconscious. They don't even do this purposely. They just send you this and then they're going to look at how you respond. And if you are super pushy, whatever. Deal is dead."

### BRD-REPLY-08 — Someone was CC'd: take it from there

Rule (should): When a founder hands you to a colleague by CC, wait a day, then write to that person directly (glad to meet, here is where we are) and, when appropriate, assume the close (ready to move forward, waiting on their go-ahead).
Source: 3.6. PART 2 Objection Handling Full Training — "When a CEO CCs another team member, you can use this opportunity to keep the momentum going."
Source: 3.6. PART 2 Objection Handling Full Training — "Follow up directly with the CC'd person: - "Hi [Name], great to meet you! [CEO's Name] mentioned you'd be handling this campaign. Here's where we left off-looking forward to your thoughts.""

### BRD-REPLY-09 — Unsure what a reply means? Analyse it before answering

Rule (should): When a brand's reply is ambiguous, work out what it likely means, which objections may sit behind it, and the reply that moves the conversation forward, then answer. When a term is used loosely, ask rather than assume: UGC is content for the brand's own channels, not a sponsorship on the creator's; an 'initial test' means one to three paid videos, not a free one; 'demos' means audience demographics.
Source: 3.5. How to Use AI Like a Pro — "Explain what this response likely means, what potential objections they might have, and the best way for me to reply to move the conversation forward."
Source: lesson video "3.5. How to Use AI Like a Pro" @1:51 — "Explain what this response likely means, what potential objections they might have, and the best way for me to reply to move the conversation forward."

### BRD-REPLY-10 — Brand wants everything at once: send a shortlist of three at most

Rule (must): Brand reps push for the whole roster, rates and analytics in one go. Slow down: send one or two creators (three at most) inside the email body; a sheet only when pitching five or more.
Source: 4.1. Working With the Brand - Coach Ammar — "Send a shortlist of three creators maximum, ideally one or two, because a long list makes the decision harder and cheapens every name on it."
Source: 4.1. Working With the Brand - Coach Ammar — "Send three or fewer names inside the email itself, and switch to a Google Sheet only if you're pitching five or more."

### BRD-REPLY-11 — Creator not available: tell the brand at once, with alternatives

Rule (must): If the chosen creator is booked or refuses the rate, write to the brand immediately: the creator is not available for this window, alternatives are ready, may you send a revised shortlist?
Source: 4.1. Working With the Brand - Coach Ammar — "Creators get booked or turn down the rate. Go back to the brand immediately rather than sitting on it. Tell them the selected creator isn't available for this window, that you have alternatives ready, and ask whether you can send a revised shortlist."

### BRD-REPLY-12 — When to propose a call, and how to run it

Rule (should): Big brands rarely take calls; mid-sized brands are worth a relationship call after one or two campaigns; small brands, nervous about spending, often close after a call. Prepare 15 minutes, plan 30, open casually for a few minutes, state the agenda, ask what they did with creators before and what they expect, and set realistic expectations so you can beat them.
Source: 5.Masterclass on calls — "Big brands like NordVPN generally don’t do calls."
Source: 5.Masterclass on calls — "Smaller companies might be more nervous about spending money and might need a call to feel comfortable."

### BRD-REPLY-13 — 'Not open to partnerships': look at why before answering

Rule (should): Do not interrogate a brand that declined. Look at its history: no money (an early startup) — leave it for now; burned by earlier creator deals — find the likely reason (e.g. those creators carry so many sponsors their audience stopped trusting ads) and answer with a creator who avoids it and a lower-risk first step. Only say what the research really shows.
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @10:00 — "So you can just go and do your homework, look at what they've done and see why they're not doing partnerships. Maybe they're just broke, they don't have any money, and in that case, just leave them alone."
Source: lesson video "3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands" @34:00 — "If they told you about they got stung before because this influencer didn't get good results, you can go do some digging, find the numbers that were wrong."

### BRD-REPLY-14 — A student's experience: 'we don't do paid collaborations right now'

Rule (tip): When a first contact says the brand is not doing paid collaborations and asks for your socials, answer plainly what you are (an independent agent working deal by deal for a creator) and that the proposal is a paid collaboration; then follow up. The answer can change once you reach the person who owns the budget.
Source: lesson video "6.How Abdelmenan Closed 4,500 on his first deal" @48:59 — "I'm an independent agent that works with influencers on a deal by deal basis. Was reaching out for one of my influencers to potentially showcase one of your products on a video."

### BRD-REPLY-15 — Answer everything they asked, then end with one forward question

Rule (should): When a brand asks for details (content, platforms, terms), answer each point clearly and make the content concrete and attractive (what the video will show, which selling points it covers). Then do not close with 'looking forward to working together': end with one simple question that moves it on (which platform suits them best, when they would like to start).
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @15:21 — "instead of looking forward to working together, I would have asked. So, which platform do you feel would be the best for you?"
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @13:44 — "If you just said, hey, we'll just do a TikTok shot, a TikTok or a YouTube short, or whatever. Not really interesting. Whereas if you say, we'll cover all the aspects about your product, all the selling points that are important, we do lifestyle shots and this and that. And now it's interesting."

### BRD-REPLY-16 — Asked for a creator profile you don't have? Say so

Rule (should): When a brand describes the creator it wants (age, country, niche) and none on the roster fits, say so plainly and promise to come back once you have the match — then go and find them. Never send a misfit just to answer.
Don't: Tell a brand with a small budget that we will go and hunt for creators in its range: find them quietly and come back when there is a match, or move on (many such brands cannot pay)
Source: lesson video "3.4. Qualifying Your Inf. Brands - Coach Ammar" @28:30 — "It's okay if you don't have a good guy, it's okay to let the brand know. At the moment, we don't have those. Let me get back to you as soon as I get them."
Source: coaching call "October 8th: Deal Breakdown & Strategy with kush" (2025-10-08) — "I wouldn't frame it that way. Like, I would do it privately. I would just go and find some influencers myself, in this range, and just get back to him whenever I have them."

### BRD-REPLY-17 — 'We can send the creator free product'

Rule (should): A product in exchange for a video is not a deal for the agency or the creator: ask for a fee. Only a very expensive product may count toward the price (product plus a reduced fee). Always some cash up front. This is the default of house choice HC-31; the alternative is BRD-NEG-31.
Source: lesson video "3.3. Brands and their objectives" @17:37 — "Same thing with free products, or like, unless it's a mad expensive thing, unless you want to send me like a 5,000 bed or like a car or like an entire PC, I'm not doing a free video."
Source: lesson video "3.3. Brands and their objectives" @18:15 — "But make sure you're always getting cash upfront because that's what you want as an agency"

### BRD-REPLY-18 — 'Please write from your company email' (fraud concerns)

Rule (should): Treat it as a security policy, not a rejection: acknowledge it warmly, comply if the agency has a company address, and if not, say so honestly and ask what they need to verify you so you can move forward. Never claim credentials or past brand work you do not have.
Source: lesson video "3.5. How to Use AI Like a Pro" @2:34 — "Acknowledge is a super important one when people give you objections."
Source: lesson video "3.5. How to Use AI Like a Pro" @3:57 — "let me know exactly what you need from me to move forward and I'll get it sorted ASAP."

### BRD-REPLY-19 — Interested, or asking for the roster: send the creators now

Rule (must): When a brand says it is interested, asks for recommendations or asks to see the roster, answer with the specific creators and their key numbers straight away, not with questions about what they need: they expect us to be the experts, so work out their product, audience and shipping countries ourselves. A casual 'always open to more creators' is the opening to pitch. Read what they answer: 'send your roster' after we pitched one creator usually means not that one (often the budget). Ask about goals only if they pass on the creators sent or ask broadly how we could help. If an earlier question went unanswered, follow up by simply sending a few creators.
Source: coaching call "February 21st: Roast my email (Brands)- Ammar" (2026-02-21) — "Okay, so once when they say we are interested, don't give them, don't ask them any more questions. Go straight away with your offer."
Source: coaching call "22/4 - RME" (2026-04-22) — "What I usually do, if they ask me for roster, I give them, like, this is what you want, nice. If they are, if they say, like, no, we have to pass, then I will go, like, okay, what are your goals"

### BRD-REPLY-20 — Asked for details or the rate: answer the question

Rule (should): When a brand asks for more details about a creator, send four things: the channel link, the audience analytics, the rate and the availability. When it asks for something specific, such as the rate, give exactly that; never answer with a question about their budget, which makes their job harder. When steering a brand from the creator it asked about to better-priced alternatives, put the prices and the standout audience numbers in the same email. The cold first email still carries no price (FND-WRITE-18). Whether the rate goes with the first details or only once they like a creator is house choice HC-22 (BRD-REPLY-26).
Source: coaching call "October 21: Brand Detective - Dylan" (2025-10-21) — "I think giving the price up right away is pretty good, because I mean, it's like, you know, price is the big factor"
Source: coaching call "14/1 - Deal Breakdown & Strategy w/Abu Numaan" (2026-01-14) — "If it makes sense to pitch it before that, pitch it before that."

### BRD-REPLY-21 — Answer a brand promptly, even when we have nothing yet

Rule (must): Never leave a brand's reply unanswered for days while sourcing or waiting on a creator. Reply promptly: acknowledge exactly what they need, say we are checking and will come back shortly, or, if no matching creator is available, say so plainly and that we will come back as soon as one is. Don't explain internal hold-ups such as a slow creator, and never send a close-enough creator in place of what they asked for. If our reply is late anyway, one short apology and straight to the substance. Some coaches instead stay silent while sourcing (BRD-REPLY-22); which applies is house choice HC-21.
Source: coaching call "13/5" (2025-05-13) — "It's not professional for a brand to get back to you and you don't you don't get back to them it's like you're ignoring them basically"
Source: coaching call "13/5" (2025-05-13) — "i understand that you want this at the moment i don't have any influencers available that meet your criteria i have them available i will let you know"

If it takes longer than a week, he writes to say he is on it. Both sides agree on one thing: never send a close-enough creator in place of what they asked for. ### BRD-REPLY-23 — Asked about our fee: the price is all-in

Rule (must): If a brand asks what the agency charges, answer truthfully without opening up the margin: our price is the all-in rate for the creator's content, and we work within their budget per thousand views to find the right creators. Never quote a percentage fee we do not charge. (One coach suggests quoting a 'standard 10-20%'; house reading, ethics first: no invented numbers.)
Source: coaching call "October 29: Q&A w/Younes" (2025-10-30) — "think just saying, we usually stick to the brand's budget and find matching or find the right influences for you, that kind of thing."

### BRD-REPLY-24 — Brand and creator stay in separate threads

Rule (must): Never copy the creator into the brand's thread, or the brand into the creator's: we represent the creator and handle the communication. If a brand copies in the creator we are pitching, ask politely that it not happen again.
Source: coaching call "October 6th: Brand Response Rescue - Michael" (2025-10-06) — "There's no reason to CC him, because you're already in communication with him."
Source: coaching call "22/7" (2025-07-22) — "But for you to CC in the influence that I'm talking to you about, it's a bit unprofessional, and I would be really appreciative if you didn't do this in future conversations."

### BRD-REPLY-25 — Referred to a colleague: write to them and say who sent you

Rule (should): When a contact points you to a colleague (an out-of-office naming a backup, or 'X handles this'), write to that colleague now and say who sent you; if they were copied in, reply in the same thread so they can see the referral. Only name someone who really referred you (FND-ETH-11). If no one else is named, note the return date and write again then. If the colleague you were passed to stays silent, politely tell the person who referred you that you have written to them and not heard back.
Source: coaching call "March 26th: negotiations" (2026-03-26) — "Hey, um, this guy was out of office, um, and he referred me to reach out to you"
Source: coaching call "29/4 - RME" (2026-04-29) — "hey, I spoke with Michael. He said we should reach out to you and present you a roster of our influencers."

### BRD-REPLY-26 — Coaches Ammar, Ahmed, Osama and Michael: creators first, analytics next, price last

Rule (tip): Several coaches answer a brand's first 'send details' or 'show us your roster' with the creators only (channel links and a line on each), then the analytics once the brand likes one, and the price only after that, so the brand is invested in the creator before seeing a price tag. The lesson and other coaches send the rate with the details and answer a direct price question with the price (BRD-REPLY-20); which applies is house choice HC-22.

### BRD-REPLY-27 — Asked for a shipping address first: the paid fee comes first

Rule (must): If a brand wants to ship a product before any fee is agreed, say plainly, before giving an address, that the creator makes videos for a paid fee only and that receiving the product does not mean a video. Product-only gifting campaigns need dozens of creators to make sense, so they are not for a small roster.
Source: coaching call "23/6" (2025-06-23) — "we'll receive the product, but that doesn't mean we're guaranteeing you that we're going to make a video, we only do videos in exchange for a flat fee"
Source: coaching call "March 31st: Advanced Brands Q&A - Osama" (2026-03-31) — "So, because one influencer is not enough to do that sort of campaign. You need like a good 50, maybe like 20 to 50 influencers to do something like that."

### BRD-REPLY-28 — A request outside our niche: keep the door open, keep the focus

Rule (should): When a brand asks for creators far outside our niche, thank them, say we will come back when we have a fitting creator, and keep working our own niche. Take it on only if it is a quick one-off that will not pull focus: every new niche brings creators who then need brands.
Source: coaching call "August 18: Q&A - Younes" (2025-08-18) — "Thank you for showing me. I'll get back as soon as I have someone that fits."
Source: coaching call "August 19: Q&A ( Advanced Brands) - Osama" (2025-08-19) — "it's good short term, you'll be like, okay, cool, that's fine, I got deals for these people, made profit, good, but long term is a headache, because you have two niches now to focus on"

### BRD-REPLY-29 — The brand's marketing agency answers: treat it as the brand

Rule (tip): When a brand's outside marketing agency replies, work with it as with the brand's own staff and never try to go around it. Expect sharper pricing and harder negotiation; some agencies control large budgets and will hand the creator sourcing to us once they trust us.
Source: coaching call "All Q/A Calls in November" (2024-11-14) — "they're just going to be smarter when it comes to pricing and they know a bit more. And yeah, that's it. It doesn't make a big difference. It's like instead of having an employee, you have an agency."
Source: coaching call "All Q/A Calls in December" (2024-12-19) — "Sometimes the agency is actually the one spending so much money, and they're not super smart agency, so they actually make me do the service, and I spend all the money."

### BRD-REPLY-30 — A 'brand' writing from a personal address and asking for creators: verify first

Rule (must): When someone who says they represent a brand writes from a personal address (Gmail and the like) and asks for creators or the roster, don't send creator details yet. Ask politely why they aren't writing from the company's domain, and suggest a short call to learn exactly what they need. Rosters sent blind can be phished, or used to go around the agency.
Source: coaching call "Negotiations 18.12.2025" (2025-12-18) — "If I would respond to him, I would ask him to hop on a call. Like, okay, but by the way, why are you not using your domain?"
Source: coaching call "Negotiations 18.12.2025" (2025-12-18) — "How about we jump on a quick call first? And how about we do this first? Let me know what you're looking for exactly so I know which profile to send over and this and that."
