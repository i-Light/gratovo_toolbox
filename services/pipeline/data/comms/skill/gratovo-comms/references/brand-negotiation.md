# Brands — Negotiation

### BRD-NEG-01 — Margin: negotiate creators down, mark up to the brand

Rule (must): Profit comes from buying the creator below market and adding a markup (the course gives 30-70%) on the price to the brand. Never quote the brand a creator below the price that creator first asked you: you can come down later, never up.
Do: Anchor the brand on the creator's original ask or higher
Don't: Pass a negotiated discount straight to the brand
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Add a markup (30–70%) to the negotiated price for the brand."
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Even if you negotiated your influencer down, never pitch him to the brand for lower than what he pitched you first."

### BRD-NEG-02 — Agency share: 20-30% of the deal (commission model)

Rule (should): When the agency works on commission rather than markup, its share is about 20-30% depending on deal size; a lower share is only for the first deals that build a portfolio.
Source: 2.3. How do influencers get paid CPM vs Straight — "On both options your commission range will be around 20% - 30%, taking into account some facts like the size of the deal."

### BRD-NEG-03 — Deal types with brands

Rule (must): Sell flat fees for clear deliverables, or CPM deals for awareness (negotiate the cap and a longer monitoring window, up to 90 days). Avoid affiliate deals. If a brand insists, offer a hybrid (upfront fee plus commission, both shared with the creator). Avoid product-only barter unless the product is very valuable.
Source: 3.3. Brands and their objectives — "As An Agency, We Try and Avoid Affiliate Deals: - What It Is: The influencer earns a percentage of sales or a flat fee per conversion. - Challenges: Requires trust in the brand and clear tracking mechanisms."
Source: 3.3. Brands and their objectives — "If The Brands Really Insist, We Offer Hybrid Deals: - What It Is: A combination of flat fees and affiliate commissions. - When to Use: When brands want to minimize upfront costs but still incentivize performance."

### BRD-NEG-04 — Never absorb a lowball alone

Rule (must): If the brand comes in low, do not eat the loss: go back to the creator, explain the brand's constraint, and ask whether they can adjust a little to make a good opportunity work — as a team.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "If the brand lowballs, don’t take the loss yourself. Go back to the influencer and explain the brand’s budget constraints."
Source: lesson video "3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands" @49:38 — "If the brand lobbles you, you don't wanna take the loss yourself and be like, oh, it's fine, I'll shoulder it. You go back to the influencer, you explain the brand's budget constraints, and you frame it as a team effort."

### BRD-NEG-05 — A deep lowball is often a test

Rule (should): Stay firm and calm; the brand is often probing your real floor. Justify the budget with add-ons (a story or short-form mention, a view guarantee) — and only offer what the creator has already agreed to.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Most of the time, they're testing you to see what your actual max is."
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Guarantee a certain number of views (e.g., "100k views guarantee"). Can help with trust issues. (make sure the influencer agrees to the same guarantee before pitching it"

### BRD-NEG-06 — Flat rate stuck? Offer pay-on-results

Rule (tip): When a brand balks at a flat rate, propose a CPM (pay-per-view) structure based on performance.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "If a brand struggles with flat rates, offer a cost-per-mille (CPM) structure: "Would you be open to a pay-on-results system? We could structure it based on performance metrics like views or engagement.""

### BRD-NEG-07 — Is the brand's offer fair?

Rule (must): Compare it with CPM and fee benchmarks, adjusted for the creator's engagement, size and niche fit. If it is low, keep the door open: explain why the creator is worth more, ask what their concern is, and counter with a number anchored on what similar campaigns cost.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "How to Know If a Brand's Offer Is Fair - Compare it to industry benchmarks (CPM rates, average influencer fees). - Consider the influencer's engagement, audience size, and niche relevance. - If it's a lowball, don't close the door to them but rather explain why he's worth more and what their concerns is ? Then offer a counter offer that is representative of his worth."

### BRD-NEG-08 — Protect margin: start high, add value instead of cutting

Rule (must): Pitch slightly above your target to leave room. When pushed, keep the fee and add an extra (with a real reason why the fee cannot drop: production cost, the creator's standards, market rates).
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Always pitch slightly above your ideal rate so you have room to negotiate while still hitting your margin goals."
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Instead of lowering the price, bundle extras: - Example: "We actually can't do $X for a video because (insert valid reason, like production cost, or the influencer standards, or the market standards). That being said, we understand your budget constraints and really want to make things work, so we're willing to do an exception and include an additional Instagram post at no extra cost to maximize reach. on the campaign.""

### BRD-NEG-09 — Anchor high with the brand

Rule (must): Start above what you will accept: pitch the creator at their original ask (or slightly above) even after negotiating them down, and never below their ask unless it was absurd. Stay inside the niche's natural CPM range and under any budget the brand has named: brands read CPMs at a glance, and a price above the range gets ghosted rather than negotiated. With a brand you already work with, pitch only a little above your target and hold it.
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "Always start with a higher price than what you’re willing to accept."
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "Never pitch below the influencer’s asking price unless it’s absurdly overpriced."

### BRD-NEG-10 — Small budget suspected: signal flexibility, not discounts

Rule (should): Do not open with a discount because a brand looks small; say you are open to finding a win-win if the opportunity fits.
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "If you suspect the company doesn’t have a big budget, avoid looking desperate by pitching discounts upfront."

### BRD-NEG-11 — ROI-hesitant brand: bundle to lower the unit price

Rule (should): When a brand can pay but doubts the per-video price, offer a bundle (e.g. three videos for less per video) — then renegotiate the creator's rate for the bundle so the agency is not the only one giving ground.
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "Sometimes brands aren't broke but hesitant about ROI (Return on Investment). In such cases: - Offer bundles to sweeten the deal. - Example: If a single video is $5k, propose 3 videos for $12k (bringing it down to $4k per video)."
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "If they accept, go and ask the influencer for a discount again based on the bundle (for ex here if we was accepting 3k for 1 video, make him accept 6k for 3 videos) To ensure you're not the only one taking the L."

### BRD-NEG-12 — Lowball with potential: counter a little above, frame as a first collaboration

Rule (should): Decide whether the brand is worth it. If yes, get the creator's agreement by framing it as a first collaboration with standard rates later, and counter slightly above the brand's number rather than accepting it outright. If the creator holds firm, say you need to stay near their standard rate and leave room to adjust.
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "I know you don’t typically do this, but this could be a great first collaboration, and we’ll aim to raise the price for future deals."
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "I've spoken to [Influencer Name], and while this is below their typical rate, we're happy to start with $ 3500 this as a first collaboration. (don't leave money on the table, and show you got standards, if you accept their offer directly, looks needy"

### BRD-NEG-13 — Justify the price with the creator's value

Rule (must): Back every price with why the creator is right (engagement, niche authority, audience match) and why the brand wins (credibility with its target, a natural fit in the content, expected ROI).
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "Always justify the cost by showing the value the influencer brings to the table."
Source: lesson video "3.1. How To Make Offers That Close Instantly" @14:08 — "Here's the engagement we have with this guy. Here's the type of engagement your guy had. I think this is one of the reasons why you didn't get results. It's also why we're charging above market, but because we're getting results above market. Those little objection handling. Now, I didn't do any magic. I just show them it makes sense. We're charging more because you get better results."

### BRD-NEG-14 — A brand with too little budget: trade deliverables, not value

Rule (should): Say the creator usually works with higher budgets, show why it still makes sense, ask whether the budget can move, and offer to lighten deliverables or timing to meet in the middle.
Source: 3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands — "Let us know if there’s flexibility in timing or deliverables, and we can adjust accordingly."
Source: lesson video "3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands" @18:32 — "For example, it could be instead of having the video delivered that soon, they get it delivered later on. Instead of having full choice on which video is going to be on, it's going to be on whatever video has a spot, right?"

### BRD-NEG-15 — Keep prospecting, and be ready to walk away

Rule (must): Never pause outreach because one brand is interested. Before pitching, decide the minimum rate, the margin you need and what is a bad fit; walk away from insulting offers, deals with no margin and wrong fits — it protects the reputation.
Don't: Threaten to take the creator to the brand's competitor
Source: 3.SECRET SAUCE Brand Negotiation - Coach Kushtrim — "When a brand responds to you, keep sending emails to other brands, so don't assume this brand will close"
Source: 3.SECRET SAUCE Brand Negotiation - Coach Kushtrim — "Being willing to say "no" prevents bad deals and bad relationships."

### BRD-NEG-16 — Pitching a discounted creator low loses money

Rule (must): After negotiating a creator down, still pitch the brand at the creator's original price or higher: brands negotiate down and you can never raise a quoted price. Students who pitched in between lost $1,000 and $5,000.
Source: 5.How to pitch influencers (lowball version) — "One student lost $1,000, and another lost $5,000 because they pitched the brand too low and ended up cutting into their profits."
Source: 5.How to pitch influencers (lowball version) — "Always pitch the brand the same price or more than the influencers original price."

### BRD-NEG-17 — Several objections at once: lock, adapt, add value

Rule (should): Lock in what is already agreed, offer an in-budget alternative where a piece is too expensive (an integration instead of a dedicated video), justify a higher rate with added value (a guarantee pre-agreed with the creator), and use availability only when it is real.
Source: 5.Handling Multiple Objections in Negotiations — "Always keep the deal alive: If the initial offer doesn’t work, propose an alternative that aligns with the brands needs or budget."
Source: 5.Handling Multiple Objections in Negotiations — "Create urgency where possible: Highlight limited availability to encourage faster decision-making. BE CAREFUL WITH THIS."

### BRD-NEG-18 — Small percentages on big deals: add value to move the number

Rule (tip): On larger deals (over about $3K), small moves matter: a 10% lower creator fee or a slightly larger brand spend justified by added value (another creator, an extra service) can nearly double the margin.
Source: 5.Increase Profits by Adding More Value — "To increase your profit margins, focus on adding value to the deal without significantly increasing costs."
Source: 5.Increase Profits by Adding More Value — "In larger deals (over $3K), start negotiating for small amounts—just a 10% reduction on influencer fees or a $10K increase in the brand’s budget."

### BRD-NEG-19 — State a price cleanly — never 'but it's negotiable'

Rule (must): When asked for a rate, answer in two or three lines: what is on offer (the format), the price, and when it can run. Never add that the number is negotiable or that we could go lower — that invites the cut before they have even asked. Anything said about availability or other bookings must be true.

### BRD-NEG-20 — Brand offers less: a smaller deliverable at their number, or the same price

Rule (should): When a brand says it can only pay less than the quoted price, offer what fits their number (a smaller deliverable, e.g. a story instead of the integration). If they still want the full deliverable, hold the price calmly and explain what it includes — only things that are true for this creator (their production, scripting, editing). Never lower the price without lowering the scope.

### BRD-NEG-21 — Price a niche creator by what the niche brand gains

Rule (tip): A niche creator who gets low offers from general brands can be worth several times more to a brand whose buyers are exactly that audience: a thousand of the right viewers is worth far more to it than a thousand random ones. Pay the creator their usual rate and price the deal to the specialist brand by the value of that audience — this is the agency's margin.
Source: lesson video "3.2. How to Make Attractive Offers" @28:52 — "this is where you get your arbitrage. If this guy usually gets offers from like these goofy random brands, but like chocolate brands, NordeVPN, e-sim cards, whatever, and they offer him two grand, three grand, because it's just like general stuff. You go super specific, you still pay him two grand, but you go and sell it to that brand that's super in need of that type of audience and to them. A thousand eyeballs from this guy is extremely valuable."

### BRD-NEG-22 — A student's first brand negotiation: what worked and what he would fix

Rule (tip): From a $9.5K first deal: pitch the brand above the creator's ask; when the brand proposes a CPM deal, ask for a fixed fee with a true reason (the creator's production costs); move down in small steps, each with a reason — a big drop at once looks needy; justify the price with specific audience facts (country, age, buying power), never just 'a great fit'; and get every requirement of the brief (placement in the video, timing) before settling the creator's price.
Steps: Pitch above the creator's ask → CPM counter: ask for a fixed fee with a true reason → Concede in small, reasoned steps → Justify with specific audience facts → Lock the brief's requirements before the creator's price

### BRD-NEG-23 — A student's experience: bridge a price gap with a view bonus

Rule (tip): When a brand will not reach the price, a base fee plus a bonus once the video passes a view threshold can close the gap — only with a bonus the creator has agreed to and a person has approved.
Source: lesson video "6.How Suleyman Made 9.5k in 5 days" @32:48 — "And then I came back to him with 5.8 plus$850 bonus if the video passed 300k views."

### BRD-NEG-24 — Concede in small steps, and only for something

Rule (must): Brands expect a counter and keep room above their first offer, so counter at least once; a negotiation usually takes four or five emails. When we come down, come down a little (a few hundred dollars, about 5%); dropping thousands at once tells the brand the first price was inflated and costs its respect. To go lower than that, take something away: a shorter integration, a later placement, a smaller view guarantee, or a different creator. Never lower a price in a follow-up to silence or before the brand has asked.
Do: The other way works too: a longer integration (or an extra placement) for a higher price, once the creator has agreed to it
Don't: Leave the currency of a price unstated, or let a brand assume a different one; Explain a higher price by the season ('Q4 rates')
Source: coaching call "October 11th: Deal Breakdown - Michael & Kamal" (2025-10-11) — "they have explicitly told me, never accept the first price. I ask them why. Because they always leave room for 15 to 20% up."
Source: coaching call "All Q/A Calls in December" (2024-12-05) — "You send one, which is the price. They counter with the price. You counter back with the price. And then they accept or they counter once more just in the middle"

### BRD-NEG-25 — Guarantees and caps: defined and priced up front

Rule (should): A view guarantee is a negotiation tool, never part of the first email: offer it when the brand pushes back on price or wants performance, as the alternative to a pure CPM deal that does not cover the creator's production costs. Price it: a guarantee at the creator's full average costs more (25-50% more), or the same price carries a lower guaranteed number. Say in advance what happens if it is missed (a story, or part of the integration reposted as a short), or the brand will expect a whole new video. Agree the counting window and any cap in writing. On CPM deals a view cap sits above the creator's average, never below. Every guarantee is agreed with the creator first (BRD-OFFER-14).
Source: coaching call "17/1 - RME (Brands) w/Ammar" (2026-01-17) — "You can say, okay, 3K, but 100K view guarantee."
Source: coaching call "December 3rd 2025: Deal Breakdown & Strategy" (2025-12-03) — "we're open to performance-based deals. However, CPM deals, have production, because production costs is this. All this type of stuff. How about we do a view guarantee?"

### BRD-NEG-26 — Exclusivity, usage rights, whitelisting: ask the creator first, add modestly

Rule (should): A standard sponsorship lives only on the creator's channel; using the video in the brand's own ads (usage rights, whitelisting) and blocking competitors (exclusivity) are extras. Before answering the brand, ask the creator whether they accept and at what price, and ask the brand for how long. Charge the brand what the creator adds, plus a modest margin (a few percent a month for whitelisting); on a deal already well priced, offering a reasonable term at no extra cost can be the better move. If the creator refuses the term at that rate, tell the brand plainly: the term costs more, or it comes out. Asking the creator first is the default of house choice HC-30; the alternative is INF-CLOSE-07.
Source: coaching call "September 2: Q&A (Advanced Brands) -Osama" (2025-09-02) — "Yeah, it's just on the influencer's channel, unless obviously there's a thing called usage rights, where the brand would basically buy that sort of ad and use it for a certain period."

### BRD-NEG-27 — First deals: a small margin is fine, a loss or a giveaway is not

Rule (should): On the first deals with a brand, the relationship matters more than the margin: a small profit (even a few hundred dollars) is worth taking to start. Not zero, and not below the niche's CPM range: a brand never lets you climb back from a price it has had once. When the budget leaves nothing, offer a cheaper creator or a lighter deliverable, or walk away.

### BRD-NEG-28 — Brands that sponsor every niche anchor on the cheapest one

Rule (tip): Mass-market brands (VPNs, shopping extensions) can sponsor any niche, so they price every creator like a cheap entertainment channel. Their lowballs are normal and rarely move much. Show what their budget really buys (a much smaller creator) next to what the strong creator costs, keep them for unusually cheap creators, or spend the effort on brands whose buyers are this niche (BRD-NEG-21).
Source: coaching call "March 9th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2026-03-09) — "Because they can go into an entertainment niche, which is just $10 CPM and sell it. So then they're going to be like, why should I double my price? Why should I pay double to an automotive influencer?"

### BRD-NEG-29 — A creator drops out: say so plainly and offer another

Rule (must): If a creator the brand wanted can no longer do the deal, tell the brand plainly that it is not going ahead with that creator. No invented excuse (a fake scheduling clash); the creator's private reasons need not be shared. In the same email, offer a creator of similar or better value, or ask whether they want someone like them or to see alternatives. If the creator's rate has gone up, say that it has.
Source: coaching call "9/6" (2025-06-09) — "you can't lie, and you, you know, that's the main issue, you can't really lie, just tell them, listen, uh, with him, unfortunately, we can't really do it anymore"

### BRD-NEG-30 — Integrations longer than 90-120 seconds: push back

Rule (should): When a brand asks for a 2-5 minute integration, explain that an ad that long makes viewers leave, which lowers the video's reach and the brand's results, and keep it to 60-90 seconds (up to 120). For more exposure, offer an extra deliverable instead, such as a story or a short, at its own price.

### BRD-NEG-31 — Coach Osama: a product sent with a deal never reduces the fee

Rule (tip): Coach Osama never takes a product's retail price off the creator's fee: what the brand gives up is only the product's cost to make, far below its price, and the creator still has to be paid for the work. He asks the brand's flat fee first, then which product comes with it, and says plainly that the product is not a form of payment. One of the options of house choice HC-31; the default is the lesson's (BRD-REPLY-17): only a very expensive product may count toward the price.

### BRD-NEG-32 — A promised long-term partnership is no reason to discount the test

Rule (should): Nearly every brand asks for a lower rate on an 'initial test' with talk of a long-term partnership; usually it is a negotiating line, sometimes it is true. Price the test on its own merits. If they want a lower rate for volume, tie it to a committed multi-video package (BRD-AFTER-16), not to a promise.

### BRD-NEG-33 — Ask how, not if

Rule (should): When a brand hesitates, don't plead for the deal. Treat it as happening and ask what would make it work for them: the budget, the timing, the deliverable. The answer shows their cards and gives the next offer something real to fit.
