# Influencers — Pricing

### INF-PRICE-01 — Judge a flat price by its CPM

Rule (must): CPM = price ÷ average views × 1000. Compare it with the niche's typical range and adjust for engagement and audience quality (up if strong, down if weak).
Steps: Average views per video (recent, not the outliers) → Asking price ÷ average views × 1000 = CPM → Compare with the niche range below → Adjust for engagement, audience country and age
Do: YouTube niche ranges: tech & gadgets $30-50, finance $40-80 (avoid haram finance), health/fitness $20-35, entertainment $10-20, lifestyle/beauty $15-25, travel $12-20, education $25-40, food $10-20
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "A Tech influencer averages 100,000 views per video. - His asking price is $3000. - You divide The Price by The amount of views. - You Multiply it by 1000. - The CPM is $30. - The Tech niche average price is $30, so this influencer is fairly priced."
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Typical CPM Rates by Niche Here are some average CPM rates for different niches to guide your pricing: - Tech & Gadgets: $30-$50 - Finance (careful to avoid haram): $40-$80 - Health/Fitness: $20-$35 - Entertainment : $10-$20 - Lifestyle/Beauty: $15-$25 - Travel: $12-$20 - Education: $25-$40 - Food/Cooking: $10-$20"

### INF-PRICE-02 — Flat rate or CPM deal: who carries the risk

Rule (must): A flat rate puts the risk on the brand (fits trusted creators with a track record); a CPM performance deal puts it on the creator (fits new partnerships or cautious brands). CPM deals come with a view cap and a monitoring window (typically 30 days); ask for 60-90 days before the deal is secured and put it in the contract.
Do: Spell out rate, cap, monitoring window and payment timing in any CPM deal
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Flat Rate: Ideal for trusted influencers with a strong track record. Brands accept the performance risk."
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "You can request the brand to extend it to 60-90 days, but this needs to be done before securing the deal, and it must be included in the contract."

### INF-PRICE-03 — Premiums and penalties

Rule (should): Price up for authority in the niche, rare brand deals, top production and a loyal audience; price down for engagement under 2%, low-effort high-frequency posting, audiences tired of ads, and inexperienced creators.
Do: Some brands pay well above the CPM to tie their name to a creator (an ambassador) or for long category exclusivity: price association and exclusivity as extras, never as part of the base rate
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Negative Adjustment (Lower Price Must be Negotiated ) - Low Engagement: Engagement rate below 2% is a red flag. - Frequency of posting: An Influencer posts twice a day and doesn't put much effort in his content. - Oversaturated Ads: Audience fatigue from constant brand deals."
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Positive Adjustment (Higher Price Justified) - High Authority: They're a leader in their niche. - Exclusive Content: Rarely work with brands, increasing their scarcity value. - Top-Quality Production: Professional visuals and storytelling."

### INF-PRICE-04 — Other platforms are priced off YouTube

Rule (should): Anchor on YouTube CPM, then go down: Instagram about half (typically $5-15), TikTok about a third ($5-10), Facebook $5-8, X $1-3.
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Instagram - Overall avg CPM: $5-$15 (rarely more) - Shorter content and lower retention rates than YouTube. - Niche matters, but less than Youtube. - If you want a simple rule, follow the same Logic as youtube, but divide the avg CPM prices by 2."
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "Always anchor your pricing with YouTube CPM as the standard, then adjust downward for platforms with less retention and engagement."

### INF-PRICE-05 — CPM performance deals: the usual terms

Rule (should): In a per-view deal the rate itself is lower than valuation CPMs (often $5-15 per thousand actual views, higher for mature first-world audiences), with a cap, a monitoring window and payment some weeks after the video goes live; every term is negotiable with the brand.
Source: 2.3. How do influencers get paid CPM vs Straight — "The cost per thousand views usually goes from 5 - 15 dollars and it depends on multiple factors, being the most influential ones: the country, niche and estimated age of the audience."
Source: 2.3. How do influencers get paid CPM vs Straight — "A CPM deal can look like this : - 10$ cpm - 30 days monitoring period (views after 30 days don't count) - Cap at 1m views (so you max get paid $10k, even if you make 5 million views) - Paid 45 days after the video goes live This is just an example, each variable can change and be negotiated with the brand."

### INF-PRICE-06 — Pricing is a judgment call — a person confirms it

Rule (must): The course is explicit that pricing has no formula that settles it; the workflow computes the CPM view and a suggested range, and a person confirms every number before it is sent.
Source: 2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing — "To keep it real, there is no clear cut rule to pricing influencers."

### INF-PRICE-07 — Average views: last 12, drop the extremes

Rule (must): Take the last 12 pieces of content on the platform being priced, drop the highest and the lowest, and average the other 10. Leave out uploads from the last week or so, which haven't had time to reach their usual views, and use recent videos rather than the archive: brands price on what the creator gets now. When views are steady an eyeball estimate is fine; when they swing wildly, always calculate.
Do: For a creator who uploads rarely, average the last three months rather than the last 12 videos
Source: 2.5. How to Price Influencers Step-by-Step Tutorial — "Gather the last 12 pieces of content (videos, posts, or stories depending on the platform). 2. Remove the outliers: Drop the video with the highest views and the one with the lowest views. These extremes can skew the average. 3. Calculate the average of the remaining 10 posts to get a realistic view count."
Source: lesson video "2.5. How to Price Influencers Step-by-Step Tutorial" @0:37 — "You gather the last 12 pieces of content. Alright, I have the last 12 pieces of content in front of me. You remove the top performer and the worst performer."

### INF-PRICE-08 — Ask their price first; estimate it when they will not say

Rule (must): Ask the creator what they charge for the specific deliverable, usually the 60-90 second integration. If they answer that it depends on the brand, say why a ballpark matters: without it we can't tell which brands fit them, so we won't pitch them anywhere. If they still hold back, estimate it: average views ÷ 1000 × the niche CPM.
Do: Step-by-step lesson benchmarks: tech $20-40, health/fitness $15-30, beauty/lifestyle $10-25, gaming $10-20, general entertainment $5-15, luxury goods $30-50 (the pricing masterclass gives higher tech/fitness ranges — see house choices)
Source: 2.5. How to Price Influencers Step-by-Step Tutorial — "Ask the influencer what they charge for the deliverable (e.g., a YouTube video, an Instagram post, etc.)."
Source: 2.5. How to Price Influencers Step-by-Step Tutorial — "(Average Views / 1,000) x Niche CPM = Estimated Price"

### INF-PRICE-09 — 'What would you pay me?' — don't name the first number

Rule (must): Ask for their ballpark or usual rates first. If they insist, give a realistic placeholder range with an honest exit (the brand has not been pitched yet), and read their reaction. Never an insultingly low number for a creator whose value you know.
Source: 5.Influencer is Asking me To Price Him — "When influencers ask for a price, avoid being the first to commit."
Source: 5.Influencer is Asking me To Price Him — "If you know the influencer gets a million views and usually charges $10K, don’t insult them with a $500 offer."

### INF-PRICE-10 — Swinging views: CPM only, or a lower flat fee

Rule (should): When a creator's recent views jump around (10k, 200k, 20k...) the average lies: offer pay-per-view only, or a flat fee well under the average-based price, and do the same toward the brand. A single low video of a different kind (a Q&A among unboxings) is not a pattern; repeated swings are.
Source: lesson video "2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing" @16:22 — "So you're gonna see 52,000 views, 57,000 views, 1.3k views. Imagine if the brand paid for this video. They would get smoked on the bill, right? Crazy, crazy amount of money lost, really, really bad. And it doesn't just happen once, right? Be careful because sometimes if it just happens once, it's a special video."
Source: lesson video "2.4. Understanding Influencer Pricing" @19:08 — "you want to highlight the inconsistency of the views, for example, or the engagement rate that's pretty low, and either offer a CPM deal structure only, so no flat fee, because it's too inconsistent. We can only pay you on a CPM basis, and you're going to do the same thing for brands, or You can talk about the engagement and get just a cheaper price."

### INF-PRICE-11 — Short-form: cross-posting comes free, and the CPM is lower

Rule (should): Reposting the same short video on another platform (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) is usually thrown in during negotiation, not paid twice. If a manager sells a short-form bundle, ask once for the single-platform price; if they won't split it, negotiate the bundle down and move on. Short-form and Instagram CPMs run at roughly half of YouTube long-form.
Source: coaching call "September 29: Brand Response Rescue - Michael" (2025-09-29) — "But yeah, like cross-posing is typically something you wouldn't charge double for."
Source: coaching call "October 27th: Influencer Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-27) — "How much would it be independently, you know, for just one platform? Just TikTok, for example, or YouTube shorts, or whatever. And just see what it says. If it says, no, I don't split it up, it's a bundle deal, whatever, then stick with it, negotiate it down, and move on."

### INF-PRICE-12 — AI and coding creators: higher CPMs that swing widely

Rule (should): AI is the niche where CPMs vary most: coaches have seen everything from tech-like rates to $100 and beyond, because AI startups spend fast to get ahead of their competitors and very specialised creators lead small audiences. Coding and programming creators price like tech ($30-50), not like general education, since software brands pay well for those viewers. Check what similar AI creators actually charge and what AI brands actually pay before trusting any single figure; creators with an agent quote higher, as the agent adds its cut. Every number goes to a person for approval (INF-PRICE-06).

### INF-PRICE-13 — Dedicated videos: two to three times the integration; packages cost less per video

Rule (should): A dedicated video costs about two to three times the creator's integration rate, not more. Several videos for one brand earn a per-video discount from the creator; about a third off (three for the price of two) is a common target, depending on how much demand the creator has.
Source: coaching call "September 18: Q&A - Younes" (2025-09-18) — "Yeah, and, by the way, Dedicated is 2 to 3 times the integration, just to clarify that. So, not just 2 times, he's up to 3 times as well."
Source: coaching call "October 2nd: Brand Detective - Michael" (2025-10-02) — "So if his integration price is 5K for a dedicated video, he would be somewhere around 10 to 15K."

### INF-PRICE-14 — Pay a good creator fairly

Rule (should): Squeezing a strong creator to the lowest possible rate backfires: other agencies will offer more, and the creator starts turning down our deals. With a creator worth keeping, agree a fair rate and take a reasonable margin on it, building the relationship rather than doubling the price once.
Do: When a creator new to sponsorships asks what to charge, explain the usual CPM range for their niche honestly and propose the lower end for a first deal, with the true reason: brands pay less on a first deal, and a good first deal leads to more work
Source: coaching call "March 25th: Student Success Breakdown" (2026-03-25) — "other guys are gonna come, they're offer him more money, because they're not as good, like, negotiating as you, right, he's gonna give them an offer, and they're gonna give an offer, yeah, and they're gonna get them deals"
Source: coaching call "13/6" (2025-06-13) — "You take your profit, maybe 30%, 20%, 30% on top of that, and you start to build that relationship, right? Instead of like, you know, just a one-off and then make like 100% profit"
