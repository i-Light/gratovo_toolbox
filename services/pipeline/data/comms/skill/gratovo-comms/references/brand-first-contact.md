# Brands — First contact

### BRD-FIRST-01 — Pitch structure: creator, gap, idea, call to action

Rule (must): Open by introducing the creator through the strongest facts from the creator audit; show you researched the brand by naming what it does now and where there is an opening (an audience or a format it has not used yet), framed as an opportunity, never as a gap in its marketing (BRD-OFFER-17); propose a campaign idea that connects the creator's audience to the brand's goal; close with a clear call to action. Adapt the framework to each brand — never one pitch for all.
Source: 3.4. How to Build a Strong Pitch - Coach Abu Numaan — "Introduce the creator using the strongest insights from your Influencer Audit and avoid copying templates verbatim."
Source: 3.4. How to Build a Strong Pitch - Coach Abu Numaan — "Show you've done your research by pointing out what the brand is currently doing and where there's room to improve."

### BRD-FIRST-02 — Wrong person reached: write fresh, ask to be redirected

Rule (must): If the right contact cannot be found, write to others from the top of the hierarchy down; with someone outside marketing, say plainly you could not find or reach the right person and ask them to point you there. Never paste the same message to several employees, and do not name the colleagues you have also written to (BRD-FIRST-13).
Source: 3.1. Where to find high paying brands — "Do not copy and paste messages to different employees, for each time, let them know you're reaching out because you didn't know who was the right person/you couldn't get in touch with them and kindly ask them to guide you to the right person."
Source: 3.1. Where to find high paying brands — "If you're going very deep with employees unrelated to this field of work, ask them to redirect you to the right people."

### BRD-FIRST-03 — Write as the expert, pre-handle objections

Rule (must): Never 'just reaching out': write with the quiet confidence of someone who represents strong creators and knows the brand's niche, and frame the offer so the usual objections are answered before they come up — without sounding over the top.
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "just reaching out." You're a professional representing exceptional influencers who deliver real results. You're here because you know what you're doing and because working with you will benefit them."
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "The goal is to avoid objections before they even arise—this is called pre-objection handling."

### BRD-FIRST-04 — The brand email formula

Rule (should): Personal opening about the brand's recent campaigns or goals; the creator and the one reason they fit (BRD-OFFER-18); a confident close toward next steps. The lesson's formula also puts the full audience overview, deliverables and pricing in this email; coaches now keep those for the brand's reply (FND-MIND-22, HC-22).
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "Your Go-To Formula - Personalized Opening: Compliment their recent campaigns or align your pitch with their goals. - Influencer Overview: Include platforms, audience size, demographics, and success stories. - Deliverables & Pricing: Be specific about what's included. - Close Confidently: Encourage the brand to discuss next steps."

### BRD-FIRST-05 — Be the problem solver

Rule (should): Show how the creator helps hit the brand's KPIs (audience aligned with its objective, engagement, trust) with a specific campaign idea tied to their goal and timing.
Source: 3.3. Brands and their objectives — "Highlight how you help brands hit their KPIs with precision."
Source: 3.3. Brands and their objectives — "Mention specific campaign ideas and how they align with their goals."

### BRD-FIRST-06 — Present as a boutique agency that hand-picks

Rule (should): Brands are pitched all day by big agencies that send lists. Position the agency as a small team (or an agent) that only brings the creator it genuinely believes fits their goals — quality over quantity. This positioning works even for a new agency because it is about method, not size.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Position Yourself as a Small Personalized Agency (or even an Agent) Brands get pitched all day by massive agencies who don't actually care about fit. You need to make them feel like you're different from the start."
Source: lesson video "1.5. Marketing crash course" @17:26 — "You want to be the best agency for them in that specific niche, for that specific influencer, for that specific brand"

### BRD-FIRST-07 — Short brand campaign email

Rule (should): For a brand campaign email: a personalized opener (a recent influencer collaboration of theirs is ideal), a one-line value proposition, a short proof point (only real brands or creators), and a soft yes/no question such as whether they are open to working with creators this quarter.
Source: 3.Bonus - SmartLead Campaigns - Coach Osama — "Structure: personalised opener → brief value prop → social proof (brands/creators you've worked with) → soft CTA ("Are you open to working with creators this quarter?")."
Source: 3.Bonus - SmartLead Campaigns - Coach Osama — "Smartlead's AI can pull in a brand's recent influencer collaborations and reference them in your opening line."

### BRD-FIRST-08 — Text-style brand note: matching creator, ask to send the stats

Rule (tip): A short brand first touch can name something specific they do, say you have a creator who matches their style, and ask whether you can send the stats.
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "Hey {name}, Love what you guys are doing with UGC. Got a creator that matches your style can I send over the stats?"

### BRD-FIRST-09 — Never present the creator as eager

Rule (must): Do not tell a brand the creator is 'super eager', 'excited' or 'happy to do anything'. It makes the creator and the agency look needy and sets the price expectation low: it may bring a few more replies, but they come with lowball offers. Present the creator as a strong fit, calmly.
Source: lesson video "8.Abu Lahya Reviewing Emails" @16:36 — "Also, super eager to share, like your influencer is super eager to share. What that sounds to my ears is, you can throw any offer, I'm gonna accept any price. He's working for anything, no matter what. Super eager neediness just smells needy."
Source: lesson video "8.Abu Lahya Reviewing Emails" @17:27 — "Maybe you get a little bit more responses, but then you're going to get trash offers."

### BRD-FIRST-10 — A student's experience: even a support address can redirect you

Rule (tip): When no decision maker can be found, a support or general address can still pass the message to the right person — ask them to.
Source: lesson video "6.How Abdelmenan Closed 4,500 on his first deal" @44:20 — "I've gotten replies from support, they redirected me to the right person"

### BRD-FIRST-11 — Ask whether they are open to partnerships before asking them to work with our creators

Rule (should): Asking a brand in the first email whether it wants to work with 'our creators' is a yes-or-no on a stranger's offer and kills many leads. One course approach opens with whether the brand is open to partnerships and, once it says yes, brings the creators with concrete numbers (platform, size, fit). Other coaches put the creator and a key fact in the first email: which applies is house choice HC-14.
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @26:41 — "would you be open to partnerships without even mentioning our guys? And then, if yes, boom. What do you have in mind?"
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @27:01 — "the first email has a clear yes or no, and you have fifty percent chance of killing the lead. Whereas, if you're digging deeper and you go, first email, are you open to partnerships? There's a high chance they are."

### BRD-FIRST-12 — With the creator's OK, say they want to work with this brand

Rule (should): Before pitching a brand, ask the creator whether they are interested in that brand (or its category) and whether you may say so. A brand that hears the creator asked to be put forward replies more often and is less tempted to go around the agency. Only say it once the creator has agreed.
Source: lesson video "3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do" @13:55 — "most of the time, you can actually ask the influencers to help you close deals because they actually want to close deals. So, you can say, Hey, do you mind if I mention that you asked me to reach out to this brand so that it's easier to broker the deal?"

### BRD-FIRST-13 — Several people at one brand: two at a time, never naming each other

Rule (must): Write to two people at a brand at once (FND-DLV-23), each message worded for its reader, and never tell one that you also wrote to the other: the reader then leaves it to the colleague, and the line shows in the inbox preview. Follow each up (about three follow-ups); if neither answers, move on to the next two. If both reply, tell one of them politely that you are continuing with their colleague, or ask which of them decides. If the first three or four people in marketing ignore you, the brand is probably not interested: spend the effort on more brands rather than more employees. (The lesson's line naming a colleague is out of date, FND-MIND-22.) Writing to two at once is the default of house choice HC-25; the alternative is BRD-FU-16.
Don't: Write to a colleague straight after one employee has clearly said no
Source: coaching call "March 28th: Roast my email (Brands)- Ammar" (2026-03-28) — "I've reached out to two people at the same time from the company, you know, reach out, follow up, follow up, follow up. If they don't answer, I move to another two people"
Source: coaching call "March 28th: Roast my email (Brands)- Ammar" (2026-03-28) — "If both of them answer, you can ask, like, which of you, because you have, like, replies from both those. Also, I'm in contact with Jeff, which one of you is the decision maker?"

### BRD-FIRST-14 — Personalize a brand email on their marketing, not their product

Rule (must): We write as the owner of a marketing agency, not as a satisfied customer: the personal line comments on how a recent creator integration of theirs was done (what the creator said about the product, how smoothly it was placed), never on how good the product is. Watch the integration itself, take one concrete detail, and say what caught your eye in a few plain words; never claim it drove sales. Never compliment an integration that flopped. A line that would still work after swapping the brand and creator names is not personal. If the brand has never used creators, use something about the person you are writing to (a new role, from LinkedIn); congratulate on an award only when the person won it themselves. Finding it should take a minute or two (search YouTube for 'sponsored by' the brand, recent uploads). Don't open with 'I saw' or 'I noticed'. (Coach Osama would also test product compliments; this is the majority view.)
Source: coaching call "10/1 - RME (Brands) w/Ammar" (2026-01-10) — "Basically, you talk about marketing. This is all you talk about, okay? You don't talk about how soft the mattress is, how good those earphones are."
Source: coaching call "3/1 - Roast my emails (Brands) w/Ammar" (2026-01-03) — "You are not satisfied customer. You are marketing something, something, okay? Executive, owner of a marketing agency."

### BRD-FIRST-15 — End a brand email with one easy, confident instruction

Rule (should): The first email's only job is a reply, so it ends with one clear, easy step: 'Should I send the details?' Never a hesitant permission question ('Would you like...?', 'Are you open to something new?'), which invites a no. Proposing a quick call also works: a busy reader who will not take a call often replies with a lighter yes instead.
Source: coaching call "10/1 - RME (Brands) w/Ammar" (2026-01-10) — "Would you like to? No, I would not like because I'm not interested, right? But if you say, should I send?"
Source: coaching call "3/1 - Roast my emails (Brands) w/Ammar" (2026-01-03) — "And just in CDA, clear instructions. Just do it, you know. Should I send some details, quick overview, this and that, blah, blah, blah."

### BRD-FIRST-16 — Describe our tie to a creator accurately: 'we work with'

Rule (must): To a brand, say we work with the creator. Say we represent them, or write on their behalf, only when the creator has explicitly agreed to our outreach for them; say we manage them only with a management agreement; never call ourselves official partners. Words that suggest exclusive management we don't have raise questions we can't answer truthfully (BRD-REPLY-04).
Source: coaching call "November 5th 2025: Deal Breakdown & Strategy" (2025-11-05) — "if you've told a guy, look, I'm going to be doing outreach for you, right, if you made a clear term, then use it, if you represent exclusively, of course you can use it, but if that's not the case, then I wouldn't"
Source: coaching call "November 22th: Review/ Roast my email- Ammar" (2025-11-22) — "Because you're not working with him on exclusivity, right? You're not exclusively managing him. So I'm representing. It's a word we should not use because they will ask you about it."
