# Foundations (always apply)

## Mindset and power dynamics

### FND-MIND-01 — A professional bringing money, never a fan

Rule (must): Write as a business owner bringing the reader money. No needy tone, no over-complimenting, no putting the creator on a pedestal.
Don't: Gush about their content; Sound grateful for their attention
Source: 2.How to Message Influencers - Coach Ammar @0:00 — "you are not a fanboy. You're a professional bringing value to the influencer. They need you as much as you need them."

### FND-MIND-02 — Compliment the work, not the person

Rule (must): Praise, if any, is about a specific piece of work ('that segment was seamless'), never about the person ('you're amazing'). Praise hands over power and kills negotiation later.
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "Compliment the work instead of the person."
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "Fanboy talk hands them all the power. After three paragraphs of praise you can't negotiate rates or ask for changes."

### FND-MIND-03 — Nice, short, never needy, never the first offer

Rule (must): Always be nice; never sound needy; never send bulky messages; never accept the first offer from either side — moving the number shows you are in control.
Source: 2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers — "Don’t send bulky messages: It’s confusing and instantly breaks the interest of the influencer."
Source: 2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers — "Don’t accept the first offer: Try to play a bit to reduce or increase the offer whenever talking with the influencer or the brand."

### FND-MIND-04 — Facts over feelings, empathy for their reality

Rule (must): Keep messages factual and calm whatever the other side's tone; creators work to feed families and carry pressures, so acknowledge their situation while staying on the numbers.
Source: 2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama — "Stick to facts, data, and metrics. Influencers can feel when you're emotional."
Source: 2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama — "Most influencers do this work to feed their family."

### FND-MIND-05 — Brands are relationships, written with precision

Rule (must): Approach brands to build relationships, not to milk them; brands are run by people who buy when they feel understood, so be specific and show you understand their situation.
Source: 3.1. Where to find high paying brands — "Approach brand outreach with a relationship-building mindset."
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "Brands are run by people. People buy when they feel understood."

### FND-MIND-06 — Light follow-ups by default, heavy only when it matters

Rule (must): Most follow-ups are light and friendly (a bump, a touch of humor). When a real deal is stalling, a heavier one states the true consequence respectfully (e.g. the brand will move on) — never confrontational, never invented.
Source: 5.When should I use a light or heavy follow-up — "Use light follow-ups most of the time-things like: "Hey, just bumping this to the top of your inbox in case it got buried. Looking forward to your thoughts!"
Source: 5.When should I use a light or heavy follow-up — "But, when things are getting serious and you’re not getting the response you need, it’s time for a heavier follow-up."

### FND-MIND-07 — Frame every offer by what they gain

Rule (must): Present proposals from the other side's benefit (a CPM deal framed as paying only for real results), never only from what the agency gets.
Source: 5.Focusing on your Prospects Benefits — "When making offers with brands or influencers, always focus on their benefits, not just your own."

### FND-MIND-08 — Cold outreach: the only goal is a reply

Rule (must): Cold means no relationship yet; the first message only has to earn a reply — closing comes later. Every message moves the person from cold to warm, so a no today can turn into a yes months later.
Source: 1.1. Introduction to Cold Email - Coach Ammar — "Your only objective in a cold email is to get a reply."
Source: 1.1. Introduction to Cold Email - Coach Ammar — "A no today can become a yes months later, because now they know who you are."

### FND-MIND-09 — Power dynamics and thinking a few moves ahead

Rule (must): Be ready to walk away while staying respectful and pleasant; plan the conversation a few steps ahead — short messages first to get a reply, then create the need before presenting the offer. Before sending, read it as the recipient: short, clear, confident, nice, not needy?
Steps: Name the end goal with this person (a commission, a sale, a lower rate) → Plan the two to four messages that lead there; the first one only has to earn a reply → Before the offer, bring out a true need (e.g. no sponsors on recent videos) so the offer answers it → Before sending, reread it as the recipient: how does this make us look?
Source: 1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course) — "Maintain a balance of power by being ready to walk away from a deal while still being respectful and nice to deal with."
Source: 1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course) — "Create a pain point or a need before presenting your offer to make it more relevant and appealing."

### FND-MIND-10 — People buy to end a pain or reach an outcome

Rule (must): Find the specific pain or desired outcome (usually by homework, before asking), show you understand it, position the offer around it, build trust by being honest, and give a real reason to start now without pressure. B2B buyers also need logical, numeric justification they can show internally.
Source: 1.4. Sales crash course — "Brands or influencers buy because of one of two reasons: 1. They have a pain they need solved. 2. They have a desired outcome they want to achieve."
Source: 1.4. Sales crash course — "Pro tip, you can figure this one out on your own, without asking any questions if you do your homework correctly"

### FND-MIND-11 — Make them feel understood

Rule (must): People buy when they feel understood, not merely when you understand them: do the homework before writing (who they are, what they want, what is not working), write it down, and let the message show it.
Source: 1.5. Marketing crash course — "Most Important Takeaway: People buy when they feel understood, not just when you understand them."
Source: 1.5. Marketing crash course — "Before reaching out to anyone—brands or influencers—do your homework."

### FND-MIND-12 — Three business models, in order

Rule (should): Start as a broker (buy a creator's video below market, sell it to a brand); later act as an exclusive agent (15-40% on inbound and outbound deals, around 25% when starting); only an established agency manages brands' budgets.
Source: 1.6. The 3 Ways to Make Money With an IMA — "This is the best way to start making $5,000 to $20,000 a month in profit."
Source: 1.6. The 3 Ways to Make Money With an IMA — "You'll be charging 15% to 40%, depending on the workload. Anything around 25% is good when you're starting."

### FND-MIND-13 — Volume with a brain, and realistic reply rates

Rule (must): About fifty good, individually different emails a day beat five perfect ones or a hundred AI-generated ones. Normal: ~3% replies overall, 3-5% from creators (up to 10% with follow-ups), lower from brands. Three or four creator replies per hundred means it works; five replies in five hundred means something is broken — check spam placement first. Track daily sending targets, not revenue.
Source: 1.3. Mindset & Misconceptions - Coach Ammar — "Fifty good emails a day, written by you and each one different, beats both extremes every time."
Source: 1.3. Mindset & Misconceptions - Coach Ammar — "If five hundred emails only bring five replies, something is actually broken, so check your spam placement first."

### FND-MIND-14 — Write like the agency that does not need this deal

Rule (must): Every message reads as from an agency that wants this client but does not need it: short, unhurried, ready to walk away below its floor, and still pleasant to deal with. Neediness shows in a first message that stacks everything, follow-ups bunched together, big discounts handed out to keep them, and instant replies; once it shows, the deal and the price both get worse.
Do: Say the agency wants new clients and good partners, never that it needs them; Leave normal working time before replying and between follow-ups; Know the floor (price, margin, fit) before the conversation and hold it
Don't: Beg, pile follow-ups on top of each other, or rush a discount to keep them; Swing into arrogance: confident and easy to deal with, never a pain
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @17:11 — "How would the best agency in the world act? Do you really think they would send like long emails, five follow-ups within 30 minutes to just try to get the deal? Would they give the huge discounts? Would they reply within one minute to make sure the guy doesn't leave?"
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @18:03 — "We want new clients. We don't need new clients."

### FND-MIND-15 — Business readers buy on logic: no emotional power lines

Rule (must): Brands and creators are businesses, so persuade with facts and plain logic. Lines that show indifference ('this deal won't change our life') belong to emotional consumer selling; in a business message they only make the agency look difficult.
Don't: Tell a client the deal does not matter to us; Play hard to get with attitude instead of facts
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @20:07 — "And all like caring, changing your life is like emotional. Whereas with business, it is the opposite. It is completely logical. Like straight A one plus one equals two. Like it's very simple. And here, those kind of sentences, like I said, like, you know, I just give you the example with it won't change my life, but just saying here, don't use this with your clients because this doesn't work in B2B."

### FND-MIND-16 — Two things every buyer needs: trust and a sure payback

Rule (must): Whoever buys from the agency (a brand buying a creator, a creator giving us a share) needs two things: to trust and respect us — which comes from how we come across: legit, calm, competent — and to see the money coming back — which comes from an offer that makes sense: the right creator for the product, results before, a clear fit. Every pitch serves both.
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @12:03 — "When you're selling something to someone, they need two things. If you go close to join my program, you have those two things. The first one is that you trust me, the second one is that you know you will make your investments back."
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @13:04 — "And the second one. Is they know they can make the investment back, which is more like applicable to the guy itself and to the offer itself. They know they should know the offer is good, it works, the influencer had results before, the offer makes sense."

### FND-MIND-17 — A deal went wrong? Check how we came across first

Rule (should): When a deal dies, a reply never comes or something goes wrong, review our own messages before anything else: were we pleasant, did we sound needy or overexcited, did a line make the agency look small? Fix that in the next message.
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @43:57 — "Whenever you don't close a deal, whenever something messes up, or the guy doesn't respond, or whatever happens. Don't send me a message. Go back to this video and trust me, you'll find your solution here. Like, genuinely, if you go through the checklist, you would see: was I nice? Was I too, you know, needy? Did I say something that made me appear in that way?"

### FND-MIND-18 — Listen first — in email, homework replaces the questionnaire

Rule (must): Before pitching, find their pain or goal. On a call that means questions; in email it means research (their recent videos and sponsors, a brand's past campaigns) so the message can name it instead of sending a list of questions. Where a question is needed, the best one is 'why': why the last video had no sponsor, why a brand does or does not use creators.
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @7:35 — "But what that means in listening is basically what comes from listening, is getting the information. So doing your homework on your own can. Replace that if you're not on sales call and if you don't want to send them 50 questions and look weird"
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @8:18 — "If you could only ask one question, that would be why."

### FND-MIND-19 — Objections are normal: agree, then offer another view

Rule (must): Nearly every deal meets an objection; it is not a no. Agree and validate first, ask whether you may offer another way to see it, then answer the real concern with facts. Never contradict head-on. The only one to leave alone is a clear 'stop contacting us' — then stop.
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @20:22 — "99.9% of the time, you'll have objections, which means you'll have to do objection handling."
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @22:54 — "And be careful here, you never want to be frontal and brutal. You don't want to be like, hey, Why would you say that? That doesn't make sense. You're never going to be like that. You want to always agree, validate them. Completely normal. I totally agree."

### FND-MIND-20 — Present the outcome, never the effort

Rule (must): The agency is paid for the problem it solves, not for the emails it sends. Describe the work by its result for them (deals they would not get alone, better-fitting brands, time back), never by the hours or tasks behind it. The agency's share is what the deal is worth minus the creator's minimum acceptable fee — but leave the creator a fee they can respect.
Source: lesson video "1.3. Understanding business" @1:35 — "what we're doing is we're tying our work to the problem that we're solving."
Source: lesson video "1.3. Understanding business" @1:52 — "So, my thing is valued as whatever upside you have minus the minimum acceptable fee that he needs to take, right? But he cannot pay you$9,999 because then he only makes a dollar and it's not respectful."

### FND-MIND-21 — Sell like a marketer, not a broker

Rule (must): Brands work through an agency, instead of going to the creator directly, when the agency brings expertise: why this creator's audience matches their buyers, which video the ad belongs in, how the creator should present the product, what happens if it underperforms, and the return on their spend. Pitches and replies show that thinking. An agency that only passes a creator along is a commodity, and brands go around it.
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 1" @13:02 — "They're getting a deep understanding of marketing rather than just focusing on influencers and brokering deals. They're actually understanding: okay, how does marketing work? Like, I'm advertising this company with this influencer. Why does it actually work? Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 1" @14:17 — "And the main thing I speak about is how can I get your return on your investment?"

Rule (must): The recorded lessons are older than the live calls. Where coaches disagree with each other, the owner decides (house choices).

### FND-MIND-23 — Answer promptly; take time only over a tricky reply

Rule (should): Answering quickly is not needy; neediness is in the tone (FND-MIND-03). Reply while they are still in their inbox and interested. The exception is a tricky message, such as a pushback on price or a delicate objection: think it through, even for a day or two, rather than answering on the spot.

## Writing

### FND-WRITE-01 — Never read like an AI template

Rule (must): Identical AI emails are spotted instantly. Every message must be specific to the person and differ in wording, structure and flow from every other message the agency sends.
Source: 2.6. Common Mistakes When Messaging Influencers — "AI gives everyone the same email. Creators receive 50 identical ones a day and spot them instantly."
Source: lesson video "1.5. Marketing crash course" @12:03 — "Some other guys use AI softwares. It's like a whole minigun firing emails left, right, and center that doesn't hit anything. We're like a sniper."

### FND-WRITE-02 — Plain words, straight to the point

Rule (must): No fancy vocabulary: professional, direct sentences, and complete openness about what is being offered.
Source: 3.5. The foot in the door method — "Don't use any fancy words, be professional but straight to the point."
Source: 3.5. The foot in the door method — "Don't be snaky about the offer, show them you can get them results, and scale up the money once they trust you."

### FND-WRITE-03 — Vary whole sentences between sends

Rule (must): Repeated phrases across sends hurt deliverability; every email varies its sentences — and a varied line is varied as a whole sentence, never a mix of halves.
Source: 3.Bonus - SmartLead Campaigns - Coach Osama — "Spintax lets you write multiple variations of a line and Smartlead randomly picks one for each send."
Source: 3.Bonus - SmartLead Campaigns - Coach Osama — "If you spintax part of a sentence, you must spintax the full sentence — don't mix it halfway."

### FND-WRITE-04 — AI drafts need context and a human edit

Rule (must): AI output is only as good as the context it is given (role, situation, goal, format, what to avoid), and copying it without refining makes outreach robotic. The workflow feeds full case context and a person reviews every draft before it goes out.
Source: 3.5. How to Use AI Like a Pro — "The key is to give it full context and structure your prompts so the output is exactly what you need."
Source: 3.5. How to Use AI Like a Pro — "If you copy-paste everything AI generates without refining it, you’ll never improve—and your outreach will sound robotic and ineffective."

### FND-WRITE-05 — Mid-conversation follow-ups need a real working day in between

Rule (must): Follow up only on working days and only after the person had a real working window (a Friday 4 pm message waits until Tuesday afternoon; holidays add time). Keep following up until they answer, spacing each nudge further apart.
Source: 5.How can I follow up mid-conversation — "If there hasn’t been a solid workday window between your last message and the follow-up, wait it out."
Source: 5.How can I follow up mid-conversation — "you should keep following up until they respond, while delaying each follow up more and more to avoid looking spammy and robotic."

### FND-WRITE-06 — Replies: warm, contextual, one ask, easy to answer

Rule (must): Concise is not curt: thank them when they first reply, give a line of context, and make one request; once the conversation is running, skip the thanks and go straight to the point. Avoid stacking several asks, and offer simple options they can answer in a word ('brand awareness or direct ROI?').
Source: 5.How to Reply to Emails Professionally and Clearly — "Example: "Hey, please send your media kit." This comes across as cold and like you don’t care."
Source: 5.How to Reply to Emails Professionally and Clearly — "Instead of asking broad questions, give them simple options so they can reply quickly."

### FND-WRITE-07 — Precision: name the brands, describe the audience

Rule (must): Never 'a few brands in mind' or 'our audience aligns perfectly': name the brands and describe the audience concretely (who, where, why they need the product). Vagueness is the usual reason replies do not come.
Source: 5.Be Clear and Personal in Outreach — "Instead of saying you have "a few brands in mind," you should name the actual brands you're thinking about."
Source: 5.Be Clear and Personal in Outreach — "If you’re not getting high response rates or closing deals after 2-3 emails, it’s because your outreach is too vague."

### FND-WRITE-08 — One idea, connected sentences, an obvious next step

Rule (must): Each email carries one idea; every sentence leads into the next (observation → reason for writing → request), and the call to action is unmistakable. Readers skim the subject, the first lines and the ending.
Source: 5.How to Write Good Emails — "One Idea Per Email: Focus on a single subject throughout the email."
Source: 5.How to Write Good Emails — "Imagine the lowest attention span reader trying to understand."

### FND-WRITE-09 — The two-recipients test

Rule (must): If the same email could go unchanged to two completely different people, it has no personalization. Using the person's name is the minimum.
Source: 1.2. How to Write a Cold Email - Coach Ammar — "if you could send the exact same email to both of them, it has no personalization."
Source: 1.2. How to Write a Cold Email - Coach Ammar — "Even using someone's name, something most emails skip, creates a connection a copy-paste template never will."

### FND-WRITE-10 — About them, not about us

Rule (must): Flip the ratio: one line specific to the recipient beats five about the agency. Openers like 'I hope this email finds you well, my name is… and I run an agency' center the sender and could go to anyone.
Don't: 'I hope this email finds you well'; Opening with your name and your agency; Time-of-day greetings ('Morning', 'Good evening'): an email is read whenever they open it
Source: 1.2. How to Write a Cold Email - Coach Ammar — "Watch your ratio: three sentences about yourself equals one sentence about them. Flip it."
Source: 1.2. How to Write a Cold Email - Coach Ammar — ""I hope this email finds you well, my name is Ammar and I run an influencer marketing agency.""

### FND-WRITE-11 — Write at a 4th-grade reading level

Rule (must): Simple words and short sentences for a busy reader with no reason to care yet; no LinkedIn vocabulary or corporate phrasing.
Source: 1.2. How to Write a Cold Email - Coach Ammar — "Write at a 4th grade reading level. Skip the LinkedIn vocabulary and corporate phrasing."
Source: lesson video "1.2. How to Write a Cold Email - Coach Ammar" @4:05 — "But hey, write plainly. No one likes LinkedIn vocabulary. I'm thrilled to announce that I will do this. No, trust me. Fourth grade reading level. That's all you need."

### FND-WRITE-12 — Spam-trigger and hype words

Rule (must): Never use the course's spam magnets — 'offer', 'guarantee', 'opportunity', 'revenue', 'grow your brand', 'we help influencers…' — or hype language (scale, guaranteed, grow fast). Say it plainly instead ('quick idea', 'confident in the results', 'better results').
Don't: offer; guarantee; opportunity; revenue; grow your brand; we help influencers; scale; guaranteed; grow fast; Money words in a cold email: 'money', 'free', dollar signs or price figures
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "Using hype language (revenue, scale, guaranteed, grow fast, etc.)"
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "Words like: - "Offer" - "Guarantee" - "Opportunity" - "Revenue" - "Grow your brand""

### FND-WRITE-13 — Write it like a text message

Rule (must): Outreach reads like one person texting another: no paragraphs, no corporate or agency language, no pitch. Roughly 40% personalization (a post, a collab, a number), 40% one-line reason for writing, 20% a relaxed call to action ('worth showing you?', 'open to it?').
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "Rule 3 - Your email must read like a text message Not corporate. Not "agency language." Not salesy."
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "Write like a friend texting another friend."

### FND-WRITE-14 — Near-identical emails get the domain blocked

Rule (must): Anything that reads templated, robotic, corporate or mass-generated is flagged, and many near-identical emails get the domain blocked — every email must differ in substance, not just the name.
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "If Google sees 50 nearly identical emails, it blocks the domain."
Source: 1.6. Email Deliverability Training (2026 Update) — "Anything that looks templated, robotic, corporate, or mass-generated → flagged."

### FND-WRITE-15 — Never copy the course's templates

Rule (must): Course examples teach the idea; copying them verbatim is how students stay stuck (and land in spam). Generated messages never reuse a course line.
Source: 1.10. Guidelines & Best Practices — "If you try to cheat the system — skip modules, copy and paste templates from the course and look for shortcuts — you’ll stay stuck."
Source: coaching call "4/4 - RME" (2026-04-04) — "And if you are using the same thing, they will just recognize patterns and they will, you know, they won't answer your emails at best. Usually, they like to block those people, report those people. So, stay away from anything that's template"

### FND-WRITE-16 — What AI may and may not do here

Rule (must): AI refines wording, interprets emails, drafts and suggests tactics; it never invents lists of creators or brands and never sets prices. Creators and brands come from real records, prices from the CPM arithmetic, and a person checks facts and decides before anything is sent. Output is plain text, never markdown.
Source: 1.9. AI Masterclass How to Use ChatGPT Like a Pro — "Avoid AI for:
• Copy-pasting emails directly
• Listing influencers or brands
• Pricing influencers or deliverables"
Source: 1.9. AI Masterclass How to Use ChatGPT Like a Pro — "Never trust AI outputs blindly.
Always cross-check facts, rewrite for tone, and ensure compliance."

### FND-WRITE-17 — Chain the steps: analyse, summarize, then draft

Rule (should): One task per step: analyse the data (e.g. is the US audience highest?), summarize the incoming email, then write the reply from both — never one sprawling instruction.
Source: 1.9. AI Masterclass How to Use ChatGPT Like a Pro — "Split large prompts into stages. Bad: "Analyze analytics, summarize email, and reply." Good: 1. "Analyze the analytics and note if US audience is highest." 2. "Summarize this email: [email]." 3. "Write a reply based on analytics and email summary."

### FND-WRITE-18 — Step by step: the first message never carries the whole pitch

Rule (must): Do not put the price, the agency's description, the website and every reason to buy into one opening message. A first message only starts the conversation; the offer and the numbers come once they have engaged and we know what they need.
Don't: Open with price, credentials and a full pitch in one block
Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @29:57 — "Yet, some guys are trying to close influencers or close brands, sending their price. That the company name, the website, the why they're good, everything in one email and just throwing it out. Like for some reason, it would be like a silver bullet, and the guy would just reply, Yes, please bank details so I can send you money. Like, this never happens, you know, it never happens. You need to go step by step."

### FND-WRITE-19 — About five percent warmth

When: An ongoing conversation or relationship; a cold first message goes straight to the point (FND-FMT-02)
Rule (should): Sit closer to dry than gushing, but never cold: a short friendly touch (hoping their weekend went well), now and then a light smiley or a small joke. Use it sparingly — too much reads as childish or needy. The warmth tells them we will be reasonable when something goes wrong later (a late invoice, a cancelled post).
Don't: Jokes and smileys in every message; Flat, robotic replies; The word 'love' in a business email

Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @42:12 — "show respect and care in your communication by managing between not being too excited, like too all over the place with getting a deal or with talking to them, and not being too, you know, too dry, like you really don't care. Source: lesson video "1.2. Learn how to Communicate(most important course)" @38:53 — "Maybe sometimes cracking a joke, like very lightly, you know, from time to time, not all the time. Those kind of things may be things that help you, right? I'm not, but be careful. Like, this can a double-edged sword. It can help you by making you look nicer. It can also make you look like a child and look needy."

### FND-WRITE-20 — Say the unwelcome thing without the words they don't want to hear

Rule (must): Get the point across without hitting their ego: describe the consequence, not the fault. Not 'your budget is too low' but 'with this range we can look at smaller creators'; not 'you are overpriced' but 'this rate will be harder to place'. Nice in tone, unchanged in substance.
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @35:35 — "you need to make people understand what you want them to understand without saying what they don't want to hear."
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @42:12 — "You can say to a brand, your budget is too low without saying that. ### FND-WRITE-21 — No sloppiness

Rule (must): Address them by name, proofread every message (no typos, clean line breaks), answer every question they asked and cover every concern they raised. Small slips tell the reader the agency will run their campaign carelessly too.
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @29:43 — "When you're sending out an email, you actually proofread the email before that."

## Formatting

### FND-FMT-01 — Short, plain, inbox-safe

Rule (must): Aim for about 50 words in a first email and check its shape at a glance (the squint test): compact blocks (the personal line, the offer, then the question on its own line) separated by single line breaks, not every sentence on its own line. Never add links, logos, images, bold text, emojis or a fancy signature to a cold email; sign off with a simple closing and the first name. Once they have replied, links and attachments are fine, and a smiley can come later, in a warm thread.
Don't: Links, images, logos or a designed signature in a first email; Lots of one-line paragraphs and blank lines; Bold text or emojis in a cold email

## Calls (briefs, booking, recap)

### FND-CALL-01 — Propose a call only when email can't do the job

Rule (should): A call has to earn its time. Propose one when both sides need answers that would take three or four emails back and forth, when a new or nervous client needs to see a person before trusting us, when the relationship is ready to go a step further, or for a results review. If the call would only pass information one way, send an email (or a short recorded walkthrough) instead. Deals close over email all the time, so a call is never a required step. If a call would likely go badly (no time to prepare, a bad connection, someone uncomfortable on calls), stay on email. Creators prefer email: do not push them toward a call, but take one if they ask. With brands, proposing a call is good practice; if a brand ignores one call request, stay on email.

### FND-CALL-02 — Booking: a specific day, the time zone, a link that just works

Rule (must): Once a call is agreed, make booking a single step. ## Ethics (halal, honesty)

### FND-ETH-01 — Never lie

Rule (must): No invented clients, deals, results or competing offers, in any message. The course grounds this in Islam and in reputation; the workflow treats any unsupported claim as a blocking error.
Don't: Say 'we are looking at brands like X' unless X is really on the list the agency will pitch; Give a reason for a price or a delay that the agency does not believe
Source: 2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency — "Don’t lie (it's against islam and puts in danger your reputation)"
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "Be Normal and Honest: Don't be sneaky or sketchy."

### FND-ETH-02 — Alignment, not manipulation

Rule (must): Persuasion in every message works by showing real alignment of interests, backed by facts; never by pressure tactics that rely on a false impression.
Source: 2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama — "Do the work with integrity. Build genuine relationships. Focus on alignment, not manipulation."
Source: lesson video "3.4. How to Negotiate with Brands" @7:27 — "This is becoming lying and manipulation if the one of both is wrong. So, if the fact that he promoted something recently makes it that recent, like it will not go well if he promotes again, don't say, oh, he went super well, you're gonna have the same thing."

### FND-ETH-03 — Emergency social proof: last resort, true, owner-approved

Rule (must): When a prospect insists on social proof the agency does not have, try everything else first (redirect, offer specifics, examples). Mentioning an incubator of agency owners or an advisor is allowed only as a last resort, only if true, without names — and the workflow asks a person before using it.
Source: 5.Emergency Social Proof Strategy — "Use this only as a last resort to build trust."
Source: 5.Emergency Social Proof Strategy — "Stay truthful and respectful. Don’t exaggerate or make claims that could backfire."

### FND-ETH-04 — The business rules: honest, clear, consensual, no deception

Rule (must): Brokering between brands and creators is permissible when the service is honest, nothing haram is promoted and agreements are clear. Every message and deal respects: truthfulness, fulfilling contracts, no interest, no unclear terms, mutual consent, no cooperation in sin, no deception.
Source: 1.8. Halal & Haram Aspects of IMA — "An influencer marketing agency is a broker — connecting brands with influencers."
Source: 1.8. Halal & Haram Aspects of IMA — ""Whoever deceives us is not one of us.""

### FND-ETH-05 — Screen every brand: nothing haram is promoted

Rule (must): Never prospect or accept a brand whose product is haram (intoxicants, pork, drugs, musical instruments, interest-based finance and the like). The workflow flags uncertain categories for a person to decide.
Source: 1.8. Halal & Haram Aspects of IMA — "Forbidden things cannot be sold or promoted — such as intoxicants, pork, drugs, or musical instruments."
Source: 1.8. Halal & Haram Aspects of IMA — "A Muslim cannot help promote or support something Haram."

### FND-ETH-06 — Screen every creator: their main content must be halal

Rule (must): Do not work with creators whose primary content is haram. Music elsewhere in a creator's content does not block a deal when the ad itself and the brand are clean.

The agency's exact policy is a house choice; until set, the workflow follows the course and flags such creators.

Rule (must): Objection handling is for a prospect who needs what is offered and would benefit; then it is a duty to help them see it clearly.
