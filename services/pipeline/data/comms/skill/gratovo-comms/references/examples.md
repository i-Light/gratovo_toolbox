# Roast examples — learn from them, never copy them

Student emails judged by a coach on a call. The student emails and the coaches' rewrites are reconstructed from what was read aloud; only the coach's quote is verbatim. Use them to recognise what works and what fails. Never reuse their wording: a message that repeats eight words of any of them is rejected (FND-WRITE-15). Placeholders such as [Brand] mean the facts of a real case go there; never fill them with anything the CASE does not give.

## Influencers — First contact

### EX-01 — verdict: bad (Ammar Halilovic, 2025-09-10)
Situation: A student submits a first cold outreach email to a tech influencer.
Student email (reconstructed): """I've been following your videos and your [video name]. I'll reach out to see if you're open to working with brands in tech space. I'd like to find out a potential partnership that aligns with your audience. Please let me know if this is something you're open to. Looking forward to hearing from you."""
Coach's points: Do not act like a fan by saying 'I've been following your videos and [video title]', which reads like a mail-merge template; a plain 'I saw your last video' is enough. Cut obvious questions, such as asking a tech creator whether they want to work with tech brands, and replace needy phrasing ('I'd like to find a potential partnership') with a confident line naming brands that fit them, worded as brands we plan to approach unless a brand really asked (INF-FIRST-21). One call to action per email.
Coach, verbatim: "see, I'd like to find, I'd love to find, so that means I don't have, I don't have anything to offer you. I would like to, you know, basically do a job for you, if you allow me. So, too, too soft, afraid."
