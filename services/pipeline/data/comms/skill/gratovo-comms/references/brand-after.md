# Brands — After the campaign

### BRD-AFTER-01 — Win the war, not every battle

Rule (should): Let the brand feel it got a good deal, help it make money, and accept a smaller deal now for a bigger one later; brands that win come back.
Source: 3.1. How To Make Offers That Close Instantly — "Rule #1: Don’t try to win every little battle. Win the war."
Source: 3.1. How To Make Offers That Close Instantly — "Rule 2: Make other people money. If you help them win, they will come back."

### BRD-AFTER-02 — One closed deal opens more

Rule (should): After a first deal, pitch the same brand other creators from the roster and reuse the result as a case study with new brands.
Do: Pitch the next creator in the existing thread, so the contact recognises us
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "You can pitch other influencers to the same brand. (and make more money)"
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "You can reuse the case study, which makes things 10x easier."

### BRD-AFTER-03 — Share every win after go-live

Rule (should): After the content is live, keep sending good news — early views, positive comments, engagement — it keeps the brand excited and opens the next campaign.
Source: 3.3. Service delivery — "Even small wins, like positive comments or early engagement metrics, should be shared."
Source: lesson video "3.5. The foot in the door method" @16:00 — "obviously, don't build too much hype if you know it's going to tank after because then it's going to be a pump and dump."

### BRD-AFTER-04 — The money is in the upsell

Rule (should): After a first campaign that went well, propose more creators whose audiences complement the first, and extra formats (short-form, Reels) to amplify results.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "The First Deal is all You Need. Money is in The Upsells."
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "After the first campaign, suggest scaling with more influencers: "We'd love to add [Influencer Name] to your next campaign. His audience complements [first influencer], which would expand your reach and impact of the campaign.""

### BRD-AFTER-05 — Report results to the brand

Rule (must): Forward the campaign analytics to the brand with the good numbers highlighted against what is normal for the niche.
Source: 3.5. Results and reviews — "Once you receive the analytics, forward this data to the brand, especially if the results are positive."

### BRD-AFTER-06 — Check satisfaction, assume it went well, upsell

Rule (should): Ask how satisfied they are and whether the content matched their goals. When results were good, ask on the assumption that it went well and propose a bigger or recurring campaign for the next period. When a video clearly underperformed, do not ask as if it went well: say first that you are not happy with the result and offer the make-good the creator has agreed to (BRD-AFTER-07).
Source: 3.5. Results and reviews — "Always assume they are happy with the results unless they explicitly state otherwise."
Source: 3.5. Results and reviews — "If the campaign results were positive, use the opportunity to upsell the brand to a bigger or recurring deal."

### BRD-AFTER-07 — When results disappoint

Rule (must): Acknowledge the concern; reassure only with reasons that are true; offer a proportionate make-good (an extra story or post) rather than a free full redo, and say clearly what is not possible and why; apologize professionally, without neediness.
Steps: Acknowledge → Reassure only if valid and truthful → Offer compensation of real value → Set clear limits (production costs) → Apologize with confidence and a solution
Source: 3.5. Results and reviews — "Important: Only use this tactic if it's valid and truthful. If the mistake significantly affects the brand's sales or goals, do not try to spin it as a positive. Be transparent."
Source: 3.5. Results and reviews — "A completely free redo of the campaign might not be feasible, especially if it involves costs like influencer fees, production costs, and time."

### BRD-AFTER-08 — Go-live email, then good news at 7 and 14 days

Rule (must): The moment content is live (and checked), email the brand: it is live, the link, the promo code verified, full numbers at 30 days. Several creators: one email or one sheet with all links. Share good news at 7 and 14 days — never go quiet. Early numbers are reported as they are, without apologising: a video often picks up days later.
Source: 4.5. Post Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Send an email the moment content goes live. Keep it short: content is live, here's the link, the promo code is correct and verified, and full performance numbers are coming at the 30-day mark."
Source: 4.5. Post Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Between go-live and the 30-day mark, share updates whenever there's good news at the 7 and 14 day marks. Don't go quiet."

### BRD-AFTER-09 — 30-day report: simple and candid

Rule (must): A clean email with the numbers is enough. Name what worked and what did not before the brand does, with the honest reason (first integration, cold audience — a second integration usually converts better).
Don't: Claim 'we usually see' results the agency has no record of; say what is generally true of first integrations instead
Source: 4.5. Post Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Be honest and name what worked and what didn't before the brand does. If CTR was low, explain why: first integration, cold audience, and a second integration typically converts stronger. Being self-aware beats being defensive every time."
Source: lesson video "4.5. Post Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael" @3:37 — "Make it as frictionless as possible for the rep. This is what people like. This is what busy people like."

### BRD-AFTER-10 — Next step after the campaign

Rule (must): If it went well, propose the next deal tied to something specific (an upcoming video, a topic closer to their product) as a natural next step. If it did not, negotiate a free add-on with the creator (a reel, story or short) and offer it to the brand.
Do: Pitch the second deal once the 30-day numbers are in (sooner only if the views jump), tied to how this one performed; If the brand says no to more with this creator, offer other creators who fit
Source: 4.5. Post Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "If the campaign went well, pitch the next deal and tie it to something specific, a video the creator has coming up or a topic that fits the brand's product better than the last one. Frame it as a natural next step, not a new sale."
Source: 4.5. Post Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "A free add-on after a flop keeps the door open for a second deal."

### BRD-AFTER-11 — Close-out checklist

Rule (must): A campaign is closed only when: the go-live email went out with the link, screenshots were saved on day one, the 30-day metrics were pulled and reported, the creator's payment was triggered and confirmed, and a follow-up (next deal or add-on) was sent.
Source: 4.5. Post Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Confirm the go-live email was sent to the brand with the live link, screenshots of the post were saved on day one, the 30-day metrics were pulled and the report was sent, the creator's payment was triggered and confirmed, and a follow-up was sent, either the next deal or a free add-on."

### BRD-AFTER-12 — Review a big campaign on a short call; upsell later by email

Rule (should): After a big deal (five figures or more) has gone live, offer a fifteen-minute call to review the results: show what happened, hear how it went on their side, and agree what comes next. Don't upsell on that call, because it sounds greedy. Afterwards, email the upsell and tie it to how well the first campaign went. With a mid-sized brand, after two or three good campaigns, a short email can propose a call to take things further. Prepare the call and leave it with a next step (FND-CALL-04, FND-CALL-06).
Source: lesson video "8.How To Take sales calls" @53:13 — "Set up a call to analyze the results when everything went live, summarize the campaign. And usually, this is a great time to collect information to upsell them a bigger deal later on. Not on that call because you sound super greedy."
Source: lesson video "8.How To Take sales calls" @51:56 — "hey, the first few campaigns were amazing. I just wanted to actually take it to the next level. We prepared X, Y, and Z. We currently have this other brand. I'd love to speak to you about it."

### BRD-AFTER-13 — Retainers sell a monthly outcome

Rule (tip): A recurring (retainer) deal pays for ongoing value. Move from selling single creator videos to selling a monthly outcome with a fixed target (views, link clicks, new users) within the brand's budget; we choose and negotiate the creators to hit it. It fits once we have run campaigns and know the numbers well enough to commit to a target honestly.
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 1" @5:06 — "To do that, you need to go from selling just influencer campaigns and paying for an influencer And for video to paying for an actual service, which is you getting them results when it comes to marketing, or you're getting them a certain amount of traffic, or you're getting them a certain amount of views on a monthly basis."
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 1" @5:20 — "They need to have a fixed KPI that they're looking for. For example, we want to get X amount of views or X amount of link clicks or X amount of new users to our platform with a brand."

### BRD-AFTER-15 — Asking a brand for results

Rule (should): Ask about three to four weeks after go-live, when views have mostly settled. Ask first how it went for them; if it went well, ask whether they'd share the numbers, with the true reason (so we can shape the next offer around what worked). Brands often keep exact sales private, especially when results were very good; their feedback is still worth having. Ask permission before quoting their words or results on our website.
Source: coaching call "9/6" (2025-06-09) — "yeah, usually three to four weeks after the video's live, Yes. Yeah. Yeah, because usually after four weeks, it doesn't go much more up than that."
Source: coaching call "July 25th: Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-07-25) — "Let me know more details, da, da, da. If they say, oh, perform good, then you ask them, hey, would you send results? We would like to have them for multiple reasons, you know, this and that. But keep in mind, if it's super, super good, you know, it performed very well, they might not like to send it to you because they kind of giving the power to you"

### BRD-AFTER-16 — A rebooking after a first-deal price

Rule (should): If the first deal was priced low as a first partnership, say so plainly when they come back: one more video goes at the normal rate, or the first-deal rate holds for a multi-video package with the same creator. Ask for more only when the results were good; never raise the price just because it's the second deal.

### BRD-AFTER-17 — Upsell in small steps: the same creator first, then one or two more

Rule (should): When the first video did well, offer the same creator again first (a few videos, for example one a month), then add one or two similar creators; never a list of seven or ten at once. Each yes shows what the brand wants, and the next suggestion follows it closely.
Source: coaching call "September 23: Q&A (Advanced Brands) - Osama" (2025-09-25) — "essentially when you upsell to the brand, you want to give them, you want to give them like little droplets, right? You don't want to give them a whole list of like seven influences, ten influences, you want to give them, like you did a deal with one influencer, cool. I'm going to give you just one more, just to see if I'm getting... If niche right, and if I'm getting the average views right, and the engagement, and the audience"
