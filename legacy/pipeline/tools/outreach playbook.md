## 1. Core Principles

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|------|-----|
| 1.1 | **You are not a fan. You are a business partner offering value.** | The single most-repeated rule across both sources. Fan energy costs you deals, price, and respect. |
| 1.2 | **Emotions are for family. Business is business.** No "love", "amazed", "super excited". | Emotional language signals you're desperate, new, and probably copying a template. |
| 1.3 | **The goal of the first email is a REPLY, not a closed deal.** | Pitching the whole deal up front kills reply rates. Discovery first, close later. |
| 1.4 | **Cold email is a numbers game with a brain.** Hundreds of 8/10 emails beat a handful of "perfect" ones. | Even a perfect email gets missed. |
| 1.5 | **Action over analysis.** Send to learn what works; don't over-polish drafts. | 30+ min per email is unsustainable and doesn't improve results. |
| 1.6 | **The offer is the most important element.** Personalization is a supporting detail, not the centerpiece. | A specific offer is what earns the reply. |
| 1.7 | **Specific beats generic, every time.** Every section of the email is subject to a specificity test. | See [The Two Tests](#the-two-tests). |
| 1.8 | **Never lie.** No fake brand relationships, no fake titles, no fake scarcity, no "exclusive" claims. | One follow-up question exposes it and destroys trust. |
| 1.9 | **Never repeat patterns.** Same opener, same offer structure, same follow-up script = spam flag. | Providers detect templated repetition across sends. |
| 1.10 | **Diagnose before you rewrite.** Fix tech first, then leads, then copy. | See [The Three Pillars](#three-pillars-of-cold-outreach). |
| 1.11 | **You have 100K+ other options.** If this one says no, move to the next. | Prevents the neediness that leaks into copy after rejections. [BP] |
| 1.12 | **Judge emails by metrics, not opinions.** Reply rate and **positive** reply rate are the only true indicators of whether an email works. | Subjective feedback (yours or anyone else's) is not evidence. Track it or you're guessing. [RL] |

### Professional vs Desperate

| | Professional | Desperate |
|---|---|---|
| **Traits** | Confident, direct, specific | Emotional, vague, pleading |
| **Sounds like** | "We work with creators like you on campaigns for [Brand A] and [Brand B]. Should I send the brief?" | "I really hope you'll consider this. It would mean the world to us." |
| **Effect** | Offers | Begs |

### Three Pillars of Cold Outreach
Diagnose failing campaigns in this order - fixing copy while the tech is broken is wasted work. [RL]

1. **Technical (Deliverability)** - DNS, SPF, DKIM, DMARC, MX records, warm-up. The foundation.
2. **Leads (List)** - Are you reaching the actual decision-maker, or a gatekeeper?
3. **Copy & Offer** - Subject line, personalization, value proposition, CTA. Refine continuously once 1 and 2 are solid.

### The Two Tests
Apply both to every email before sending. [BP]

- **The 10-Person Test (personalization):** *Could you send this exact personalization to 10 other people?* If yes, it's generic. Redo it.
- **The Google Test (subject line):** *Could you Google this subject line and find 100 other emails using it?* If yes, don't use it.

Corollary for the body: **could you send this whole email to 10 other people?** If yes, it's a template, not an email.

> A template tries to appeal to everyone. A real email appeals to one person. Write real emails. [BP]

## 2. Power Dynamics

**Power dynamics = who has control of the conversation.**

- Needy, desperate, or uncertain -> **they** have control.
- Confident, specific, and valuable -> **you** have control.

This matters commercially, not just tonally: over-praise and fan energy inflate the influencer's price expectations and weaken every subsequent negotiation. [RL] [BP]

### The Five Rules

**1. Never apologize or over-explain.**
- Banned: "I'm sorry to bother you, but...", "I hope this isn't too much, but..."
- Instead: direct subject + value proposition. No preamble.

**2. Don't ask permission for everything.**
- Weak (asks permission): "Would you be open to hearing about this?", "Is it okay if I send you details?"
- Strong (offers a choice): "Should I send the brief?"
- The strong version *assumes* they want to hear more. You're giving them a choice, not requesting one.

**3. Mention names confidently.**
- Weak: "we work with brands in your space."
- Strong: "we're working with NordVPN and [other brand]."
- **Only if true.** [RL] is emphatic that inventing brand relationships is fatal. If you genuinely have them, name 2-3. If you don't, use honest service framing instead (see [The Offer](#55-the-offer)), not vague implication. See [Conflict H](#17-where-the-sources-disagree).

**4. Never say "maybe" or "perhaps".**
- Banned: "Perhaps you might be interested..."
- Instead: "We work with creators like you on campaigns for [Brand]."

**5. The Frog in the Pot Principle.**
You cannot walk them down to a low price in one email. Negotiate across the sequence.

| Email | Move |
|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce the creator. "Should I send more details?" |
| 2 | Send analytics. "Would you like to discuss rates?" |
| 3 | Negotiate. "We can do $3K instead of $5K." |

Email 1 -> "only $500 available" -> they bail.
Email 1 -> Email 2 -> Email 3 at $3K -> you close.

### The Mental Game
- Every email is business. Separate emotion from the deal.
- Don't get emotional after rejections.
- Don't say "maybe someday" - it limits you.
- Re-read this section when your emails aren't converting, and whenever you feel desperate. [BP]

## 3. Deliverability & Infrastructure

### Domains & Inboxes
- Use **.com, .net, or .org**. Avoid `.online` - it needs far more warm-up and still trips filters.
- Use **secondary domains** for cold outreach; keep your primary brand domain out of the blast radius.
- Run **2-3 domains x 2-3 inboxes each** to distribute volume and contain reputation damage.
- **Sender name: use a personal first name** ("Ali"), not a company name. Higher open rates.
- On a brand-new domain, a **simple first name** in the signature is often best.
- **Profile picture: a real, professional photo of yourself.** Builds trust, humanizes the send.
- Set **DMARC policy to `quarantine`** for better Gmail inbox placement.

### Warm-Up
- Warm up a new account for **2 weeks minimum** before any outreach (longer-horizon guidance: keep warming 60-90 days).
- **Instantly warm-up baseline: 10 emails/day, 30% reply rate, weekdays only.**
- Raise warm-up reply rate to **80% only after 4-5 weeks** - doing it early is a common cause of landing in spam.
- **Keep warming continuously** (~10/day) even during active campaigns.
- After warm-up, start outreach at **5-10 emails/day** and ramp gradually to 20-30/day.
- Instantly's free warm-up trial is 2 weeks; then pay, or switch to an alternative like Warmup My Inbox.

### Send Volume
- **Cap total daily sends at ~50 per inbox.** That total includes warm-up + initial outreach + follow-ups + tests + replies.
- Example split: 10 warm-up + 30-35 outreach + 5-10 replies.
- **Follow-ups count toward the daily total.**
- **Space out sends** to mimic human behavior. Blasting many emails in quick succession is a spam trigger.
- **Pause cold outreach on weekends.** It breaks the automated-looking pattern and lets the domain rest. Use weekends for research, drafting, and pre-building offers.
- Set explicit daily sending limits in your tool (Instantly) so you never exceed provider caps.
- **Holiday season: maintain volume.** Someone is always in the office.

### Spam Triggers to Eliminate
- **Open-rate trackers (pixel images)** - trigger Google spam warnings and damage domain reputation.
- **Links** of any kind in the first email (media kits, portfolios, videos). **Multiple links in a signature is a direct route to spam.** [BP]
- **Images**, including in signatures. Image-heavy signatures are a major red flag.
- **Heavy HTML / pasted rich text.** Excessive blank lines inflate email size and trip filters.
- **Fancy signatures** - keep minimal or plain text; a decorated signature reads as a promotion.
- **Identical or near-identical emails** sent at volume.
- **Copy-pasted follow-up scripts.**
- **Spammy words in brand outreach:** "paid", "money", "guarantee". Plus the full [banned list](#14-banned-words--phrases).

> **Calibration:** don't over-index on individual phrases. Ordinary business language like **"we work with" is fine** - the overall content and sending pattern of the email are what filters actually weigh. The [banned list](#14-banned-words--phrases) exists mostly because those phrases are *weak copy*, not because any single one is a filter trigger. [RL]

- **"I hope you're doing well"** - so universal it actively hurts deliverability. [BP]
- **Bold text** in initial emails.
- **Emojis.**
- **Fake "RE:"** prefixes on new threads.

### Formatting Rules
- **Plain text only** for initial cold emails.
- On Windows, paste with **Ctrl + Shift + V** to strip formatting.
- **Consistent font. No bolding in initial emails** - save bold for offers in follow-ups only. [BP]
- **No emojis.** Kills professionalism and open rate.
- **No em-dashes.** They immediately read as AI-generated. Use a regular hyphen (-) or a period. [BP]
- **Compact spacing.** Multiple blank lines create "hurdles": they make the email look long, break flow, and trigger filters. Use single line breaks between the three sections, nothing more.
- **3 sections maximum**: personalization, offer, CTA.
- Always include a **short professional sign-off** ("Best," / "Best regards,"). Never "Faithfully yours", "Yours truly", or "Thanks and regards" (thanking them for what?).
- Run copy through **Grammarly**; grammatical errors and inconsistent fonts read as low-effort spam.
- Follow-ups may be slightly richer than the initial email, but stay conservative.

## 4. List Building & Targeting

### Niche Selection
- **Avoid broad niches** ("SaaS, Productivity, Tech"). A tight sub-niche improves targeting and results.
- **Avoid niches that are too narrow** ("Muslim men's fitness") - you need enough influencers and brands to sustain volume.
- **Follow the influencers, not the brands.** If creators in your niche work with a brand, that brand buys influencer marketing - reach out to them.

### Influencer Selection
- Target creators with **50k+ average views per video** - the workload is identical to a small creator, the revenue is not.
- **Sweet spot to start: creators in the 120k-150k view range.** Goldmine for experience and profit.
- **Avoid 1M+ view creators initially** - usually agency-managed and need advanced negotiation.
- **Nano-influencers still have a place** - brands with small budgets or very specific audience needs.
- Build a list of **at least 150 prospects** to sustain volume.
- **Build a roster of 5+ influencers before pitching brands.** Pitching a single creator burns the lead.
- **Don't assume a creator is unavailable** because their recent videos were unsponsored or self-promoting. Email them anyway.
- Use **SponsorBlock** to detect sponsorships on YouTube: **yellow = brand sponsorship, green = self-promo (merch)**. Affiliate links in a bio are NOT sponsorships and don't count as brand deals.
- **Calculating average views:** take the last 12 videos, drop the highest and lowest, average the remaining 10.
- **Finding influencer emails:** YouTube bio/description -> Instagram Linktree -> Facebook page. Tools: Nano-Influencer and SimilarTube (30 reveals each); a fresh YouTube account unlocks more reveals.

### Where to Find Research Material

**For influencers:** [BP]
1. Their recent videos (see the [timeboxing note](#13-workflow--speed) - [RL] caps this at a 2-minute clip; [BP] suggests scanning the last 5).
2. Specific details, not generic observations.
3. Their website / LinkedIn for recent achievements.
4. **Sponsorships they DON'T have yet** - this is where the offer angle comes from.

**For brands:** [BP]
1. Their website for upcoming launches.
2. LinkedIn for recent hires and promotions.
3. Instagram for upcoming campaigns.
4. Their blog for recent articles.

### Brand Contact Targeting
- **Target decision-makers, not gatekeepers.**
  - Small companies: CEO / founder first, then marketing, sales, growth managers.
  - Larger companies: Head of Marketing, CMO, Marketing Manager, sales leads.
- **Apollo search titles:** "Partnerships", "Growth", "Marketing", "Digital Marketing".
- **Avoid the wrong titles** - e.g. "Affiliate Specialist" is not the influencer-marketing decision-maker.
- **A recent promotion into a partnerships role is a prime opening** - it is both a research signal and a personalization hook: *"Saw you got promoted to Head of Marketing at [Company]. Since you're now handling partnerships, I thought I'd reach out."* [BP]
- **Email 2-3 contacts at the same brand simultaneously**, with slightly varied personalization.
  - If two reply, ask who the decision-maker is.
  - Pitching the same influencer to multiple contacts: use a **different offer** per contact.
  - Group pitch: keep the same creators but **vary the copy**.
- If no reply after the full sequence, **rotate to 2-3 new contacts** at the company.
- **Re-engage original contacts after 2-3 months** - enough time has passed to look fresh.
- **Verify the brand actually runs influencer marketing** before pitching. Don't name brands that don't do it, or that are too small to be impressive.

### Non-Marketing Contacts (Referral Play)
When you land on someone who clearly isn't the right person, send a **low-friction referral ask** instead of a pitch:
> "I'm having trouble finding the right contact for influencer collaborations. Could you point me in the right direction?"

## 5. Anatomy of a Cold Email

**Hierarchy of importance:** Subject Line -> Offer -> CTA -> Personalization.

### 5.0 The Template

```
Hi [Name],

[PERSONALIZATION - 2-3 sentences]

[OFFER - 2-3 sentences]

[CTA - 1 sentence]

Best,
[Your Name]
```

That's it. No more. Three sections, single line breaks, no signature block. [BP]

### 5.1 Length
| Context | Target |
|---|---|
| Initial email (general) | **50-70 words / ~6 sentences max** |
| Initial brand (B2B) email | **30-50 words**, ~50 words is the sweet spot |
| Follow-up | **25-30 words / ~3 sentences** |
| First follow-up specifically | **max 3 sentences** |

Suggested internal split for a 50-70 word email: **3 sentences offer, 2 sentences personalization, 1 sentence CTA.**

B2B proportional split: **~20% personalization / ~65% offer / ~15% CTA.**

### 5.2 Subject Line

**Your subject line is ~70% of your open rate.** [BP] If the email isn't opened, nothing else you wrote matters. It is the email's "thumbnail". [RL]

**Three patterns that work:** [BP]

| Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity + Personalization** | "Your [specific thing] video sparked an idea"; "Question about your recent [topic] post"; "[Name], quick question"; "Your backflip video caught my eye"; "Question about your collaboration with [Brand]" |
| **Curiosity + Mystery** | "You were right about [topic]"; "An idea for your next campaign"; "Collaboration idea" |
| **Curiosity Loop** | "Quick question for [Name]"; "Your reach" (unfinished); "Quick one for you" |

**Audience split:** [RL]
- **Influencers: hyper-specific and personalized**, tied directly to the personalization line.
  - "Enzo, wake up" (a YouTuber who modified a Ferrari); "Your video made a point"; "Tom enjoyed this. You will too." (creator's dog is named Tom); "Make things easier for yourself"; "Is it that easy?"
  - **Personalized Subject Lines (PSLs):** write a unique curiosity-driven line per influencer.
- **Brands: generic and curiosity-driven**, so it doesn't read as a sales pitch.
  - "Thoughts on this?"; "Quick question"; "Hey [Name]"; "Jack, thoughts?"; "Are you sure?"; "Did this happen to you?"; "Edward pointed me your way" (high reply rate); "Idea for your Q3 campaign"; "Your recent product launch"; "[Name], thoughts?"

**Patterns that don't work:**
- "Collaboration Request" - everyone uses it.
- "Partnership Opportunity" / "Partnership Request" - spam-folder favourite; also implies you already have a deal ready.
- "I Hope You're Doing Well" - overused.
- "Brand Sponsorship" / "Paid Partnership" - too obvious, a direct pitch rather than a curiosity loop.
- Agency-branded lines: "Norvipian X Miskeen Agency" - irrelevant to the recipient.
- Generic course templates - 800+ other people are sending the same line.
- Aggressive / misleading lines: "Unskippable, please open urgently" - sets expectations the body can't meet, which produces spam reports.
- Overly personalized lines that can be misread ("Would it help with joint pain?").

**Rules:**
- **Apply the Google Test.** If you could Google it and find 100 identical emails, rewrite it.
- **Don't repeat subject-line keywords in the first sentence** of the body.
- **A/B test subject lines constantly** - the cheapest lever on open rate. Try 2-3 variations.
- **Vary subject lines across a follow-up sequence.**

### 5.3 Personalization

**Purpose: prove you did homework. Not fan energy, not love, not amazement - proof of research**, plus a hook into the offer. Nothing more.

**What to reference:** [BP]
- A specific recent video or post.
- A specific *detail* from that content, never generic praise.
- A recent professional achievement (promotion, silver play button, new product launch).
- Something from their website or LinkedIn.

**Rules:**
- **One single, specific, recent detail.** Not a summary, not a list, not an analysis.
- **From recent content** - old videos don't work, the creator has forgotten the details.
- **Add your own thought or observation**, don't just restate what happened.
- **Non-flattering / neutral details work better than compliments** ("That chair must be a paid actor").
- **Brand personalization is different:** comment on their **marketing**, not their product. A recent creator collab, a campaign, a competitor's strategy, a LinkedIn post, a product launch. You are a marketing peer, not a customer.
- **Personalization must be bridgeable to the offer.** If you can't connect it in one line, it's the wrong detail.
- **No personalization at all is a valid test** for brand outreach - it can stand out. Test it deliberately. [RL]

**Good examples:**
- "I watched your recent BMW restoration video. The way you diagnosed the fuel pump issue - just swapping the solenoid - was a solid catch."
- "I watched your latest video on the iPhone 15 camera. The way you compared it to the Sony A7 was spot-on."
- "The progression from Navimo to CI/CD made by vector time actually clicked."
- "Those blue doors are really squeaky. I wish I could adjust them for you."
- "The tree on your way up to the top didn't seem secure at all."
- "Saw you got promoted to Head of Marketing at [Company]. Since you're now handling partnerships, I thought I'd reach out."
- "I noticed you're launching the new product line this month. Timing's good."
- Empathy angle: "Pulling in sponsors while trying to make content can be tedious."

**Failure modes:**
| Failure | Example |
|---|---|
| Generic praise | "I love your content", "Your videos are amazing", "I loved your video", "decent content", "Your content is really engaging" |
| Vague observation | "I noticed you're doing great work", "I noticed you have a large following", "I saw you're in the fitness space", "The way you built your channel is impressive" |
| Video summary | "In your last video, you spent 60 quid on..." |
| Fanboy POV | "I was shocked", "It was my first time learning", "I'm proud of you, man", "Your story really motivated me", "Such amazing scenery" |
| Creepy | "I've been watching your content for a while now" |
| Self-focused | "I thought I wanted to share...", your own background or opinions |
| Overlong / unbridgeable | A Muhammad Ali quote with no path to the offer; a long technical teardown of a product |
| Customer-review tone (brands) | Praising the product instead of the marketing |
| Personal life events | "Congrats on your baby" - overused. (A *professional* promotion is fine; a personal milestone is not.) |
| A question | "It seems like you haven't had any partnerships?" - makes it a survey, not an opportunity |
| Correcting their content | Never. Unprofessional, gets you blocked. |
| Sloppy openers | "Your recent Sunday recess..." |
| Unverified claims | "I'm not sure if your Cal Newport recommendation was sponsored or personal" - confirm before you write |

**The Specificity Checklist** - all four must be true: [BP]
- [ ] Mentions a specific video / post / achievement?
- [ ] Mentions a specific detail, not generic praise?
- [ ] Could NOT be sent to 5-10 other people?
- [ ] Shows you actually engaged with their work?

### 5.4 Bridge Line
The transition from personalization to offer. Not mandatory, but it's what makes an email read as one message instead of two pasted halves. [RL]

- Use a bridge word or phrase: **"Speaking of..."**
  - "Speaking of things that are secure, we can help you secure sponsorships..."
- **Avoid awkward pivots** like "Jokes aside...".
- **Order matters** - sometimes swapping personalization and bridge is stronger:
  - Original: "Probably a matter of time... [bridge] ...Claude script images seems like a life hack."
  - Improved: "Claude script images seems like a life hack... [bridge] ...Probably a matter of time."
- A generic offer pasted after a specific opening feels jarring. The offer must flow **logically** from the personalization.

### 5.5 The Offer

**Your offer = what you're proposing + why they fit.** Not "we're a cool agency", not "we work with awesome brands".

**Four-step structure:** [BP]
1. State who you represent / work with.
2. Mention 1-2 names (creators or brands).
3. Say why **they specifically** are a fit.
4. Make the offer.

**Rules:**
- **Sell benefits-of-benefits**, not features. [RL]
  - Feature: creator does a Squid Game challenge.
  - Benefit: audience sees real people in a unique scenario.
  - Benefit of benefit: audience feels immersed -> powerful emotional connection for the brand.
- **Sell an outcome, not a stat.** Numbers alone give no business reason to partner.
- **Explain WHY the fit works**, always.
- **Start with what THEY get, not what YOU want.** Never open the offer with "I'd love to connect..." or "We're looking for...". [BP]
- **Don't repeat yourself.** "Hi, I hope you're well. I'm reaching out because... I wanted to see if..." is three openings for one message. One clear message, no redundancy. [BP]
- **Frame around improvement and ROI**, not replication.
  - Bad: "We work with creators like Jordan who could bring the same lifestyle integration."
  - Good: "We noticed an opportunity to improve the ROI of your past campaign. Jordan's approach to [angle] could achieve [outcome]."
- **Include a real timeline or urgency** - "launching next month", "onboarding for Q4", "finalizing the list this week". This is **real** urgency tied to an actual campaign, never invented scarcity. [BP] + [RL]
- **Name brands only if the relationship is real.** If it is, name 2-3 confidently. If it isn't, use honest service framing ("We're building a list of creators for upcoming campaigns with brands like X") - never vague implication. See [Conflict H](#17-where-the-sources-disagree).
- **Say "we work with" / "we collaborate with"** - only say "represent" if your agency email is genuinely in the creator's official contact info.
- **Use "I" for initial outreach** to build a direct human connection. Use "we" only once you actually have a team or agency.
- **Never overpromise:** no "better brands", no "structuring your Q3/Q4 campaigns" if you're new, no "Anchor would be the perfect sponsor".
- **Pre-build 5-10 distinct offers** rather than reusing one. See [Offer Testing Framework](#offer-testing-framework).

**Good offer examples:**
- "We work with creators in the automotive restoration space. You have 150K average views and 12% engagement - that's strong. We're onboarding influencers for a tech brand launching next month."
- "I represent two creators: [Name], who averages 200K views with automotive content, and [Name], who specializes in product reviews. Both have 40%+ US audiences with high engagement. Should I send their profiles?"
- "We're working with NordVPN and Raycon on Q4 campaigns. You fit the profile. Both are looking for creators in your niche with 50K+ US audience."
- "We have an influencer who makes Steam Deck content and has never showcased a gaming chair. His audience would be interested in his preferred brand."
- "This creator's audience is buying cars and needs to check them - a perfect fit for your service."
- "Pete is full of energy" -> pitch to an energy drink brand (not his view count).
- "This mechanic is restoring a Mustang" -> natural integration for a tool brand.

**Bad offer examples:**
- "We have some amazing creators who would love to work with your brand." ("Amazing" is fluff.)
- "We specialize in connecting influencers with brands." (Everyone says this.)
- "We think your audience would be a great fit for our network." (No specifics.)
- "We'd like to discuss a collaboration." (No offer.)
- "Would you be open to exploring partnership opportunities?" (Too soft, no offer.)
- "We believe there's a great opportunity here." (Says nothing.)
- "We represent several amazing creators who would love to work with your brand."
- "Content that naturally incorporates brand messaging" (AI-speak, explains nothing).

**The Offer Checklist** - 3+ of 4 means the offer is strong: [BP]
- [ ] Mentions specific brands OR creators by name?
- [ ] Mentions a metric (views, engagement, audience location)?
- [ ] Explains WHY they're a fit, not just "you're great"?
- [ ] Has a clear timeline or urgency?

### 5.6 The CTA

**Your CTA in an initial email is "respond to this email"** - not "sign a contract". One CTA per email. A single, direct, low-friction, yes/no question.

**The formula:** [BP]
- **Strong = Statement + Question** - "We work with creators like you. Should I send more details?"
- **Weak = Question + Question** - "Would you be interested in learning more?"

**The power-dynamics rule:** in initial emails, the CTA puts the ball in **their** court. It does not ask permission.
- "Should I send more details?" -> they have to say yes or no.
- "Would you be open?" -> they can just ignore it.

**Strong (forces a response):**
- "Should I send the brief?"
- "Should I send more details?"
- "Should I send over their analytics?"
- "Should I send his profile over?"
- "Can I send you [Creator]'s profile?"
- "Are you interested?"
- "Are you open to partnerships?" / "Are you open to exploring partnerships?"
- "Would that interest you?"
- "Are you the right person to talk about this?"
- "Who is the right person to connect with?"

**Weak (easy to ignore) - do not use in an initial email:**

| Banned | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Would you be open to this?" | Asks permission, zero power dynamics |
| "Are you interested in exploring this?" | Easy out |
| "Let me know if this might be a fit." | Easy out |
| "Let me know if you're interested." | Passive, puts the work on them |
| "I'm happy to discuss further if you'd like." | Pleading |
| "I'm curious if you'd be interested..." | Self-focused, hedged |
| "I'm hoping we can work together." | Begging |
| "Does this sound useful to you?" | Invites a "no" |
| "Do you think it makes sense to explore this?" | Invites a "no" |
| "Please let me know." | No prompt |
| "Worth a conversation" | Vague |
| "Are you happy to receive their media kits?" | Tentative, needy |
| "Shall I send the analytics?" | Weak framing |
| "Want me to send details?" | Asks about *their* desire. "**Should** I send details?" assumes the next step and is the direct form |
| "Happy to send if you're interested." | Passive |
| "Can I hear back from you?" | Desperate |
| "Curious about the reason behind it?" | Creates friction |
| "Let's hop on a 10-minute call." | Too much friction for email #1 |
| "Let's book a chat." | Closing too early |
| "I'd love to hear from you." / "Feel free to reach out." | No demand for a response |

> **Contested:** [BP] classes *"Let me know your thoughts"* and *"What do you think?"* as **strong** (they demand an opinion); [RL] repeatedly flags them as **weak**. Default to an explicit yes/no question and treat these two as fallbacks only. See [Conflict A](#17-where-the-sources-disagree).

**Additional rules:**
- **Multiple CTAs dilute the message.** One only.
- **The CTA must relate directly to the offer** you just made.
- **Long, complex questions create work** for the recipient and get ignored.
- **Do not attach analytics unless asked.**
- **For influencers specifically: do NOT use "Should I send more details?"** - it invites budget/timeline/contract questions you may not be ready to answer. Prefer "Are you open to partnerships?" or a direct analytics request. [RL]
- **CTA strength varies by stage:** strong in email 1, softer as the sequence progresses. See [Section 8](#8-follow-up-sequences).

## 6. Influencer Outreach (B2C)

**Goal:** get a reply. **Tone:** casual but professional, value-driven, direct. Never a fan.

### Structure
1. Personalized, curiosity-driven subject line tied to the personalization.
2. One specific observation from recent content.
3. Bridge line.
4. Pain-based offer.
5. One yes/no CTA.

### Offer Framing by Sponsorship Status
Diagnose their situation first (SponsorBlock, and look for sponsorships they **don't** have yet), then pick the matching angle:

| Their situation | Angle |
|---|---|
| **No sponsors** | "I noticed your last few videos weren't sponsored. We can help fill those empty slots with relevant brands." |
| **Some sponsors** | "We can help you get better-fitting, long-term partnerships." |
| **Many sponsors** | Flip it - high sponsorship volume is a green flag proving they convert. "You clearly deliver for brands; we specialize in securing long-term, high-value deals" / "we can fill the gaps we saw in your last few videos" / "we can help you avoid audience fatigue by finding more relevant sponsors." |

### Additional Offer Angles (rotate to avoid repetition)
- **Pain-focused:** "I noticed you haven't been doing sponsorships lately..."
- **Diagnostic + solution:** "Only 2 of your last 10 videos were sponsored" -> offer the fix.
- **Realization-focused:** "I realized you work with X brand; Y brand could be a good fit."
- **Request-focused:** "You mentioned needing a new keyboard; I can connect you with brands."
- **Service-framed (honest when you have no brands yet):** "We're building a list of creators for upcoming campaigns with brands like X" / "We can find brands that fit your content" / "We can line up brands that fit your content and manage the deal end-to-end."
- **Audience-value framed:** "Pickleball and hydration brands are looking for creators with a fitness-oriented audience." Influencers sell **access to their audience's attention**, not their personal preferences - frame the offer around what their audience is worth to a brand.
- **Concrete brand names (only if real):** "I have brands X & Y in mind for you."

**Provider positioning:** "We are lining up authentic creators with brands. If this interests you, let me know" beats "Would you consider a partnership?" - the first positions you as the provider; the second asks them to do the work.

**Build a curiosity loop, don't pitch.** The initial email's job is to make them interested enough to *ask you for more*, not to present the whole deal. Briefly state why they're a good fit, then ask if they're open to partnerships. Hold the detail back for the reply. [RL]

**Personalization is about THEM, not you.** A long, self-focused intro gets ignored regardless of how well written it is.

### Tone & Language Rules
- **Greetings:** "Hi [Name]" or "Hey there" / "Hello team" for cold outreach. Never "Dear Sir/Madam". **Religious greetings only after a relationship exists.**
- **Neutral, not inflated language:** "a strong fit", not "a great fit". Over-praise inflates their price expectations.
- **Professional phrasing:** "Your take on X was insightful", "The video's analysis was compelling."
- **No self-introduction in the first email.** Too early; it shifts focus from them to you.
- **Don't mimic an influencer's casual style** or open with jokes in initial outreach - it lowers your power dynamic.
- **Never point out flaws** (low subscriber growth, mistakes in their content). Instant alienation.
- **Avoid managerial / exclusivity language** - "Let me handle the business side" implies management and exclusivity, which deters creators.
- **Don't ask if their videos are sponsored.** Assume they're open to deals and present the opportunity.
- **Don't use the word "partnership"** in a vague offer - it implies you have a specific deal ready and invites questions you can't answer.
- **No false urgency or scarcity** ("Only a few spots remain"). Real campaign deadlines are fine; invented ones are not.

## 7. Brand Outreach (B2B)

**Goal:** a reply from a busy marketing manager. **Tone:** professional, formal, concise, marketing-fluent.

### Structure
- **30-50 words.** Decision-makers may run long emails through AI filters.
- **~20% personalization / ~65% offer / ~15% CTA.**
- **The offer is paramount** - do NOT lead with personalization the way you would for an influencer.
- **Plain text, no links, no images, no video, no signature block.** Follow-ups can be longer and more detailed.

### Personalization (B2B)
- **Never reference the influencer's video when emailing a brand.** It is unprofessional and irrelevant to the brand's business goals - they don't care what a creator did, they care what it does for them. This is the most common B2B personalization mistake. [RL]
- **Reference a business achievement instead:** a recent product launch, a funding round, a major retail deal (e.g. landing in Walmart), a new market, a recent campaign.
- **One concise, relevant line.** A single sharp sentence outperforms a long, detailed analysis - and the analysis eats the word budget the offer needs.
- You are writing as a **marketing peer**, not a customer and not a fan of their creator.

### Offer Rules
- **Frame around past performance, not future promises.** "This creator has done X" beats "This creator can do Y."
- **Pitch 2-3 creators, never one.** De-risks the offer, prevents rejection on a single creator, shows range.
- **Lead with your strongest creators** - 50%+ US/UK audience and strong average views. Frame smaller creators as a **cost-effective alternative to one large creator**.
- **Metrics that matter:**
  - **Views over subscribers.** Views drive sales; subscribers guarantee nothing.
  - **Views-per-video, not monthly views.** Brands buy a single integration.
  - **Engagement rate over raw views.** A 7% ER creator beats a bigger 1% ER creator.
  - **Specific audience demographics with a country breakdown:** "31% US, 26% UK" - not "64% Tier 1", not a bare "56% US audience, age 34-52".
- **Metrics that fail:**
  - "Over 1 million combined followers" / "33% combined reach" - hides individual performance and exposes a thin roster.
  - "2.4M impressions" with no platform, timeframe, or engagement rate.
  - Raw views alone - invites conversion-rate questions you can't answer. Prefer past collaborations as proof.
  - Any stat without context. Add timeframe: "1.87 billion views on Incogni content over the last 6 months."
- **Research the brand's ICP and current marketing** before writing the offer.
- **Identify a marketing flaw or opportunity** in their recent campaigns and explain how your creators fix it.
- **Propose a campaign idea**, don't just send a media kit.
- **"Brands won't pay for the same audience twice"** - "similar audience to your last creator" is a losing pitch. Show unique value.

### Offer Testing Framework
Build **5-10 distinct offers** by mixing and matching these types, then generate Spintax variations and track which wins:

1. **Audience Analytics** - "40k views, 90% US audience."
2. **Content Fit** - "Your backflip videos are a natural fit for X brand."
3. **Case Studies** - "Your long-term partnership with Y brand proves you deliver results."
4. **Stable Performance** - "Stable views and a strong engagement rate."
5. **Sub-Niche Focus** - "Two creators in your sub-niche."
6. **Creative Content Ideas** - a specific integration concept.
7. **Long-Term Partnership Value.**

Document results in a tracking sheet. Keep the core offer constant per campaign so A/B tests isolate one variable.

### Case Studies
- **Include a case study in the initial email** when you have one - immediate social proof.
- You do **not** need a case study to start campaigns.
- Request analytics **~2 weeks post-campaign**, framed as a casual performance check-in.

### Foot-in-the-Door
- **Start small.** As a beginner, pitch 3k-5k follower influencers to build trust and land first paid deals - brands hesitate to pay large flat fees to unknown agencies.
- **Propose a low-risk test campaign ($3k-$5k)** for a first deal, then scale.

## 8. Follow-Up Sequences

**Most people give up after one email. That is exactly why follow-ups work.**
**Best response rates come from emails 3-5 in the sequence.** [BP]

**Frame every follow-up against two possibilities:** they either (a) missed the email entirely, or (b) read it and chose not to reply. A follow-up that only nudges serves (a) and wastes (b). Adding value serves both. [RL]

### Universal Rules
- **Always reply in the original thread.** Never start a new thread; never fake an "RE:".
- **Never copy-paste follow-up scripts.** Spam filters catch patterns, and 800+ people are sending the same course template.
- **Personalize each follow-up differently** - ideally with a *new* recent video or post, not a rehash of the first one.
- **Each follow-up must add something new** - a new angle, a new creator, a new offer, new information, or real urgency.
- **Never repeat the same offer.** Re-sending an unchanged pitch signals you have no other options, which weakens your position as much as it bores them. [RL]
- **Concrete value-adds, in rough order of strength:** a relevant case study -> a different influencer profile -> a new content concept for the same creator -> a different sub-niche angle.
- **Vary your subject lines** across the sequence.
- **Follow-ups should be shorter than the initial email** (25-30 words, ~3 sentences).
- **Focus on the offer, not new personalization** for the later ones. [RL]
- **Trim content from the initial email** rather than writing from scratch.
- **Never send follow-ups back-to-back.** Space them out; rapid-fire sends trigger filters.
- **Build a library of ~30 unique follow-ups per outreach type** (10 strong / 10 soft / 10 closing), with 10+ variations of the soft reminder alone.
- **Never send an aggressive follow-up.** Polite and professional throughout.
- **Always end with a closing follow-up.** It shows respect and often produces a reply.
- **Bold text is permitted in follow-ups** (for the offer only), unlike initial emails.

### Counts & Spacing

The two sources differ. Reconciled recommendation:

| | [RL] Roast Library | [BP] Blueprint | **Use** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Influencer follow-ups | 4-5 | 3-5 | **4-5** |
| Brand follow-ups | 2-3 | 3-4 | **3** |
| Spacing | Day 1 / 2 / 4 / 6, then weekly | 3-5, then 4-7, then 5-7 days | **Start at [BP]'s 3+ days; only compress toward [RL]'s cadence if deliverability is provably healthy** |

See [Conflict B](#17-where-the-sources-disagree). The tighter [RL] cadence carries real spam risk on a new domain; the 50/day inbox cap applies either way, and follow-ups count toward it.

### The Sequence

**Email 1 (Initial):** Personalization + Offer + Strong CTA.

**Email 2 (Follow-up):** NEW personalization (a different recent video or post) + brief offer + CTA.
```
Hi [Name],

Saw your latest video on [new topic]. Great breakdown on [specific detail].

We're currently working with [Brand] on a campaign for creators like you.
Should I send more details?

Best,
[Name]
```

**Email 3 (Follow-up):** Add real urgency + softer CTA. Or swap the offer entirely - a different creator, a different sub-niche, a new angle.
```
Hi [Name],

Following up on the opportunity I mentioned. We're finalizing the list by
end of week. If this could be a fit, let me know.

Best,
[Name]
```

**Email 4 (Final / closing):** Ask for a decision or a date.
```
Hi [Name],

Understand if timing's off. If things change, I'm one reply away.

Best of luck with everything.

Best,
[Name]
```

### The Closing Email

> **Contested.** [BP] closes soft and no-pressure (above). [RL] argues that a soft close is passive and signals you're giving up, and prefers an assertive request for a date: **"Should I circle back in Q3/Q4?"** / "Is the timing wrong, or should I circle back in a few months?"
>
> **Recommended:** use [RL]'s framing - ask for a *date*, not permission to leave. It keeps the door open and produces an actionable reply. Keep [BP]'s warmth of tone, but not its concession. Specifically avoid: "Let me know if you change your mind" and "If this isn't something you're exploring right now, no worries at all." See [Conflict C](#17-where-the-sources-disagree).

**Frame the decision as your fault, not theirs.**

### Alternative Sequences
- **3-step (brands / general):** soft reminder -> value-add or adjusted offer -> closing breakup.
- **4-step (influencers):** reminder -> curiosity ("I know you're busy, but there might be something here") -> new offer ("Perhaps these brands aren't a fit. What about X or Y?") -> closing.
- **With a pressure step:** soft nudge -> stronger push (real deadline) -> new offer/angle -> closing.
- **Beyond the formal sequence:** break the pattern conversationally ("Is now a better time?") or with a meme.

### When You Only Have One Influencer
The standard advice - "swap in a different creator" - assumes a roster. If you genuinely only have one:

- **Don't swap influencers.** Rotating names you don't really have is a lie waiting to be called, and rotating your only creator out and back in reads as disorganized. [RL]
- **Pivot the concept instead:** pitch the same creator with a **different content concept**, or reposition them against a **different sub-niche** of the brand's audience.
- This keeps each follow-up genuinely new without inventing a roster. See [Conflict I](#17-where-the-sources-disagree).

### Follow-Up Anti-Patterns
- `Hi [Name], Just checking in to see if you had a chance to look over my email. Best, [Name]` - this exact template is in every course, everyone uses it, filters flag it.
- "Just wanted to bump" / "Please give me your thoughts."
- **"Circling back"** - generic filler, adds nothing.
- **"Did you read my email?"** - rude, accusatory, and instantly ends the conversation.
- Pitching a strong content concept for the first time in a follow-up - **too late; put your best ideas in the initial email.**
- Promising "more details" and then sending a generic follow-up - breaks trust and causes ghosting.
- Sending a follow-up as a fresh email.
- Getting emotional after a rejection.

## 9. Handling Replies & Objections

| Scenario | Response |
|---|---|
| **Influencer: "Yes, I'm interested"** | Immediately ask for **analytics and rates** for a specific integration length (60-90 seconds). Gives a concrete next step and qualifies them. |
| **Influencer: "What brands do you have?" / "What do you have in mind?"** | Name **2-3 specific brands** and explain the fit, then ask for their analytics and rates so you can tailor the best matches. |
| **Influencer: "Which specific brand?"** (pressing for one name) | Don't get cornered into naming a deal you don't have. Frame it as **needing their analytics first** to identify the best-fitting brand. This answers them honestly and keeps you in control of the sequence. |
| **Influencer wants 50% upfront** | Not standard - don't accept it as though it were. State the business standard: **Net 30 days after the integration goes live.** |
| **Influencer: "Show me a brand first"** | Answer the question (2-3 brands + why they fit), then ask for analytics. |
| **Influencer: "I'm full" / "only open to specific brands"** | Acknowledge their position, offer one specific brand idea as a **carrot**, then request their media kit. Shifts the power dynamic back. |
| **Influencer insists on a brand you don't have (e.g. Revolut)** | Acknowledge it, then proceed with pitching your actual brand partners. |
| **Influencer asks for a brief first** | Ask permission to **mention their name in pitches to brands**. That's a foot in the door without a full brief. |
| **Influencer gives rates then ghosts on the analytics request** | Follow up persistently. **Media kits are usually fluff** - always request specific metrics: 7-day analytics, engagement rate. |
| **Influencer mentions an agency** | Ask the influencer to nudge the agency about your email. If they replied on LinkedIn, continue the conversation there. |
| **Managed influencers / agencies** | They prioritize brands and budget. If you don't have a brand yet, say you'll follow up when you do. |
| **Brand: two contacts both reply** | Ask who the decision-maker is. |
| **Brand asks for a brief** | Request the brief **after the contract is signed** - briefs contain confidential campaign details brands won't share pre-contract. |

**Closing an influencer** = they have provided **media kit + rates + analytics**. Nothing less counts.

**Do not promise a timeline** for deals. Say you'll "keep them posted" so managers don't chase you.

**LinkedIn DMs:** shorter than emails, 2-3 sentences. First message asks **if they're the right person for partnerships** - not a full pitch.

**Instagram DMs:** use accounts older than 1 year, "Partnership Request" hook, 2-3 sentences, ask for their email.

## 10. Negotiation & Pricing

- **Apply the Frog in the Pot Principle** (see [Section 2](#2-power-dynamics)). Never drop your price in email 1. Introduce -> send analytics -> negotiate.
- **Always negotiate**, even when the quoted price seems fair. Test the waters every time.
- **Use CPM as your benchmark** to guarantee a margin.
- **Build negotiation room:** if a creator quotes $3.5k, work from economics that leave margin (e.g. ~$2.3k) rather than passing the number through.
- **Standardize your CPM across creators** (e.g. $40). Inconsistent CPMs ($25 vs $50) signal no strategy and undermine credibility.
- **Justify any price difference with data** - "this creator is higher because 96% US audience."
- **Use odd, non-round pricing:** $3,350 or $4,650 instead of $3,500 or $5,000. Reads as calculated rather than arbitrary.
- **Audience quality benchmark:** 46-52% US audience is "good enough", not outstanding - negotiate down from high CPM asks on that basis.
- **Walk away from unrealistic pricing:** a creator demanding $600 against a $200 market offer with no existing brand deals doesn't understand market value. Move on.
- **First deals: propose $3k-$5k test campaigns** to build trust.

### Counter-Offer Guardrails
- **Don't lowball on a hypothetical.** Without a specific brand deal in hand you have no leverage to justify a deep cut, and an aggressive counter reads as unserious.
- **Rough ceiling: a first counter should not cut much beyond ~30-40%.** A **5K -> 2K** counter is too aggressive; **5K -> 3K** is a reasonable opening that tests their flexibility without insulting them. [RL]
- Treat the first counter as a **probe**, not your final position - the [Frog in the Pot](#2-power-dynamics) sequence gives you the room to move again later.

### Payment Terms
- **Standard is Net 30 after the integration goes live.** State it plainly when asked.
- **50% upfront is not standard.** Decline it as a norm rather than negotiating from it.
- On Net 30+ contracts, negotiate out any interest clause (see the car vertical note below).

### Vertical-Specific Notes
- **Car vertical:** target European influencers only; the influencer must sign the contract directly; negotiate out the 10% interest clause on Net 30+ payments; aim for **20-30% commission** (30% is achievable).
- **Avoid ExpressVPN / PIA** - closely tied to the IDF.

## 11. Testing, Metrics & Diagnostics

### Benchmarks

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Reply rate (healthy) | **5-15%** [RL] / **10-20%** [BP] - treat 5-10% as normal, 10%+ as good. See [Conflict D](#17-where-the-sources-disagree) |
| Reply rate (warning) | **<5%** - strong indicator of spam placement |
| Reply rate (critical) | **<1-3% after 100+ emails** - stop; you're in spam, it's not the copy |
| Emails to close 3-5 influencers | **200-300** |
| Daily send target | **40-50/day per inbox** (all types combined) |
| Weekly send target | **200-250/week per inbox** - the volume floor for meaningful results |

**Positive reply rate is the metric that matters.** A raw reply rate counts "not interested" and "remove me" alongside real interest. Track positive replies separately - that is the number that tells you whether the *offer* works, as opposed to whether the subject line got opened. [RL]

**The volume ladder:** [BP]
- 50 emails sent -> some responses.
- 100 emails -> more responses, and patterns start to emerge.
- 500+ emails -> you have a system.

**A ~3% reply rate (8 replies / 250 emails) is a deliverability problem, not a copy problem.** Diagnose in Three Pillars order before rewriting anything.

### The Weekly Tracking Spreadsheet
Track these four columns per week, per campaign. Without them you are relying on subjective
impressions, which are not evidence. [RL]

| Emails sent | Replies | Positive replies | Deals closed |
|---|---|---|---|

**Use it to find the bottleneck, not just the total.** The point of the sheet is to locate *where*
conversations stall, then aim your next change at that stage:

| Where it stalls | What to fix |
|---|---|
| Few replies of any kind | Deliverability, then subject line |
| Replies, but few positive | The offer |
| Positive replies, then silence | Your follow-through - the reply you send next |
| Stalls consistently after pricing | Pricing, positioning, or negotiation approach |
| Positive replies but no closes | Qualification, terms, or the creator/brand fit itself |

### What to Test
- Subject lines (2-3 variations).
- Personalization angles (try new detail types).
- Offers (different brands, different offer types).
- Follow-up timing (vary by 1-2 days).

### Testing Rules
- **Test one variable at a time.** Keep the core offer constant per campaign.
- **One change per week**, measured against the sheet. Changing several things at once makes the result unattributable, and changing something daily never accumulates enough volume to read. [RL]
- **Minimum 50-100 emails per variation** (100+ for reliable results).
- **Use Spintax** for variations - especially CTAs (3+ variations) and offer wording - so you get uniqueness without losing test validity.
- **Pre-build offers as a weekend task:** 5+ unique offers, each featuring 2-3 of your best-fit influencers, differentiated by specific selling points (stable views, high engagement, unique content style). Then generate 5+ Spintax variations each = 25+ unique offers.
- **Document everything in a tracking sheet. Double down on winners.**
- **Verify inbox placement** with Instantly's "Inbox Placement" test before and during campaigns.
- **Don't submit near-identical variants** to a test - that's not a test.

### Diagnostic Table
| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Low initial reply rate, follow-ups also flat | Deliverability / spam placement |
| Low open-equivalent engagement | Bad subject line |
| Good reply rate on follow-ups, poor on initial | Bad initial offer |
| Replies but no closes | Offer isn't specific enough / wrong contact |
| Sudden spam landing | Too many identical emails too quickly -> pause, warm up domain for a week |

## 12. AI Usage Rules

- **Never let AI write the final email.** AI copy is generic, voiceless, and immediately identifiable by both brand reps and spam filters.
- **AI tells to strip out:**
  - **Em-dashes** - the single clearest signal. Use a hyphen or a period.
  - "strategically-aligned", "globally-minded audiences", "highly engaged"
  - "YouTube integration sparked an idea"
  - "demonstrate how long-term collaborations can showcase product utility"
  - "content that naturally incorporates brand messaging"
  - Anything that reads like an ad script.
- **What AI is good for:**
  - Brainstorming angles and ideas.
  - Generating **Spintax variations** of an offer you already wrote.
  - Testing subject lines - prompt it as the recipient: *"If you were NordVPN, would you open this email?"*
  - Grammar and correction passes (alongside Grammarly).
- **If you use AI for personalization:**
  - Feed it the **video transcript**, not just the link.
  - **Train it on 150+ of your own best manually written examples** so it replicates your voice.
  - Generate 5 options, then select or combine the best elements.
  - Give it highly detailed prompts - vague prompts produce ad-script output.
- **Master manual personalization first.** You can't guide AI well until you can do it yourself.
- **Read the finished email aloud. Does it sound human?** If not, rewrite it. [BP]

## 13. Workflow & Speed

- **Timebox everything:**
  - Research per creator: **2 minutes** (one 2-minute video segment). [BP] suggests scanning the last 5 videos - do that only for high-value targets. See [Conflict E](#17-where-the-sources-disagree).
  - Writing per email: **7-10 minute timer.**
  - Never 20-50 minutes per email; never 30+ minutes.
- **Progressive overload:** speed is a trained skill. Drive time-per-email from hours down to minutes while holding quality.
- **Campaign target: complete a 50-lead campaign** (research, personalization, launch) **in one day.** Automate or outsource lead generation.
- **Weekend work:** no sending. Use weekends for research, drafting, and pre-building offers / follow-up libraries.
- **Combat perfectionism with a timer.** Overthinking is the main blocker to scale.
- **Cold outreach is a marathon, not a sprint.** Don't expect anything close to 100% conversion.
- References: the "How to message influencers" training module; the book **"$100M Offers"** - re-read it and apply its principles to the agency model to build genuinely differentiated value.

## 14. Banned Words & Phrases

Never send an email containing any of these.

### Openers
| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| "I hope you're doing well" | Everyone uses it. Kills deliverability. Cold email has no time for small talk. |
| "Dear Sir/Madam" | Reads like a posted letter from 1985. |
| "Hello!" with no name | No personalization at all. |
| "I've been watching your content for a while" | Creepy. |
| "I saw" / "I watched" / "I noticed" | Overused opening - **everyone** starts this way. [BP] bans them outright; [RL] recommends "I saw" as an improvement over fanboy language. Prefer leading with the observation itself: not *"I saw your video on AI agents"* but *"The progression from Navimo to CI/CD actually clicked."* See [Conflict F](#17-where-the-sources-disagree). |

### Emotional / fan language
"I love your content" - "Your videos are amazing" - "amazing" - "impressive" - "fantastic" - "I really loved" - "super excited" - "would love to" - "eager" - "I'm a huge fan" - "one of my favorite brands" - "I'm proud of you"

### Filler
"super" - "really" - "literally" - "just" (as in "just checking in") - "very"

### Weak / permission-seeking
"Would you be open to..." - "Want me to send details?" (use "**Should** I send details?") - "maybe" - "perhaps" - "I'm sorry to bother you" - "I hope this isn't too much" - "by any chance" - "no worries if not" - "if you'd like"

### Follow-up filler and rudeness
"Circling back" - "Just checking in" - "Just wanted to bump" - "Did you read my email?" (accusatory; ends the conversation)

### Vague agency-speak
"We specialize in connecting..." - "amazing creators" - "strategically-aligned" - "globally-minded audiences" - "We'd like to discuss a collaboration" - "We believe there's a great opportunity here" - "content that naturally incorporates brand messaging"

### Overclaiming
"exclusive" - "we represent [creator]" (unless your email is in their official contact info) - "guarantee" - "better brands" - "Only a few spots remain" - "perfect sponsor"

### Spam-filter words (brand outreach)
"paid" - "money" - "guarantee"

> Everything above is banned for being **weak or unprofessional copy**, which is the real reason not to send it. Only this last group is about filters, and even there the overall email matters far more than any single word - ordinary phrasing like "we work with" is fine. See [Conflict J](#17-where-the-sources-disagree).

### Sign-offs
"Faithfully yours" - "Yours truly" - "Thanks and regards" (thanking them for what?) - "Thanks in advance"

**Use instead:** "Best," or "Best regards,"

## 15. Hard "Never Do" List

**Positioning**
- Never fanboy, compliment excessively, or mix fan energy with a business offer.
- Never sound desperate, needy, apologetic, or pleading.
- Never apologize for emailing them.
- Never ask permission - offer a choice.
- Never correct or criticize the recipient's content.
- Never introduce yourself in the first email.
- Never start with what YOU want; start with what THEY get.
- Never inflate an influencer's ego - it raises their price.
- Never get emotional after a rejection.

**Honesty**
- Never claim brand relationships you don't have.
- Never say "exclusive" or "we represent X" without a signed contract or official listing.
- Never use a fake job title.
- Never use false urgency or invented scarcity (real campaign deadlines are fine).
- Never promise "better brands" or outcomes you can't guarantee.
- Never fake an "RE:" on a new thread.

**Mechanics**
- Never put links, images, or media kits in a first email - and never a multi-line signature block.
- Never use open-rate tracking pixels.
- Never use HTML-heavy or pasted rich-text formatting.
- Never use em-dashes or emojis.
- Never bold text in an initial email.
- Never use multiple blank lines.
- Never exceed 3 sections.
- Never send identical emails at volume.
- Never copy-paste follow-ups or use a course template word-for-word.
- Never send follow-ups back-to-back.
- Never send on weekends.
- Never exceed ~50 sends/day per inbox.
- Never use more than one CTA.
- Never let AI write the whole email.
- Never ask for a call, or a media kit, in email #1.

## 16. Worked Examples

### Example 1: Automotive Influencer

**BAD**
```
Hi First Name,

I hope you're doing well! I really loved how you use the iPhone 11 to
improve your sleep. I've been watching your content for a while now and
think you're amazing.

I specialize in connecting creators with brands and would love to work
with you. We care about building authentic partnerships.

Would you be interested in exploring sponsorship opportunities?

Thanks and regards,
[Name]
```
Wrong: "I hope you're doing well" (overused) - "I really loved" (fan energy) - "I've been watching for a while" (creepy) - "amazing" (generic spam word) - "specialize in connecting" (everyone says this) - "Would you be interested" (weak CTA) - "Thanks and regards" (thanking them for what?) - merge-tag failure on "First Name".

**GOOD**
```
Hi Name,

I watched your latest BMW restoration video. The way you diagnosed the fuel
pump issue - just swapping the solenoid - was a solid catch.

We work with automotive creators on campaigns for tech brands. You fit the
profile. Should I send the brief?

Best,
Name
```
Right: specific reference to recent content - a specific technical detail (solenoid swap) - no emotional language - clear offer (tech brands + automotive creators) - strong yes/no CTA - professional but direct.

### Example 2: Tech Creator Pitched to a Brand

**BAD**
```
Hello!

I am reaching out on behalf of tech creators we work with. We have amazing
guys with large followings who are super eager to share everything about
your brand.

We specialize in connecting highly engaged, globally-minded audiences with
strategically-aligned brands.

Would you be open to exploring this further?

Best regards,
[Name]
[Agency Name]
[Website]
[Phone]
```
Wrong: no personalization at all - "amazing guys" (fluff) - "super eager" (desperation) - "strategically-aligned" (AI-speak) - multiple links in the signature (lands in spam) - vague offer - weak CTA - em-dashes.

**GOOD**
```
Hi Name,

I noticed you're launching the new product line this month. Timing's good.

We work with tech creators who average 150K+ views on product reviews.
Strong engagement, mostly US/UK audience. Should I send profiles?

Best,
Name
```
Right: specific reference to their launch - clear metrics (views, engagement, geography) - specific offer (product-review creators) - short and direct - strong CTA - no fluff, no links.

### Example 3: A Full Sequence

**Email 1 (Initial)**
```
Hi Sarah,

I saw your recent content on sustainable fashion. The angle on fast-fashion
waste was eye-opening.

We're currently onboarding creators for an ethical clothing brand launching
Q4. Your audience fits perfectly. Should I send the brief?

Best,
James
```

**Email 2 (3 days later)**
```
Hi Sarah,

Following up on the opportunity I mentioned. The brand is looking to

Are you still interested?

James
```

**Email 3 (5 days later)**
```
Hi Sarah,

Last one from me. If you want details, I'm here. If timing's off, no worries.
```

What works: email 1 is personalized and specific; email 2 adds *real* urgency (finalizing this week); email 3 is respectful with no pressure; each is brief; subject lines vary; no templates.

**What to change per [RL]:** email 3's "if timing's off, no worries" concedes. Prefer *"Should I circle back in Q1?"* - it asks for a date instead of closing the door.

## 17. Where the Sources Disagree

Ten genuine conflicts. Where a recommendation is given, it favours the source with more specific supporting reasoning, and errs toward the more conservative option on anything affecting deliverability. Conflicts **I** and **J** are internal to [RL] - the Sep 2 session revises earlier sessions.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | "Let me know your thoughts" / "What do you think?" | **Weak** - repeatedly flagged; vague, easy to ignore | **Strong** - "demands an opinion" | **Follow [RL].** Use an explicit yes/no question. These two are fallbacks at best, and only in follow-ups. |
| **B** | Follow-up count and spacing | Influencers 4-5, brands 2-3; day 1/2/4/6 then weekly | Influencers 3-5, brands 3-4; 3-5 / 4-7 / 5-7 days apart | **Counts: 4-5 influencer, 3 brand. | **D** | Expected reply rate | 5-15% (5-10% normal) | 10-20% | **Plan against 5-10%.** [RL]'s figure comes from live campaign diagnostics; treat 10%+ as a good outcome, not a baseline. |

## 18. Pre-Send Checklist

Run this before every send. Any "no" = don't send.

**Personalization**
- [ ] References a specific recent video / post / achievement?
- [ ] Mentions one specific detail, not generic praise?
- [ ] Passes the 10-Person Test (could NOT be sent to 10 other people)?
- [ ] Shows you actually engaged with their work?
- [ ] Bridges logically into the offer?
- [ ] About THEM, not about you?
- [ ] **If emailing a brand:** references a business achievement, NOT the influencer's video?

**Offer**
- [ ] Names 1-2 specific brands or creators (and are those claims true)?
- [ ] Includes at least one concrete metric (views, engagement, audience geography)?
- [ ] Explains WHY they specifically fit?
- [ ] Has a real timeline or urgency (not invented)?
- [ ] Sells an outcome, not a raw stat?
- [ ] Starts with what THEY get, not what you want?

**CTA**
- [ ] Exactly one CTA?
- [ ] A yes/no question?
- [ ] Demands a response rather than asking permission?
- [ ] Short and direct?
- [ ] Related directly to the offer just made?

**Subject line**
- [ ] Creates curiosity or is personalized?
- [ ] Passes the Google Test (not a course template, not 100 identical emails)?
- [ ] Keywords not repeated in the first sentence of the body?
- [ ] Right type for the audience (specific for influencers, generic for brands)?

**Formatting**
- [ ] Plain text - no links, images, tracking pixels, signature block?
- [ ] No em-dashes, no emojis, no bold?
- [ ] No multiple blank lines?
- [ ] Three sections max?
- [ ] Under the word limit (50-70 general / 30-50 B2B / 25-30 follow-up)?
- [ ] Short professional sign-off?
- [ ] Grammar checked?

**Tone**
- [ ] Professional, not desperate?
- [ ] Specific, not generic?
- [ ] Direct, not vague?
- [ ] Confident, not pleading?
- [ ] Zero banned words (see [Section 14](#14-banned-words--phrases))?
- [ ] No false claims, no fake urgency, no over-promises?
- [ ] Read aloud - does it sound human?
- [ ] Would you be excited to receive this email?

**Sequence & mechanics**
- [ ] Different from my last 10 sends (offer structure, opener, CTA varied / Spintax applied)?
- [ ] Correct decision-maker, not a gatekeeper?
- [ ] Weekday, and under 50 total sends on this inbox today?
- [ ] Warm-up still running on this inbox?
- [ ] If a follow-up: same thread, new personalization, new value, varied subject line, 3+ days since the last one, shorter than the original?
- [ ] If a follow-up: is the offer actually *different*, not the same pitch resent?
- [ ] Is there a closing follow-up planned at the end of the sequence?

**Tracking**
- [ ] Logged in this week's sheet (sent / replies / positive replies / closed)?
- [ ] Am I changing only one thing this week, so the result is attributable?

**Time**
- [ ] Under 10 minutes spent on this email?
