# Brands — Presenting creators

### BRD-PRES-01 — Match the creator to the brief before answering

Rule (must): Before proposing a creator, check the brief: audience country and age, niche fit, real engagement, budget fit and brand safety. Four of five is workable; missing the brand's main criterion is not.
Steps: Audience demographics vs the brand's countries and ages → Niche fit with what the brand sells → Engagement quality → Price within their budget → No content that conflicts with the brand
Don't: Pitch a $10K creator against a $2K budget
Source: 2.How to Qualify Influencers - Coach Ammar — "If they give you five criteria and you hit four, you can work with that. If you're missing the main one, find a better fit first."
Source: 2.How to Qualify Influencers - Coach Ammar — "Budget fit — if their budget is $2K, don't pitch a $10K creator."

### BRD-PRES-02 — Only pitch creators you believe in

Rule (must): Weak content or a weak audience burns the brand relationship; only present creators who genuinely make sense and have potential.
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "Only pitch people you genuinely believe make sense and have potential."
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "If you’re not confident in them, how can you expect a brand to sponsor them?"

### BRD-PRES-03 — Specifics, never placeholders

Rule (must): Wherever a message would say 'brands in mind', 'audience' or 'content', put the specifics: named brands, the audience split by country and age with percentages, and a concrete placement idea tied to a real upcoming video (e.g. a case brand right after an unboxing).
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "Every time you see mentions of "brands in mind," "audience," or "content," don’t just leave them generic. Replace them with specifics about your influencer."
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "Make everything you write tailored to the brand's world—that’s how you build trust and keep them engaged."

### BRD-PRES-04 — What a creator presentation contains

Rule (must): Each creator comes with: name and platforms with links, audience size and demographics (age, location, interests), examples of past content and partnerships, how you would do even better, and results where available (views, engagement, ROI). Average views go as one exact number, never a range or 'upwards of'.
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "Influencer Details: - Name(s) and platform(s). WITH LINKS ! (they will ask for them anyway) - Audience size and demographics (age, location, interests). - Examples of the influencer's previous content and brand partnerships. (if you have any) - How these examples were awesome, but you can do even better with them. - Results they've driven (if available-views, engagement, ROI)."
Source: coaching call "September 12th- Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-09-12) — "What is your average, bro? Don't give me range. So, don't give range, give exact number."

### BRD-PRES-05 — Foot in the door: one creator, not a list

Rule (must): Present the single best-fit creator. A list causes decision fatigue and makes the agency look like it fills quotas; one well-argued creator is easier to say yes to and builds trust.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "The Foot-in-the-Door method is a strategy where you focus on presenting one influencer rather than a whole list to the brand."
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Too many options mean too many decisions, and decision fatigue can cause them to pass on all options."

### BRD-PRES-06 — Prove the fit with metrics and sentiment

Rule (must): Show how the creator's audience matches the brand's target (age, gender, interests, location), their view consistency, engagement versus the usual 2-4%, and audience sentiment (questions, trust, product discussion in comments). Any ROI illustration must state its assumptions and never read as a promise.
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Demonstrate how the influencer’s audience, views, and engagement will directly impact the brand's ROI (Return on Investment) and brand awareness."
Source: 3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands — "Show how the influencer’s followers interact with their content in a meaningful, emotionally-driven way."

### BRD-PRES-07 — Asked for a list? Still send one or two

Rule (must): Do not send 5-10 creators: it looks like you do not know what they want, one disliked name can sink them all, and the total looks expensive. Send one strong match (two at most), mention there are other options, and adjust from their feedback.
Do: If the brand named a niche, send only creators in that niche
Don't: Hedge with 'and I might have others': present the ones chosen with confidence; more come when the brand asks or likes these
Source: 3.5. Pre-HandlingHandling objection Aikido Mastery — "Golden Rule: Always start small. Sending one influencer at a time is safer than overwhelming them with a massive list, especially if the prices are high. - Why? - Sending five influencers at once increases the chance that they'll dismiss all of them if even one doesn't align with their expectations. - By sending one influencer at a time, you can adjust your approach based on their feedback before pitching others."
Source: lesson video "3.5. Pre-HandlingHandling objection Aikido Mastery" @40:00 — "will it be open to working with multiple guys or just want to start with one to get things started?"

### BRD-PRES-08 — Stay inside the range they gave

Rule (must): Once a brand states a spending range, only propose creators inside it.
Source: 3.5. Pre-HandlingHandling objection Aikido Mastery — "If they give you a spending range, ensure the influencers you send align with it to avoid unnecessary rejections."
Source: coaching call "December 13th: Review/Roast my email session Ammar" (2025-12-13) — "Common sense, inshaAllah, common sense, don't sell me something that I'm not looking for."

### BRD-PRES-09 — Each shortlisted creator, in four lines

Rule (must): Assume the brand has never heard of the creator. For each: name linked to the channel; subscribers, average views and engagement rate; only the audience figures the brand cares about (e.g. US and UK share); rate per integration priced from the niche CPM at the high end.
Don't: Describe an audience with generation labels ('Gen Z', 'Boomers') instead of age ranges with percentages; Apply the niche's top CPM to a creator with very high average views: the CPM falls as views climb; Pitch a creator priced well above the niche's usual CPM: brands simply stop replying
Source: 4.1. Working With the Brand - Coach Ammar — "Assume the brand has never heard of your creator, even if they're huge in your niche."
Source: 4.1. Working With the Brand - Coach Ammar — "Rate per integration, priced off your niche CPM at the higher end so you keep margin."

### BRD-PRES-10 — Why this creator fits: one precise sentence

Rule (must): One sentence tied to the brand's actual product — the creator's content style, how they demo products, or a past integration that maps to this brand. 'He's in the travel niche' says nothing.
Source: 4.1. Working With the Brand - Coach Ammar — "One sentence, tied to the brand's actual product. "He's in the travel niche" tells them nothing."
Source: lesson video "3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do" @2:59 — "I see so many people saying, hey, I think we'd be an amazing fit. I think this would be a fit."

### BRD-PRES-11 — Curate; never call five names 'a large list'

Rule (tip): Offer to hand-pick and segment creators for their goals; with 5-10 creators, never talk about 'a large list' (that language is for 50+).
Source: 5.How to Get a Brand's Budget — "If you have 5-10 influencers, don’t mention a “large list.” Reserve that language for lists with 50+ influencers."

### BRD-PRES-12 — Turn a weakness into a strength — truthfully

Rule (tip): A creator with modest views but a very specific, engaged audience can be exactly what a brand needs; a creator with few sponsorships has an audience not tired of ads. Reframe only what is true.
Source: 1.5. Marketing crash course — "If an influencer hasn't done many sponsored deals, this could be a plus. It means their audience isn't tired of hearing ads, and your brand will feel more genuine."
Source: 1.5. Marketing crash course — "Brands want results, not random high views. So, even if you only get 10,000 views, if those views are laser-focused, it's valuable."

### BRD-PRES-13 — Check the creator has not worked with the brand before pitching

Rule (should): Before putting a creator in front of a brand, search the creator's channel for the brand's name (and ask the creator). Don't pitch a creator the brand sponsored in the last year or two, or dropped: the brand already has its own contact and the price it paid, and the pitch makes the agency look careless.
Source: lesson video "8.Abu Lahya Reviewing Emails" @28:44 — "That the influencer hasn't been sponsored previously by the brand. This could backfire."
Source: coaching call "10/7" (2025-07-10) — "just be like apologies unfortunately he's already in contract with a different one"

### BRD-PRES-14 — Narrow it down for them — and say so when nothing fits

Rule (should): Trust comes from removing options, not piling them on: tell the brand which creators would not serve its goal and why, and keep the one that does. When nothing on the roster fits yet, say it plainly and promise to come back when you have the match; brands remember the agency that did not push a misfit.
Source: lesson video "3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands" @8:39 — "you can gain someone's trust so much because every single person is so needy to get the sale and trying to just sell you everything."
Source: lesson video "3.3. Tips When Presenting Influencers to Brands" @9:03 — "I don't have anyone that fits. I'll get back to you in a month or two when I get more fits."

### BRD-PRES-15 — Analytics and rate in hand before a creator is pitched

Rule (must): Pitch only creators we have onboarded and whose audience analytics (top countries, age) and agreed rate we already hold. A brand that says yes to a creator we then cannot deliver is a brand lost, and the analytics are the first thing every brand asks for. The creator's own media kit may be forwarded once their rates are cut out and nothing in it names or brands another agency; a kit in another language is translated, with the key audience figures written into the email in English. Figures typed into the email or a sheet are fine, but send the real analytics screenshot when it is asked for or needed as proof, as an attachment, never pasted into the body as an image (it hurts deliverability).
Don't: Pitch a creator first and collect their analytics or rate afterwards
Source: coaching call "August 12: Q&A - Kushtrim" (2025-08-12) — "And, you know, because this will make, will make it look you really bad. Like, hey, let's collaborate together. But I don't have, you know, his audience analytics. Okay, so never go to a brand without having the audience analytics."
Source: coaching call "10/1 - RME (Brands) w/Ammar" (2026-01-10) — "I think it's kind of absurd because there's a guy, you'll be like, yes, I want, and then, hey, I'm going to reach out, and then they're not going to answer, and then you'll have to come up with a bunch of excuses and stuff. I mean, in theory, it makes sense because you're going to leverage that brand. Hey, there's a brand interested in working with you, but it's still risky. I would first close my influencer"

### BRD-PRES-16 — A first deal with a brand: lead with an affordable creator

Rule (should): A brand that has never worked with us would rather test a smaller, well-priced creator than pay five figures for a star: a big price asks for trust we have not earned yet. Lead a first deal with a creator who gives good views for the price (a high-engagement micro creator is often ideal), sized to what this brand already sponsors. A big creator can sit beside the main pick as a 'special mention' with their average views, or come next once the first video has delivered, but never as the foot-in-the-door pick. Build the roster with a range of sizes before brand outreach, so there is always an affordable option.
Do: Check which creators the brand has sponsored, and at what size, before choosing the lead creator; A brand weighing one big creator can be offered several smaller ones instead (each averaging, say, 25-50k views), with closer audiences and more trust for the same money
Source: coaching call "28/3" (2025-03-28) — "if you want to go for someone that's got 2k, someone that is charging 20, 30k, then obviously the brand has to build trust with you first."
Source: coaching call "7/7" (2025-07-07) — "Go for the cheap ones. As long as they've got a good audience, as long as the offer is good, then, you know, that's the main goal. After that, that's when the upsell comes, insha Allah."

### BRD-PRES-17 — Past sponsorships: what they delivered, not just the logos

Rule (should): A list of brand names proves little; brands care what the creator's past integrations did. Name a past sponsorship together with its result as seen on the public video (the views it reached, its engagement), and pick the ones that performed well or are close to this brand. Call them past collaborations. Don't lead with a recent sponsorship by the brand's direct competitor: brands assume an exclusivity period (often three to nine months) and move on; if they ask, answer truthfully. Repeat sponsors and long partnerships, even with brands from other categories, are strong evidence (a brand that came back fifteen times was getting results) and are worth mentioning unasked. All of this is the creator's record, never the agency's (BRD-OBJ-22).
Source: coaching call "December 2nd 2025: Brands Q&A" (2025-12-02) — "He did a collaboration with Adobe where he produced over 3 million views with an engagement rate of 16%, for example, right? So you don't need to like just say, oh, he worked with this, this, this, this. Just make sure that they have some substantial, something to hold on to, right? They don't care if they work with Asus, if they didn't generate views, right?"
Source: coaching call "February 25th: Deal Breakdown & Strategy" (2026-02-25) — "I'm not saying, here's a few of these collaborations with competitors. Yeah, you don't need to point that out, right?"

### BRD-PRES-18 — Sheets and the brand's own templates: few, clean columns

Rule (should): When a brand asks for more creators than fit in an email, or sends its own submission sheet, keep it clean: the creator's name linked to the channel, average views, the top audience country with its percentage, the one or two age brackets that matter, and three or four notable collaborations. Rates follow HC-22, and the same sheet can carry later details (rates, shipping addresses). Link it on one word ('here'). Fill a brand's own template ourselves, with three good creators (five at most) on a first submission, and reply in the same thread with everyone still copied. Never forward the brand's sheet or brief to a creator's management, which hands them our role. If a column is unclear, ask the brand what it means.
Source: coaching call "21/4 - Brands Q&A" (2026-04-21) — "I literally, I just put the name of the influencer, I hyperlinked it to the channel, I have the average views"
Source: coaching call "21/4 - Brands Q&A" (2026-04-21) — "I just write down, like, three, four collaborations that they had in the past."

### BRD-PRES-19 — Match the creator to what the campaign is for

Rule (should): Find out, or work out, what the campaign is for. For awareness, views count most: a creator with high views and modest engagement, or a small US share of a very large audience, can fit, stated honestly (FND-ETH-09). For sales, an engaged, tightly niche audience converts better. A brand asking for 'campaign ideas' wants this reasoning: which creators bring reach, which bring conversions, and why. For subscription products the measure is sign-ups and clicks, so that is what a results report leads with, never inflated.
Source: coaching call "18/4" (2025-04-18) — "So that's how brand awareness works. Obviously, if you can get both on the same plate, if you can get brand awareness and views, that's better than just getting views."
Source: coaching call "1/4" (2025-04-01) — "This guy will be good with brand awareness, for example, you know, because he gets too many views and stuff like that."

### BRD-PRES-20 — A pitched creator falls through: say it plainly, bring a replacement

Rule (must): When a creator the brand liked cannot go ahead (declines, goes silent, gets booked), tell the brand promptly and without drama, for example: 'it won't work out with X for this campaign after all'. Say only what we know. Never invent a reason ('he's going through something', 'other commitments'), never say he is not interested when he simply has not replied, and don't complain that he is ignoring us, which makes the agency look weak. Before writing, give a silent creator one last, dated chance, and line up the replacement: similar price and views, available in their timeline. A much cheaper one makes the first quote look inflated; a much dearer one won't be taken. Whether the replacement is named in the same email or only after asking whether they would rather wait is house choice HC-23. If they turn the replacement down, keep the door open warmly until we have a better fit.
Don't: Invent a reason for the creator's absence; Offer a replacement priced far above or far below the creator the brand accepted
Source: coaching call "30/4 Negotiations" (2026-04-30) — "And when I would go to a brand, like, hey, actually, we couldn't make it work as initially thought. And this is the replacement, so I wouldn't give them anything. Sort of explanation, because it's going to make me look bad, you know, like, if I start like, hey, this guy is ghosting us, what they're going to think is like, oh, wow, wait, they ghost you?"
Source: coaching call "October 17th: Advanced Brands (Osama)" (2025-10-17) — "just don't say, oh, the guy's not responding, otherwise it's going to make you look weak and you're going to lose the power in the conversation."

### BRD-PRES-21 — Coach Ammar: ask whether they will wait before naming a replacement

Rule (tip): When a pitched creator falls through, Coach Ammar first tells the brand the creator can't do it and asks whether they would rather wait for him or go ahead with someone else, naming nobody yet: naming replacements in the same breath can look like a made-up story to sell more creators. If they want to move on, he sends a couple of names; if they want to wait, he sets a limit (another week) before proposing others. The other coaches bring the replacement in the same email (BRD-PRES-20); which applies is house choice HC-23.

So he wants, you know, upsell more guys. What I usually do, I just let them know for some reason, he's not able. Do you want to wait for him, or we can proceed with another guy, and don't mention any names."
Source: coaching call "February 15th: Email & Website support" (2026-02-15) — "you give them limit, like, okay, let's wait for another week, if not, let's proceed with another creators."

### BRD-PRES-22 — A creator who just promoted a competitor: check, then tell the brand

Rule (must): Before pitching, and again before confirming, check the creator's recent videos for the brand's competitors. A competitor sponsorship in the last few weeks may come with an exclusivity clause and in any case blunts the new message. Tell the brand honestly that he has just promoted a competitor and offer another creator now, or the same one once enough time has passed. If two competing brands want the same creator, place him with one and offer the other a different creator, saying truthfully that he is already booked in their category.
Source: coaching call "19/6" (2025-06-19) — "it might be an exclusive term, and also, if it did, even if it's not exclusive, the fact that, he's been, he's promoted someone, just two weeks ago, then, it might damage it."
Source: coaching call "30/6" (2025-06-30) — "So what should do instead is be honest. This is what I like to tell everyone. Yes, we like to do Aikido, but here the Aikido is, be honest, a lot of them appreciate it. Say how it is, he's already done this, and honestly, you know, after thinking about it. Whatever, I don't think this is going to be a good fit after all, so for your sake, I'm thinking we can rather do this influencer instead"

### BRD-PRES-23 — Two sponsors in one video: the brand hears it first

Rule (must): If the creator plans another, non-competing sponsor in the same video, tell the brand before they agree: sharing a video is unusual and many brands object. Also check that nothing in the brand's terms bars the creator from promoting others around their dates.
Source: coaching call "December 25th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-25) — "it is something you need to let the brand know that, hey, by the way, you will be in a video where another one is sponsored because it's not, it's not super normal."
Source: coaching call "December 12th 2025: Brand (Advanced), Osama" (2025-12-12) — "Unless the video is like crazy long, but even then I would still tell the brand just to cover cases."

### BRD-PRES-24 — Name the creators and share their channels openly

Rule (should): Give brands the creators' names and channel links: they need to see whom they are paying for, and hiding names looks shady, which itself invites going around us. Established brands rarely bypass an agency that keeps bringing good creators with rates and analytics ready, and a brand that does is one to drop. Our protection is being organised and useful, and describing our tie to each creator accurately (BRD-FIRST-16), never secrecy or hints of a control we don't have.
Don't: Hint at exclusive control we don't have to scare a brand off going direct
Source: coaching call "February 28th: Roast my email Brands- Ammar" (2026-02-28) — "you being scared of, like, revealing his name, it makes you look shady, so even if they weren't going to reach out behind your back, that might make them do it."
Source: coaching call "18/2" (2025-02-18) — "It's totally okay to actually give the influence and give them the YouTube channels because You know, those are the people that are going to pay money for right"

### BRD-PRES-25 — The integration idea comes from the creator, short and realistic

Rule (should): When a brand asks how its product would appear, ask the creator how they would build it into their video (the more detail the better) and pass their idea on, briefly and realistically: what the creator does and why the product fits it naturally (chefs known for steaks would use a meat thermometer on camera). A recurring series or signature format that performs well is worth offering as the place for the integration, stated as what the series has done, never as a promise for this video. A creator who wants to test the product first is showing that he guards his audience's trust; pass it on that way. Unasked, leave integration notes out of the first presentation unless the fit is exceptional: they read as needy and salesy.
Source: coaching call "November 6th 2025: Negotiation 101" (2025-11-06) — "you can ask the influencer how would you kind of incorporate this brand into your video and then he gives you like okay the more details the better"
Source: coaching call "21/4 - Brands Q&A" (2026-04-21) — "Just don't waffle too much. Don't put too much fluff. Okay. Right? Just keep it realistic."

### BRD-PRES-26 — A first call with a brand: discovery, well prepared

Rule (should): Treat the first call as discovery. The brand usually leads at first, wanting to know who we are; then we ask the questions: their goals for the coming quarter, the size and kind of creators they want, audience countries, content ideas or a script, volume. Come prepared: a simple roster sheet to show if asked, the results of each creator's past sponsorships, a reason each one fits, and ballpark rates at hand, though price normally waits for the next call. Never guess a rate or a detail on the call: say we will check with the creator and come back. Close by booking the next call (FND-CALL-10), for example: 'let me go through our roster against this and come back Monday'. Camera on, dressed neatly but casually (no suit). One person on our side is normal, and if asked how big the agency is, answer honestly. Introduce any note-taker at the start (FND-CALL-12).
Source: coaching call "December 12th 2025: Brand (Advanced), Osama" (2025-12-12) — "So the brand, most of the time, the brand would lead the conversation because they want to get to know you and who you are, insha Allah."
Source: coaching call "November 14th 2025 - Advanced Brands (Osama)" (2025-11-14) — "It's very rare when the brand will actually drop the prices, but I would have that in case, I would have that at hand, just in case if they want to talk about prices."

### BRD-PRES-27 — A creator booked for months is an extra, not the lead

Rule (tip): Brands budget by the month or the quarter, so a creator booked far ahead (or, late in the year, only free next quarter) makes a weak main pitch. Keep them as an extra option and explain the dates when a brand wants that kind of creator.
Source: coaching call "September 25: Q&A - Younes" (2025-09-25) — "most brands, they tend to have a certain budget in place per three months or maybe per month."
Source: coaching call "September 25: Q&A - Younes" (2025-09-25) — "If a brand wants someone like him, then he can present him and explain the situation."

### BRD-PRES-28 — Never talk anyone down to win a pitch

Rule (should): Pitch our creators on their own merits. Don't warn a brand about other creators or agencies (fake views, scams); it is not our business and makes us look petty. And never tell a young brand that it is small or needs our creator to compete with the big names: it bruises the reader's pride and loses the reply.
Source: coaching call "December 27th: Review/Roast my email session-Ammar" (2025-12-27) — "I wouldn't do that, if I'm being honest. Why would I, you know, be that guy, like, hey, don't reach, don't work with them"
Source: coaching call "October 23rd: Influencer Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-23) — "What do you mean? Are we not the big dogs? You know, there might be some ego involved."

### BRD-PRES-29 — Coach Michael: among the creators that fit, lead with the best margin

Rule (tip): Among creators who all fit the brand, Coach Michael pitches first the one that leaves the agency the largest margin, and keeps creators on other platforms ready in case the brand only runs one format. For a brand's first deal this sits inside BRD-PRES-16: the lead creator must still be affordable for this brand.
Source: coaching call "October 2nd: Brand Detective - Michael" (2025-10-02) — "whoever I have the largest profit margin with, I'm gonna pitch him. And then inshallah, like once I get my foot in the door, they accept one or they may sometimes like deny long form creators."
