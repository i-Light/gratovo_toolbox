# Brands — Follow-ups

### BRD-FU-01 — Brands are slow: follow up every few days for weeks

Rule (should): Expect about two weeks for a brand to decide (follow up every 2-3 days) and two more to finalize the contract (every 3-5 days).
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "Expect timelines like this: - 2 weeks to decide if they're interested. (while you're following up every 2-3 days) - 2 more weeks to finalize contracts. (while you're following up every 3-5 days)"
Source: lesson video "4.2. Setting Up Your Contracts - Coach Ammar" @1:50 — "Some of the brands will say, Okay, we agree, everything's fine. We will send you the contract. Keep in mind, sometimes it could take five to seven days."

### BRD-FU-02 — No answer: change something

Rule (should): When a brand goes quiet, keep following up and vary the route: another employee, a different angle, a fixed email, a new thread, another platform. A heavier follow-up can say you need an answer to move forward or will offer the spot to another brand — only if that is true.
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "No responses? Follow up, reach out to another employee, use a different approach, correct your emails, start a new email thread"
Source: 3.1. Setting Expectations — "Feeling dragged around? Reach out on other platforms, use heavy followups for example : let them know you need an answer to move forward or find another brand etc..."

### BRD-FU-03 — Brand follow-up rhythm

Rule (should): First follow-up about 24 hours after the first email, then every 1-2 days after each message, and every 2-3 days once a pitch is out; never let more than 3-5 business days pass without one. This is the default of house choice HC-24; the alternative is BRD-FU-15.
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "A follow-up email 24 hours after the first email, then 1–2 days after each email, and once every 2–3 days after your initial pitch is crucial."
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "Always follow up within 3–5 business days, preferably starting 24 hours after the initial email."

### BRD-FU-04 — A real deadline makes a strong follow-up

Rule (tip): When the campaign has a real date (production or posting), use it: ask to settle next steps before it, and offer a short call to go over details.
Don't: Invent urgency (a fake deadline, 'last slots', a rival bid)
Source: 3.2. Typical Brand Responses and What to Do — "just wanted to see if we can finalize the next steps before [insert deadline, e.g., Oct 15th], as the production is due on the 16th."
Source: lesson video "1.4. Sales crash course" @26:17 — "you want to use urgency in some cases not always because here we're doing b2b which is business to business which means the sales are very logical they're logical decisions they're not emotionals"

### BRD-FU-05 — No reply: follow up. No: stay in touch.

Rule (must): Budgets and timelines decide a lot: keep following up on silence, and after a no, keep the relationship warm for when they are ready.
Source: 3.SECRET SAUCE Brand Negotiation - Coach Kushtrim — "If they don't respond, follow up. If they say no, stay in touch. Timing matters so they might be ready later."
Source: 3.SECRET SAUCE Brand Negotiation - Coach Kushtrim — "Brands have budgets and timelines, so sometimes they're not ready, even if they're interested"

### BRD-FU-06 — Brand campaign follow-ups: short, no greeting after the first

Rule (should): Two or three short follow-ups that keep you top of mind rather than re-pitching; drop the 'Hi' greeting from the second follow-up on, and close the last one simply.
Source: 3.Bonus - SmartLead Campaigns - Coach Osama — "Write 2–3 follow-ups. Keep them short. The goal is to stay top of mind — not to rewrite your pitch each time."
Source: 3.Bonus - SmartLead Campaigns - Coach Osama — "Important: After the first email, drop the "Hi/Hello/Hey" greeting in subsequent follow-ups."

### BRD-FU-07 — A ghosting brand: another person, another channel

Rule (should): Write to other employees (ask whether they are the right person and mention a recent creator collaboration of theirs), or reach them on another platform (comment, DM, LinkedIn) and ask for an email or a redirect. When a shared inbox never answers after several follow-ups, or a contact goes quiet after a deal was agreed, a short, polite note to a more senior person asking only for the next step is fine.
Source: 5.Followed up 7 times, no responses, what to do — "If a Brand is Ghosting You: - Reach Out to Other Employees: - Find someone else at the company to email. - Maybe the person you're emailing is busy or not the right person. - Ask to be redirected if needed. - You can also reach out to them on other platforms (Twitter, Discord, Instagram, Linkedin), you can comment under their posts and ask them to DM you."

### BRD-FU-08 — Revive a stalled negotiation with a fresh, true reason

Rule (should): A brand that went quiet in the middle of a negotiation is warm, and the easiest kind to close. Reopen it with a real reason to write again: something true that has changed, such as a season or event, a trend in their market, a new creator, a new format or a new deal structure. Repackage the offer; never invent facts. Keep to the follow-up rhythm (BRD-FU-03). Expect several rounds of negotiation and refusal before a first deal; later deals with the same brand come far more easily.
Don't: Invent news, or promise that a video will go viral; Hang the pitch on a war or a tragedy
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 2" @1:24:01 — "go to the other four and you revive them using what I call a unique mechanism. This is a general marketing rule where you're gonna make something up, don't lie, but find something interesting that changing your process or changing your influencer or changing the market that would be worth giving an update."
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 2" @1:25:34 — "Like people sometimes just need a different approach. You just need to repackage what you're offering."

### BRD-FU-09 — About three follow-ups per brand contact, then the next person

Rule (should): Give each person at a brand about three follow-ups after the first email (two can be enough, never more than four), the last one a closing follow-up (BRD-FU-10). If nobody answers, move on to the next person at the brand (BRD-FIRST-13, house choice HC-25) instead of sending more to the same one. What the middle follow-ups carry is house choice HC-15; how far apart they go is house choice HC-24.
Source: coaching call "August 27: Roast my Email - Ammar" (2025-08-27) — "When it comes to brands, I do less follow-ups. So, we talked about it, two people per brand, okay, two people. Let me just draw these two guys. And you do initial email, first follow-up, second follow-up, third follow-up, that's all. So, sometimes I just do two follow-ups. You know, but most of the time, third follow-ups, soft, strong, closing, those are my follow-ups, no answer, go to next two people"
Source: coaching call "15/7" (2025-07-15) — "with brands, I do every two to three days, and I do two to three follow-ups max, right, and then if they don't, if they don't answer, then I go to someone else in the, in the company."

### BRD-FU-10 — The closing follow-up: the last one, calm, ending on one easy question

Rule (should): End a brand sequence with a closing follow-up that says plainly this is the last one and accepts that the timing or the offer is not right for them now. No apology ('I don't want to bother you'), no thanks to someone who never answered, no guilt. Finish on one easy question: should we circle back next quarter, or try a different kind of creator? Coaches get many of their replies from this email.
Don't: Apologise for following up or promise not to bother them again; Thank a contact who has not answered
Source: coaching call "22/4 - RME" (2026-04-22) — "Closing follow-up, you have to let them know, like, okay, I got it, this is not what you're looking for or something, but not like, hey, I don't want to bother you anymore, I don't want to waste your time anymore."
Source: coaching call "2/7" (2025-07-02) — "So, let them know, this is the last follow-up, you won't follow them up anymore. And give the reasons, like, maybe it's not the best time, something, something, let me circle back. I got so many replies from this last one."

### BRD-FU-11 — Slower after a rate or an offer, faster on operations

Rule (should): Once a brand has the rate, the offer, the analytics or a list of creators, give them a few working days before the first follow-up and space the next ones out: the contact usually has to ask their team or a manager, and larger amounts need someone else's approval. Chasing every other day at this point reads as needy. A warm lead is never followed up the next day. Operational items (a draft, a signature, a go-live date) can be chased faster.
Source: coaching call "17/1 - RME (Brands) w/Ammar" (2026-01-17) — "When it comes to offer, if I represent an influencer, I get slower. If I represent a rate, I get slower. If it's something that's not, like, you know, you're not needy, for example, I don't draft or something. I'm a bit aggressive."
Source: coaching call "September 22: Q&A - Younes" (2025-09-22) — "Brands, they do take a lot longer time. So just have the expectations that it takes time, most likely you're not even talking to the decision maker, they need to ask their team, they need to discuss, they need to hear what's a good budget for this influencer, do you really want to pay for them?"

### BRD-FU-12 — Silence right after the price or the creators is an answer

Rule (should): Where a brand goes quiet tells you why. Right after seeing the creators and their numbers: they usually don't think the creators fit. Right after the media kit: usually the audience's countries. Right after the price: usually too expensive. Asking again whether they saw the email changes nothing. After one or two follow-ups, write with a real change: a creator we actually have who fits them better or fits their budget (check who they sponsor now), or a smaller deliverable at a lower price (BRD-NEG-20). Never lower the price from one follow-up to the next: it looks desperate and gives away all leverage. Any new price goes to a person for approval first (INF-PRICE-06).
Don't: Cut the price in an unanswered follow-up
Source: coaching call "Sep 23rd - Student Success Breakdown (Mubashir)" (2026-09-23) — "let's say you pitch a guy to a brand, and they, they ask for the price, right, and you gave the price, and now they go to you, why they go to you, it's too expensive, right, if you just keep following up, like, hey, did you see my email, hey, did you see my email, email, you know what's gonna happen, they're gonna, you're gonna do it two, three times, and they're just gonna block you"
Source: coaching call "September 7th - Brand Workshop with Himzo" (2026-09-07) — "If they've ghosted me at the point of I've just shared the channels, the analytics and they've ghosted me, majority of the time what that's telling me What's me is that these influencers are no good. The influencers, they're not relative to the brand. They don't like them."

### BRD-FU-13 — A brand that never answered can be tried again later, with something new

Rule (tip): When no one at a brand answered, it is fine to go back to the first contacts after a month or two with a new subject line and a new offer (a creator who fits better, a new angle), or to pitch the same creator to another person there. A brand that once talked to us is not a dead lead: whenever we have a creator who fits what they asked for, write again. Spend most of the effort on new brands, though: a brand where nobody answers often does not want agencies at all.
Source: coaching call "September 10: Review/Roast my Email - Ammar" (2025-09-10) — "I usually go up to 10 people. If I don't hear anything from them, that's more likely like two, two and a half months from first to last. I just go back to them. New subject line, new offer"
Source: coaching call "Impromptu Google Meet Meeting - December 01" (2025-12-01) — "For those that never responded, this is fine, like if you have a brand, like a brand, a whole brand, not responding to you, not a single employee, then yes, this is fine, I approve of this, I do it myself. But, if you're not going to do this, on and on, and not find more brands, then no, you have a better chance closing a deal, or getting more responses, if you find other brands."

### BRD-FU-14 — Follow up in the same thread

Rule (should): Send follow-ups as replies in the thread of the first email: several new threads to the same person look like spam to the mail provider and to the reader. At most the last follow-up gets a new subject line (Coach Osama tests this). A fresh thread is for a new person, or for a new offer once the sequence has ended (BRD-FU-13).
Do: Once a deal is moving, give separate subjects their own thread: a new creator pitch, an upsell, draft approvals
Source: coaching call "December 13th: Review/Roast my email session Ammar" (2025-12-13) — "Continue with follow-ups. Because too many threads to one person. It's, you know, living trace is showing like this guy is like spamming people. Let us remove him. So, inshallah, finish follow-ups. Three, four follow-ups."
Source: coaching call "28/4 - Brands Q&A" (2026-04-28) — "Yes, the last follow-up, I like to play a, play a little bit, and change the thread, just the subject, not the thread, but just the subject line, when you change. Subject line, just creates a new thread, anyway"

### BRD-FU-15 — Coaches Himzo and Ammar: brands get more time between follow-ups

Rule (tip): Coach Himzo finds a follow-up every two days too aggressive with brands: two or three days before the first, about three more before the second, and a week before the last. Coach Ammar also goes slower with brands than with creators, because they have to ask someone up the chain. One of the options of house choice HC-24; the default is the lesson's rhythm (BRD-FU-03).

### BRD-FU-16 — Coaches Himzo, Michael and Younes: one person at a brand at a time

Rule (tip): Coach Himzo writes to one person at a brand and finishes their follow-ups before the next, because two colleagues sitting together and both receiving the email is a bit much. Coach Michael moves to the next person after one or two unanswered follow-ups; Coach Younes overlaps them (one follow-up to the first, then the next person, finishing both sequences). Whether to tell the next person you wrote to their colleague: Coach Michael does, as good manners; Coach Himzo does not before the contract stage, because the two may not get along. The house default stays not naming them (BRD-FIRST-13). One of the options of house choice HC-25.
Source: coaching call "July 8th: Brands Q&A with Himzo" (2026-07-08) — "Some people reach out to two employees at one brand. I personally don't really like doing that. I do one person, one brand, and then do the follow ups. The reason I don't do two people at once in the one day is essentially like I worked in an office before, you're sitting next to your colleagues and if you're both receiving an email from someone, it's sort of, maybe it's a bit much, so I don't like doing that."
Source: coaching call "August 11: Q&A - Michael" (2025-08-11) — "Me personally, I will contact one lead. And then if after like one or two follow ups, they don't respond to me. Then I will follow up with the, I will contact the other lead and mention that I reached out to the other contact that may be their co-worker or something like this."

### BRD-FU-17 — Coaches Abu Numaan, Younes, Osama and Ammar: a light first nudge, then change something

Rule (tip): Before each follow-up, ask why there was no answer. The first follow-up assumes they missed the email and is a light nudge. From the next one on, assume they saw it and something didn't land, so change or add something true: a different angle or sub-niche, the creator's next video, a better-fitting creator offered as an alternative while still standing by the first. A bare 'just following up' on an email they already read changes nothing. Holds for creators as well as brands. One of the options of house choice HC-15.
Source: coaching call "December 27th: Review/Roast my email session-Ammar" (2025-12-27) — "when you're following up, and before you just follow up, think, like, why am I following up? Am I following up because I think this guy didn't see my email or did he see my email and he's ignoring it for a reason? So if he's seeing your email and he's ignoring it for a reason, if you just say, hey, just following up and you make it really brief and he's not going to reply to you because nothing's changed, right?"
Source: coaching call "October 29: Q&A w/Younes" (2025-10-30) — "When you do a follow-up, always think about why exactly you're doing the follow-up. So usually, the first follow-up is because, okay, maybe the influencer didn't see my email. So first of follow-up should be something light."
