# Influencers — Negotiation

### INF-NEG-01 — Know when to walk away

Rule (should): Negotiate an overpriced creator down; but if they are managed by a difficult agent, refuse to come down, or have weak audience and engagement without being cheap, drop them and move to the next creator.
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "If the influencer: - Is managed (and the agent is a headache) - Is overpriced (and doesn't want to negotiate down) - Has an audience and engagement that aren't impressive (and isn't dirt cheap) Then you can't help them. Move on to someone else."
Source: coaching call "October 2nd: Negotiations 101 - Kushtrim" (2025-10-02) — "because he knows he can play with you, or he can chop you in pride, just like that, and if you bring him deals, it's going to be a headache long-term"

### INF-NEG-02 — Pull the creator's price down, push the brand's up

Rule (must): Between the two sides you negotiate for your own margin too: always try to lower the creator's price and to raise what the brand pays, without sounding needy. A fixed price is still negotiable.
Source: 2.3. How do influencers get paid CPM vs Straight — "you should always try to lower his offer, and when handling the price with the brands, suggest to them to raise it for the influencer."
Source: 2.3. How do influencers get paid CPM vs Straight — "This time the influencer sets a fixed price for his offer and that's it, easy as that. Fixed doesn't mean unnegotiable! So no matter whatever price they offer you, do your best on the negotiation."

### INF-NEG-03 — How hard to push depends on the gap

Rule (must): Price at or slightly above your estimate: still ask for 10-20% off. Well above: negotiate firmly but politely. Below: do not squeeze, keep the deal and the margin.
Source: 2.5. How to Price Influencers Step-by-Step Tutorial — "If the price matches or is slightly higher: Fair, but negotiate down (10-20%). - If the price is significantly higher: It's overpriced; negotiate aggressively but politely. - If the price is lower: Great deal! Don't negotiate too hard but ensure you leave room for agency profit."
Source: lesson video "8.Business FAQ With Abu Lahya x Fawaz" — "if the rate is fair, the rate is fair. Uh I'll still try and squeeze something if I can."

### INF-NEG-04 — Walk away without burning the bridge

Rule (must): When a creator is far above the numbers and will not move, close politely: you will look for something in their range and come back if it fits. Keep them on the list for later.
Don't: End on a negative note or criticise their price
Source: 2.5. How to Price Influencers Step-by-Step Tutorial — "Don't burn the bridges, just say "we'll look if anything can fit within that range and get back to you if it does". Keep them in your pocket."
Source: lesson video "2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology)" @25:31 — "No problem. We'll come back to you if any other opportunities could align closer to your budget."

### INF-NEG-05 — Negotiating is expected

Rule (must): Creators rarely expect their first price to be accepted; paying it without discussion tells them they undercharged and that you are inexperienced.
Don't: Answer a quoted rate with delight ('that's great!'): even a fair rate gets a small, reasoned counter
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Influencers expect negotiation. They rarely expect their first price to be accepted."
Source: lesson video "2.7. Good vs Bad examples of approaching influencers" @6:30 — "whenever someone gives a price, it's always too expensive."

### INF-NEG-06 — Friendly on top, firm on numbers, never attack their price

When: Use a budget cap only when a real brand budget exists; otherwise argue from niche averages and ROI (FND-ETH-01).
Rule (must): Be warm and professional in tone and firm on the figures. Never call their price too high — that hits their ego. Put the gap on facts outside them: what the agency can pay for this campaign, niche averages, the return the brand needs. Every such fact must be true (FND-ETH-01).
Don't: 'That's way too high!'; Invent a brand budget
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Never attack their pricing. If you say, "That’s way too high!" you’re talking to their ego, not their reasoning."
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Be strategic underneath: You’re firm on the numbers and confident in your approach."

### INF-NEG-07 — Keep control: lead with questions, never sound needy

Rule (must): Steer by asking the questions and framing the offers; when they push back, acknowledge and redirect calmly. No 'we really need this' language. If control slips (a creator pushes hard or argues the numbers), do not argue: answer with a light, good-humoured line and a short pause to check on our side, then come back with the next step.
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Avoid looking needy: Don’t use language like, "We really need this to work.""
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Ask questions: Keep the influencer responding to you, not the other way around."

### INF-NEG-08 — Target 30-50% off the first price

Rule (should): Most creators inflate their first number; aim for 30-50% below it, occasionally far more, anchored on industry standards rather than your preference.
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Your baseline goal should be 30-50% off their initial price, and in some cases, even 70-80% off."
Source: lesson video "2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology)" @10:15 — "always anchor for the brands, always anchor high. So if you want to get 5k, do 7, and then you come back to 5, it's a good deal. And for the influencers, anchor with the discount."

### INF-NEG-09 — Framings that make 'no' hard

When: Long-term and budget framings only when they are true for the deal at hand.
Rule (should): Use one framing per message: direct ('if the budget is closer to X, would that be a dealbreaker?'), reverse ('are you completely against moving closer to X?'), long-term (aligning now opens bigger budgets later), hybrid (lower flat rate plus a performance bonus), or an early anchor on what such budgets usually are.
Do: Long-term framing only when repeat work is genuinely possible; A true non-money upside counts: a brand their audience will welcome
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "If the budget is closer to $3K for this campaign, would that be a dealbreaker?"
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Reverse Framing Flip the script: "Are you absolutely against moving closer to $2K?" - Why it works: - No one wants to look inflexible. - It shifts the burden of refusal onto them."

### INF-NEG-10 — Justify, don't attack

Rule (must): Every counter-offer comes with a reason that respects their value and is true: what the agency itself can pay for this campaign ('the most we can do for this one is X'), the CPM that keeps the brand's return, the audience share brands actually pay for, what comparable creators accepted (only if the agency really has them), or real repeat business. Use 'industry standard' only when the creator really is above it — a fairly priced creator knows the maths.
Don't: Say the brand's budget is capped at a number the agency will then exceed when selling; 'From our experience' or 'we have creators on similar deals' when the agency has neither; Offer more money for less work when the creator has only gone quiet, not said no
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Justifications smooth the negotiation and avoid triggering defensiveness."
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "We typically aim for $25 CPM to ensure the brand sees a clear return on investment."

### INF-NEG-11 — Three common negotiation moves

Rule (should): Overpriced: acknowledge their value, name the real cap, ask whether moving toward it is a dealbreaker, and get firmer if they do not move. Lowball and wait: state the number once, then stay silent and let them come down. 'My rates are non-negotiable': respect it, say you will check the brand's constraints, and look at making the deliverable lighter for them.
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Influencer Quote: $10K Your Response: "Thanks for sharing your rate. I completely see the value you bring, but for this campaign, the budget caps at $4K. Could you consider aligning closer to that range or would that be a dealbreaker?" -- if he doesn't budge, escalate and be a bit more firm."
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "Influencers hate losing deals—if you're solid on your ground, they'll often negotiate themselves down."

### INF-NEG-12 — Know what brands usually pay

Rule (tip): Use typical brand budgets as leverage when a creator quotes high: health/productivity about $1.5K-5K, tech and gadgets about $2K-10K, luxury about $5K-15K per campaign for mid-sized creators.
Source: 2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology) — "what brands typically pay can help you frame stronger arguments. - Health/Productivity: $1,500 - $5,000 per campaign (mid-sized influencers) - Tech & Gadgets: $2,000 - $10,000 per campaign (mid-sized influencers) - Luxury Goods: $5,000 - $15,000 per campaign"

### INF-NEG-13 — Place the creator: overpriced, fair or underpriced

Rule (must): Compare their rate with what brands pay in the niche (mid-size YouTube integrations about $1K-8K, dedicated videos $10K+; 1M+ creators $10K-100K+ for integrations) and your CPM estimate. Overpriced: counter 40-60% lower (70-80% if wildly off). Fair: shave 20-30%, up to 30-50% at most. Underpriced: lock it in and resell higher.
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "YouTube (mid-sized influencers): $1,000–$8,000 for integrations; $10,000+ for dedicated videos."
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "Overpriced: You'll need to push for big discounts (40-60%, maybe 70-80%). - Fairly Priced: You want to negotiate to squeeze in your profit. (30-50%) - Underpriced: You can upsell them for a massive margin."

### INF-NEG-14 — Five negotiation guidelines

Rule (must): Stay polite and collaborative; never burn a bridge (thank them, keep the contact); no aggressive confrontation — back a counter with logic; mention future deals or repeat campaigns when true; never sound desperate, state the budget calmly and be ready to walk.
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "You either win the argument or you win the sale, chose wisely."
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "If you can’t reach an agreement, thank them, wish them well, and keep their contact info. You never know when a new brand budget might appear."

### INF-NEG-15 — Overpriced: strong counter, polite exit, note who pays them

Rule (should): Counter far lower with a realistic number and ask if it is a dealbreaker; if they will not move, end warmly and promise to come back with a bigger budget. Note which brands pay them that much — those brands are prospects.
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "Don’t be afraid to let them know you’ll pass if they don’t come closer to your target."
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "Take note of the brands that actually pay them that high; you might pitch those brands yourself later"

### INF-NEG-16 — Underpriced: lock it in without saying so

Rule (should): When a creator charges far below their value, accept (or gently test slightly lower) without telling them they are cheap, then price them to brands at their real value.
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "Lock them in at (or slightly below) their rate without revealing they’re too cheap."
Source: lesson video "1.3. Understanding business" @11:31 — "Maybe the guy is. Is charging 500 bucks, but it's super underpriced, and you're just gonna top off the price to 1500 for brand and you make 1k."

### INF-NEG-17 — Lowball once, then let silence work

Rule (should): Make the low counter once and then wait; keep it to two or three exchanges at most, never a long haggle. Silence after a counter is not a no: do not cave and accept the first price because they took a few days to reply.
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "Lowball Once (don't drag it forever) : Then be quiet and let them think."
Source: lesson video "2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology)" @24:43 — "Usually, by the way, never drag negotiations for too long. Long, I usually try and do two and three back and forth max."

### INF-NEG-18 — Stubborn or 'non-negotiable': hybrid or a smaller test

Rule (should): Offer a lower flat fee plus a performance bonus, or one smaller test campaign at a slightly lower rate with bigger budgets if it works; if they still refuse, say you will come back with a campaign that fits their rate.
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "My rate is $7K, non-negotiable." - That's within fair range but leaves you less margin. Action 1. Try a Small Dip "I respect that. If you'd be open to it, let's test one smaller campaign at $6K. If it goes well, we'll look at bigger budgets."
Source: 2.7. Negotiating with influencers (Step by step) — "If they're stubborn ("No, I need $5.5K!"): "Could we try $4.5K plus a performance bonus if it does well?"

### INF-NEG-20 — Deals fail on trust more than money: lead with data

Rule (must): Come in as a credible, high-status agent with the numbers ready (audience, engagement, the brand's metrics, benchmarks). Keep emotion out even if they are rude, show you understand their reality, mirror their tone, propose rather than ask, and confirm the outcome in writing with the data.
Steps: Gather the data before writing → State what you propose and why this brand fits them → Answer pushback with facts, not feelings → Confirm the decision and next steps in writing
Source: 2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama — "Most negotiations fail because of a trust problem, not a money problem."
Source: 2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama — "If they're serious, be serious. If they're casual, match that tone."

### INF-NEG-21 — Ask for a lower price only with a true reason

Rule (must): Never ask a creator for a bare percentage off: it tells them they are overpriced and insults them. Every request for a lower rate comes with a reason, put from their side — and the reason must be true (the coach's 'any reason works' is overridden here by FND-ETH-01).
Don't: 'Can you do 20% off?' with nothing behind it; A reason the agency does not actually believe; Probe a creator's lowest price from a second identity or another domain
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @2:14 — "if I say, bro, can you just do 20% off? I'm telling him in his face, you are overpriced, I do not want to pay you. This is super disrespectful."
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @2:57 — "when you're trying to close influencers on a discount, always justify it."

### INF-NEG-22 — Four true levers for a lower creator rate

Rule (should): Look for the reason in this order and use the first that is true: (1) what they sent — audience countries or ages that the brands we pitch cannot use (e.g. the brands ship only to the US and a large share of views comes from elsewhere); (2) unused capacity — few recent videos sponsored against what they say they can take, so a lower rate fills more slots; (3) potential — a long-term relationship and more deals in their niche, so volume makes up for the rate; (4) the value chart — price follows value, so take work off their plate (brand research, outreach, deal handling; scripting only if they accept it) to justify a lower rate. Production itself stays theirs.
Steps: What they sent: country and age fit with the brands' markets → Capacity: sponsored vs. unsponsored recent videos against what they can take → Potential: long-term work and volume in their niche → Value: the work the agency takes off them
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @3:07 — "Let's say they're not getting as many partnerships as they can handle, you can leverage that as well."
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @4:26 — "if you cannot leverage what they sent, you can leverage potential."

### INF-NEG-23 — Stand on their side: harder to sell at this rate, more deals a bit lower

Rule (should): Frame the counter as the two of you against the market: we like the work and want a long relationship, but we believe this rate will be harder to place with brands; a little lower means more deals for them. Keep the ask modest with managed creators (around 10-20%), and keep the story consistent with what we later tell the brand.
Don't: Push a managed creator for half off; Tell the creator brands will not pay a rate we then sell to a brand
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @20:16 — "we love the relationship, we'd love to work with you long term. However, put yourself with him and be like, We do believe it would be a bit harder to sell this at that price."
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @20:54 — "10-20% less won't make a big difference if you can make twice more videos, right? Or if you can get twice more deals. Would you agree, right? And here, don't be greedy. That's the last point. Don't be greedy about trying to get like a whole 50% off or whatever."

### INF-NEG-24 — A creator resists the cut: acknowledge it, then the upside

Rule (tip): A no to a rate is rarely only about money: it is pride and the feeling of being used. Say plainly that we know it is a real ask, that they are free to refuse and we respect it, then give the true upside of working together. Ask, never demand.
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @41:03 — "when someone says no to your offer or to your price, there's usually a reason behind it. It's not only just the price. Usually it's like respect. It's like, I usually get paid that and what would I change for you? All these things. and you can just pre-shot them and say look we don't ask you we're actually asking you to do a favor you don't have to accept it we understand if you cannot do it it's fine we respect it"
Source: coaching call "October 4th: Deal Breakdown&Strategy: MC&KH" (2025-10-04) — "I understand why you may have anchored yourself this way, but due to the budget that we have allocated to this campaign, this is where we would like to see you at"

### INF-NEG-25 — A student's experience: 'around X' means there is room

Rule (tip): When a creator gives a rate as 'around' a number, they are signalling it can move. (And the agency should never phrase its own prices that way.)
Source: lesson video "6.How Suleyman Made 9.5k in 5 days" @16:21 — "And he said around 10, like when they say around, that's good for you. They say around there, it's telling you that they are open to negotiation."

### INF-NEG-26 — Negotiate the creator once a real offer exists (when their rate is already low)

Rule (tip): One student preferred to take the creator's rate as a starting point and only negotiate once a brand had made an offer, because before that there is no real number to point to — and claims about brand budgets must be true. The coaches' default is to negotiate early (INF-NEG-03); this is the honest alternative when no real budget is known (house choice HC-13).
Source: lesson video "6.How Suleyman Made 9.5k in 5 days" @16:55 — "when you're negotiating when you don't have a deal on the table, I'm like, what am I really negotiating? Because, like, I can just tell him, okay, the brands I'm thinking have 8K in mind. The influencer can just then ask you, oh, but how do you know?"
Source: coaching call "1/1 - Influencers Q&A w/Younes" (2026-01-01) — "If you decide to close him, try to get a brand deal, and then once you have a brand deal, you'll be like $7 CPM. Yeah. I don't have an issue with it. But then it's got to be low."

### INF-NEG-27 — A student's lesson: excitement and neediness cost the margin

Rule (tip): On his first deals a student agreed to a creator's rate without pricing the channel, and later offered a creator more than he needed to, out of fear of losing them. Price the creator from their numbers before naming or accepting any rate, and offer what the numbers support.
Source: lesson video "6.How Abdelmenan Closed 4,500 on his first deal" @26:52 — "I did not calculate his views, engagement, and all of that based off the course."
Source: lesson video "6.How Abdelmenan Closed 4,500 on his first deal" @54:56 — "I could have offered him like less than that because he would have accepted it either way, but I was desperate and I got needy"

### INF-NEG-28 — A student's experience: 'another agency offers me more for the same brand'

Rule (tip): Stay calm and polite; ask whether they have already signed, and say you will try to get a better offer if not. It may be a bluff to raise the rate — do not panic into overpaying.
Source: lesson video "6.How Abdelmenan Closed 4,500 on his first deal" @37:30 — "If I may ask, have you already signed the contract and everything? Because I can try and negotiate to get you a off higher offer than the one you have."

### INF-NEG-29 — One weak point at a time, after the strengths — then let it sit

Rule (must): Never list every flaw (engagement, ages, countries, views): the creator feels attacked and wonders why you came at all. Acknowledge what is strong, name the one or two facts that lower the price, state the number, then wait. Do not chase a counter-offer with more messages; silence works for you.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @51:03 — "you need to focus on one and two maximum About this. Because if you say everything, you say your engagement rates are bad, your age is bad, your demos are bad, your average views is bad. If you say all of this, it's gonna be like, why are you working with me in the first place?"
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @52:24 — "pick one point and deliver it clearly. That's it. Then just wait for them to respond."

### INF-NEG-30 — Before every creator negotiation

Rule (should): Check their past sponsorships (so you never offer a brand they already had), calculate engagement, read the top countries, ages and gender against the brand's market (where it ships), pick your strongest one or two arguments, and set the walk-away number.
Steps: Past sponsorships → Engagement rate → Countries, ages, gender vs. the brand's market → Strongest one or two arguments → Walk-away number
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @57:28 — "research the past sponsorships so that you don't shoot yourself in the foot by giving them brands that they already worked with. Calculate the engagement rate. Check the top audience countries, prepare your strongest arguments, you know, and know your walkaway number."
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @42:34 — "If the brand ships just to the UK, you understand that the brand just wants UK audience."

### INF-NEG-31 — Two rounds: our number first, the reasons only if they refuse

Rule (must): The first counter is short: their rate is above what the brands we work with pay, here is our number, would that be a dealbreaker? No criticism of their channel; the limit is on our side ('we can't do that', never 'you're too expensive'). Only if they refuse, bring one true, concrete reason, framed as an opportunity: few of their last ten videos were sponsored and a lower rate lets us fill more of them, or their audience in our brands' target countries is modest. Two sentences are enough.
Source: coaching call "December 4th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-04) — "It's very normal to just counter without really giving much of a reason"
Source: coaching call "December 4th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-04) — "Because if you start using ammo right away, they might feel attacked, you might look like you're trying too hard, and it's not that normal to do it."

### INF-NEG-32 — With a real deal in hand, ask once more, honestly

Rule (should): A rate agreed before there was a brand can be revisited once when a real deal arrives: say a brand wants to book them for a given month, state the exact rate we can pay for this campaign, and ask whether it is a dealbreaker. Give no invented reason: 'this is what we can allocate for this one' is true and enough; a reason is only given when something real changed (the brand switched product, the scope shrank). If they refuse a number they had called a dealbreaker, the real offer can still be put to them.
Don't: Invent a budget cut, a competing creator or a brand condition to justify a lower rate
Source: coaching call "26/6" (2025-06-26) — "I've talked with this brand, they're willing to have you booked next month if you have anything available. However, you can only do it for 1k, is that a daybreak?"
Source: coaching call "Negotiations 18.12.2025" (2025-12-18) — "so the brand changed the product instead of this product so I used that to kind of justify oh since we changed the product we're going to do 1.7 and yeah this is what happened so you can still negotiate in late and say hey you know You know, we settled on 2.9, however, for this one, we can only allocate 2k without justifying or saying anything, because it can be very tricky, you know, it can fall under lying and all of that."

### INF-NEG-33 — View guarantees: only for volatile channels, agreed before the brand hears it

Rule (should): Ask a creator for a view guarantee only when their views swing; a creator with steady views and steady sponsors will walk away at the request. Mention it early ('your views vary, so we will probably need a guarantee'), and confirm the exact terms with the creator before offering anything to the brand (BRD-OFFER-14). Frame it as protecting their full fee, unlike a CPM deal, at no extra work: if the video falls short, the same ad read goes into another video.
Source: coaching call "March 25th: Student Success Breakdown" (2026-03-25) — "The only time you should put a view guarantee in, is if the guy's got, like, volatile views."
Source: coaching call "March 25th: Student Success Breakdown" (2026-03-25) — "if a guy's getting, like, consistent views, and he's getting consistent sponsorships, and you try to tell him, look, I'm going to do a view guarantee, it's probably going give you, like, no, like, get away from me."

### INF-NEG-34 — Negotiate one core deliverable; packages cost less per video

Rule (should): When a creator sends a full rate card, negotiate only the deliverable brands buy most from them, usually the 60-90 second integration; the cut you get there carries over to the rest. Dedicated-video rates are mostly anchors, since brands rarely buy them. When a brand wants several videos, the creator's per-video rate drops more than the brand's: three videos for the price of two is the usual benchmark.
Source: coaching call "29/7" (2025-07-29) — "He's basically trying to upsell, to upsell you, basically, you know, and all the bundles that he can make. And all, yeah, all the, basically, he's trying to get bundles from you, by just negotiating the one that you're interested in, and then after, when you get a brand, then you can, you know, negotiate the bundles."
Source: coaching call "6/2" (2025-02-06) — "pick two things let's say the 60 seconds and the 90 seconds and just negotiate that the rest is not that much difference and it will go along with it anyway"

### INF-NEG-35 — CPM deals: the creator's rate and cap sit below the brand's

Rule (should): Run CPM deals only with creators whose views are steady. Agree the brand side first, as high a CPM and view cap as it accepts, then offer the creator a lower CPM and a lower cap; creators usually counter on the rate, not the cap. A creator who will not take a flat fee will often accept a fair CPM once a real campaign exists.
Source: coaching call "November 7th 2025: Brands (Advanced)" (2025-11-07) — "I only do them with guys who have good averages, you know, and if they are averaging, and the way that you make money with CPM deals is, you get a guy, let's say he's averaging 400k views, right?"
Source: coaching call "October 8th: Deal Breakdown & Strategy with kush" (2025-10-08) — "I'll get a guy and I'll negotiate with the brand a 300k cap, let's say like a 20 CPM or something, and then I'll go back to the guy and then I'll offer him like a 10 CPM, for example, with a 200k cap"

### INF-NEG-36 — An agreed rate for a deal stands, on both sides

Rule (must): Once a rate for a specific deal is agreed, we never come back asking for more from the brand or offering less to the creator, and we don't raise the creator's pay unasked. If a creator tries to raise an agreed rate once a brand is in, remind them calmly, without emotion, that they agreed to it; most accept. A creator who keeps moving agreed numbers is not worth keeping.
Source: coaching call "27/6" (2025-06-27) — "Yeah, just stick to it to him. If you offer to him, don't go back. Because that's, that's how I burned the brand."
Source: coaching call "6/2" (2025-02-06) — "But you have to kind of respect your agency and also kind of have to make him respect your agreement or your agreement with him."

### INF-NEG-37 — Price on current views, not subscribers or old hits

Rule (must): A creator's price is judged on what their recent videos get (the last ten or so), never on subscriber count or past viral videos: brands pay for what a video will get now. Put it in numbers when they anchor on the past (their rate on today's average is a CPM no brand pays). Never pitch a creator to a brand on organic numbers their sponsored posts don't reach; it misleads the brand.
Source: coaching call "December 3rd 2025: Deal Breakdown & Strategy" (2025-12-03) — "don't even look at the subscribers, it doesn't matter."
