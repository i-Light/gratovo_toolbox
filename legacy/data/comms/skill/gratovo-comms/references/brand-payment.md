# Brands — Payment

### BRD-PAY-01 — Payment reminders: good news first (the chocolate pill)

Rule (must): Never chase money bluntly. Lead with real, positive campaign results, then lightly check that the invoice arrived and offer to help with anything — the reminder is one line inside a warm update. Never invent an excuse (such as a bank problem) to justify asking.
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "When following up on payment, it’s crucial not to come across as needy or overly focused on the money."
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "This approach—what I call the “Chocolate-Pill Method”—hides the payment request inside a layer of positivity and professionalism."

### BRD-PAY-02 — An invoice is required to get paid

Rule (must): Brands' finance teams cannot pay without an invoice. Every invoice has: a unique number (never reused), issue date, the agency's name and address (legal name if not incorporated), the brand contact, a specific description of the service, the total due exactly as agreed, the currency, payment terms (Net 30 / 15 / 7 / due on receipt) and the payment options; optionally exchange-rate notes and who covers transfer fees.
Source: 3.4. How To Collect Payment — "Without an official invoice, most brands can’t release your payment internally."
Source: 3.4. How To Collect Payment — "Never send two invoices with the same number."

### BRD-PAY-03 — Send the invoice as a PDF with a short note

Rule (must): Send the invoice the day the video goes live, with the link (on a CPM deal, when the agreed tracking window ends): a PDF (never an editable document or a link) attached to a short, polite email. Check that the currency paid matches the currency invoiced; USD is the easiest default.
Source: 3.4. How To Collect Payment — "PRO TIP: Always send your invoice in PDF format Why You Always Send Invoices as a PDF Professionalism: A PDF looks clean and official."
Source: 3.4. How To Collect Payment — "Double-check the payment currency before confirming — make sure it matches what you invoiced for."

### BRD-PAY-04 — Payment accounts and what to share

Rule (should): PayPal is fine for the first small deals (about 2.9% fees); for larger or international deals ($500+) offer Wise or Revolut bank details. Share only the PayPal email or the receiving bank details — never card or banking credentials.
Source: 3.4. How To Collect Payment — "Switch to Wise or Revolut when you start closing bigger deals ($500+ and up) to save money on transaction and currency conversion fees."
Source: 3.4. How To Collect Payment — "Only share your PayPal email address with brands — never your card or banking info."

### BRD-PAY-05 — Bank transfer for large payments

Rule (should): Bank transfer is the default for larger payments (put IBAN/SWIFT in the invoice notes); PayPal suits small or urgent payments (up to about $1,000) despite its fees; Wise or, as a last resort, Western Union where PayPal is unavailable.
Source: 5.What are the best payment methods — "For most cases, bank transfers are your best bet."
Source: 5.What are the best payment methods — "For smaller payments (like $1,000 or less) or if you need to get paid fast, PayPal works well."

### BRD-PAY-06 — The brand pays the agency, not the creator

Rule (should): The brand pays the full amount to the agency, and the agency pays the creator. Asking the brand to pay the creator's share directly looks unprofessional and shows our margin; only if there is truly no other way, and then a person decides.
Source: coaching call "October 9th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-09) — "So if you have no options at all, then you could request the brand to do it, but it's not going to look the best."

### BRD-PAY-07 — Common brand payment terms

Rule (tip): The course names 50% up front and 50% on completion as the most common split. In Coach Younes's experience brands more often pay 100% after the video is live, within 30 days, as the contract states. Read the contract's terms before writing about payment.
Source: coaching call "30/1" (2025-01-30) — "The course I believe says the most common is 50 50 But for me personally, I've noticed it's more 100% after"

### BRD-PAY-08 — Overdue past the contract's terms: say so plainly and ask for a date

Rule (should): First check the payment terms in the signed contract. Once they have passed, a warm-up note is no longer enough: write plainly that the invoice is now past the agreed terms and ask when it will be paid. If it drags on, ask for a firm date and copy their finance team. The signed contract is the agency's protection; mention it only if politeness has run out.
Source: coaching call "August 25: Q&A - Younes" (2025-08-25) — "Now, the thing is, you need to double check your contract on how long it says it's going to, you know, they're going to pay."
Source: coaching call "August 25: Q&A - Younes" (2025-08-25) — "hey, this is being now over a month, month and a half, just wondering, you know, if we can get this sorted as soon as possible, because we're still waiting"

### BRD-PAY-09 — Prices all-in; invoice exactly what was agreed

Rule (should): Quote and agree prices as all-in (including any VAT), and invoice exactly the agreed amount: no VAT or transaction fees added afterwards. A surprise line on the invoice reopens a closed negotiation.
Source: coaching call "September 8th - Brands Q&A (Osama)" (2026-09-08) — "Yeah, don't, don't give yourself a headache and oh yeah, we're gonna charge you 6K plus VAT. No, 6K including VAT. Happy, yes, that's it."
