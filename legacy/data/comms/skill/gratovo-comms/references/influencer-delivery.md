# Influencers — Delivery

### INF-DELIV-01 — Brief the creator clearly

Rule (must): Send the brand's requirements in an organized way: the two or three main things to mention, the full brief attached, an invitation to ask questions, and a request to share the draft.
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "Send them the brand’s requirements in a clear, organized way."

### INF-DELIV-02 — Requirements in simple points; fill the gaps

Rule (must): Send the creator the brand's requirements broken into easy points; if the brand gave no guidelines, clarify the expectations yourself.
Source: 3.3. Service delivery — "Be clear and concise in your communication with the influencer."
Source: 3.3. Service delivery — "If the brand has not provided any specific guidelines, you’ll need to fill in the gaps and clarify the expectations."

### INF-DELIV-03 — Draft date with room for revisions

Rule (must): Ask the creator for a draft and agree its date, leaving enough time before the campaign deadline for a round of revisions.
Source: 3.3. Service delivery — "Make sure there’s enough time for revisions (if needed) before the campaign deadline."

### INF-DELIV-04 — Go-live checks

Rule (must): Once approved, confirm the posting date and that every hashtag, link and product tag the brand asked for is in.
Source: 3.3. Service delivery — "Make sure everything goes live on time, including any hashtags, links, or product tags that need to be included."

### INF-DELIV-05 — Deadlines get reminders before they are late

Rule (must): Every creator deadline (script, draft, go-live) gets a reminder, and the creator is chased professionally before the date, not after.
Source: 4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar — "Chase creators on timeline professionally: set calendar reminders for every deadline and follow up before it's late."

### INF-DELIV-06 — Forward the brief complete, links tested, read-receipt in writing

Rule (must): Check the brief has talking points, dos and don'ts, promo code or tracking link, assets, draft deadline, go-live date and where to send the draft; test the tracking link yourself; ask the creator to confirm in writing they read and understood it.
Source: 4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar — "Test the tracking link before forwarding it. A broken link has killed a real deal before."
Source: 4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar — "Get written confirmation the creator has read and understood the brief with a simple message: "confirm you've read and understood everything."

### INF-DELIV-07 — Review every draft before the brand does

Rule (must): Check the creator's draft against the brief — promo code, key messages, disclosure, music requests — and fix issues before it reaches the brand.
Source: 4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar — "When the draft comes in, review it yourself against the brief before sending it to the brand: check the promo code, key messages, disclosure, and whether they asked for no music. Fix issues before the brand ever sees them."

### INF-DELIV-08 — Product campaigns: shipping

Rule (must): Get and double-check the creator's address, send it to the brand with the exact product, chase the creator every 48 hours until receipt is confirmed, then give the brand the timeline.
Source: 4.4. Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Chase the creator every 48 hours until they confirm receipt, then give the brand an estimated timeline for next steps."

### INF-DELIV-09 — Script first, then the draft

Rule (must): No filming before the script is approved by you and then the brand. The recorded draft comes as a private link; check key messages, the promo code said correctly, and music (ask for versions with and without) before sending it to the brand.
Source: 4.4. Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Don't let creators record before the script is approved."
Source: 4.4. Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Review it yourself first: check that all key messages are there, the promo code is stated correctly, and there's no background music (always ask for a version with and without)"

### INF-DELIV-10 — Go-live reminders at 7 days, 72 hours, 24 hours

Rule (must): When the go-live date is set, plan three check-ins: 7 days out (confirm with the creator, tell the brand), 72 hours out (again; call if no reply), 24 hours out (creator confirms; brand told it goes live tomorrow).
Source: 4.4. Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "Set three reminders the moment the go-live date is locked. At 7 days out, message the creator to confirm the date and update the brand: "Going live in 7 days." At 72 hours out, message the creator again with the same update to the brand, and call them if there's no reply. At 24 hours out, get the creator's confirmation they're posting tomorrow and alert the brand: "Content goes live tomorrow, will ping you as soon as it's up."

### INF-DELIV-11 — Check the live post before telling the brand

Rule (must): When it goes live, first check: the promo link works, the paid-partnership tag shows, the video is public, and the integration sits where the brief said. Only then notify the brand.
Source: 4.4. Campaign Delivery - Coach Michael — "When the post goes live, don't notify the brand yet. Check everything yourself first"
Source: lesson video "4.3. Onboarding the Creators - Coach Ammar" @17:22 — "One tip from experience: check that promo code as soon as video goes live. Happened to me, the guy didn't copy it correctly, he missed two letters"

### INF-DELIV-12 — Give the creator a tighter window than the brand's

Rule (tip): When the brand allows a posting window (e.g. the 1st to the 16th), ask the creator to choose a day inside a shorter one (the 1st to the 10th), so a small slip still lands on time.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @59:21 — "I get brands saying to me, Look, we want a video to be out between 1 to 16 of March, let's say, right? Before between the 1st and the 16th. I say to the influencer, look. Just so the influencer doesn't know that he's got much flexibility, I say, Look, you've got to choose between the one and tenth, which day do you want?"

### INF-DELIV-13 — Creator and brand at odds: we are the buffer

Rule (should): When a delay or a demand makes the creator and the brand angry with each other, keep them apart. Acknowledge each side's frustration privately, never pass one side's anger on to the other, and carry a calm, workable proposal between them. It is normal and part of the job. House reading, ethics first: the coach calls this 'manipulation with the right intention'; here we never tell either side anything untrue. Empathy, yes; false statements, no.
Don't: Forward an angry message as it is; Tell one side the other agreed to something they did not
Source: lesson video "7.2026.00.00.Abu Lahya 2026 Q&A PT 1" @35:15 — "It's normal and it happens, and it's good that you stayed on his side. 'Cause that's that's how you deal with those relationships. You're like, on that side, you're like, yeah, the brand, you're right, the influencer's crashing out. On that side, you're like, yeah, influencer, you're right, the brand is just crashing out. And you just yeah, that's that's actually good that you're the middle man so they don't crash out with each other."
Source: coaching call "March 25th: Student Success Breakdown" (2026-03-25) — "when a brand is angry, and you want to, like, relate that to him or something, I wouldn't send screenshots, like, directly, you've got to be kind of strategic"

### INF-DELIV-14 — Our content requirements: honest, matter-of-fact, never a lecture

Rule (must): Requirements that come from the agency's principles (no background music during the integration, no stock footage with immodest images) are stated as what they are, our agency's policy, never as the brand's unless the brand really asked. Say it in one neutral line, without religious language or a lecture. When asking for a cut in a draft, say the draft is good, name the part to remove rather than the person in it, and never invent a marketing reason for it. When the requirement is raised is house choice HC-33.
Source: coaching call "5/4 - Email/Website Support Call" (2026-04-05) — "And I said, by the way, no any music throughout the integration? That's the policy of our agency. He said, this is the stupidest rule I ever heard."
Source: coaching call "May 28th: negotiations with kush" (2026-05-28) — "We had a case, a brother, he signed a deal and everything was ready to go, the influencer was recording the ad, the integration, and he said, no woman, no music, keep it halal."

### INF-DELIV-15 — Raise our content requirements with the deal terms, before filming

Rule (should): Put the agency's content requirements to the creator with the deal terms, in the email and the contract, before anything is filmed: a creator who learns of them only after editing the video has a fair complaint. A small neutral line is enough ('no background music during the ad section'). This is the default of house choice HC-33; the alternative is INF-DELIV-16.
Source: coaching call "All Q/A Calls in October" (2024-10-31) — "He's reading the main stuff and then he's just passing. And it's a bit snaky to put it in there without his consent."
Source: coaching call "All Q/A Calls in October" (2024-10-31) — "If he did it and he edited the video and everything. And now you say remove music, it's like roll, tell me before."

### INF-DELIV-16 — Coach Osama: ask for it when the draft comes in

Rule (tip): Coach Osama leaves the no-music requirement out of the contract, because it draws questions and pushback, and asks for the music to be removed when the draft arrives, as the one piece of feedback. One of the options of house choice HC-33; the default raises it with the deal terms (INF-DELIV-15).
Source: coaching call "September 30: Q&A (Advanced Brands) - Osama" (2025-09-30) — "if you mention in the contract, you might, um, raise up some questions, right? Okay. So instead of that, what you could do is once he gives you the draft, say, Hey, please remove the music."
Source: coaching call "October 7th: Advanced Brands (Osama)" (2025-10-07) — "I don't put those in contracts because from experience and from seeing students work with influencers, what I've seen is you get a lot of backlash from people, right? Saying, oh, why not music? What is the reasoning behind it? Something like that."

### INF-DELIV-17 — Suggest where the ad goes; don't leave placement to chance

Rule (should): Placement is part of our job, not only the creator's: suggest where the integration sits, ideally in the first third of the video, right after a moment that leads naturally into the product (a problem the product solves). The creator keeps their own style; the suggestion goes with the brief or the script feedback.
Source: coaching call "August 22nd- Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-08-22) — "you as a marketing agency, you have to think when is the right time for them to say, to mention brand, it's not just leaving everything to influence their position, it's super important."
Source: coaching call "August 22nd- Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-08-22) — "whenever you mention in your first third of the podcast, anything about crashing, it should be crash, fix, repair, not working."
