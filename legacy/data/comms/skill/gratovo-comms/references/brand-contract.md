# Brands — Contracts

### BRD-CONTRACT-01 — The brand contract is with the agency

Rule (must): Ask for the agreement in the agency's name (the agency runs the campaign for the creator). Check payment terms (amount, method, deadline), deliverables and dates, and termination clauses. If the brand has no contract, the agency provides one.
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "Make sure the contract is between your agency and the brand, not the influencer."
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "Review the Contract: - Check for these details: - Payment terms (amount, method, and deadline). - Deliverables (what content is expected and when). - Termination clauses (what happens if something goes wrong)."

### BRD-CONTRACT-02 — Mirror contract for the creator; the creator signs first

Rule (must): Duplicate the brand's contract for the creator, keep every brand requirement, change only the terms that differ (the creator's pay). The creator signs first; the agency signs with the brand after. The brand and the creator never sign with each other.
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "YOU NEVER SIGN FIRST. THE INFLUENCER SIGNS FIRST, YOU SIGN WITH THE BRAND AFTER !"
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "Never let the brand and influencer sign directly with each other—this protects your role as the middleman."

### BRD-CONTRACT-03 — Every change request goes through the agency

Rule (must): If the creator asks for a change (e.g. 50% upfront), ask the brand; if it agrees, update both contracts; if not, pass on the brand's reasons. The agency stays in the middle of every request.
Source: 3.1. Brand accepted, now what — "You are the middle man, you stay in the middle all the time, and you forward each request to the other party."

### BRD-CONTRACT-04 — No contract, no deal

Rule (must): Every deal is signed before work starts; an email thread is not an agreement. Contracts protect payment, define who does what, stop either side from bypassing the agency, and give legal leverage. If a brand has no contract or won't sign one, offer to draft a short agreement ourselves; if it still refuses, payment before the video goes up is the only safe alternative, and otherwise we walk away politely.
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "If it’s not written down and signed, it’s not real."
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "Legal Protection: Prevents misunderstandings, backs up verbal/email agreements, and protects you from being bypassed by either side."

### BRD-CONTRACT-05 — What to check in the brand's contract

Rule (must): Read it all, twice. Check: all three parties named (creator, agency, brand); start date (watch US vs UK date format); term (campaign length, content lifespan, exclusivity period, platform restrictions); campaign type; deliverables (number and type of posts, length or specs, tags and tracking links, approval process and deadlines, correction window, #ad disclosure, how long the post stays up — often 2-3 years, the brand's right to request edits or removal); fees (exact amount, schedule before/after posting, method, late fees or net terms).
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "Read the whole thing. Carefully. Twice."
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "All Three Parties Are Named - Influencer - Your Agency (you) - The Brand This protects your position. It makes it clear you represent the influencer and that the brand must go through you-not directly to the talent."

### BRD-CONTRACT-06 — What the creator contract contains

Rule (must): Mirror the brand's expectations into the agency-creator contract: campaign type, deliverables, posting deadlines, the creator's payment, edits/approvals/how long posts stay up, penalties for non-delivery, and that the agency manages the relationship on the brand's behalf.
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "Use the brand contract as your template, then mirror the expectations into your contract with the influencer."
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "1. Campaign type 2. Deliverables 3. Posting deadlines 4. Payment amount (if you're paying them a portion of the brand deal) 5. Terms about edits, approvals, and post permanence 6. Penalties if the influencer fails to deliver"

### BRD-CONTRACT-07 — Signing first means carrying all the risk

Rule (must): The order is fixed: the creator signs the agency's contract, then the agency signs the brand's, then delivery begins. Keep every signed contract filed per deal.
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "If you sign the brand's contract before confirming the influencer is locked in, you're taking all the risk."
Source: 3.2. Contract Masterclass — "Save every signed contract in a clear Google Drive folder so you stay organized long-term."

### BRD-CONTRACT-08 — Signing order: the agency commits to the brand last

Rule (must): The invariant every lesson keeps: the agency never signs with the brand before the creator has signed with the agency. Lessons differ on whether the brand signs its side first (4.1) or the creator first (4.2, 3.2); both end with the agency signing the brand contract last.
Source: 4.1. Working With the Brand - Coach Ammar — "The brand signs first, then you send the contract to the creator, then the creator signs, then you sign with the brand."
Source: 4.2. Setting Up Your Contracts - Coach Ammar — "The creator signs first, then you co-sign with the creator, then you sign with the brand."

### BRD-CONTRACT-09 — Six clauses to check, and clarify in writing

Rule (must): On every contract check: deliverables; payment terms (Net 30/45/60 is a maximum, not a promise); exclusivity; indemnification; intellectual property; termination. Anything unclear is settled with the brand by email before signing — never on a verbal yes.
Source: 4.2. Setting Up Your Contracts - Coach Ammar — "Payment terms. Net 30, net 45, or net 60. This is the maximum deadline, not a guaranteed wait time."
Source: 4.2. Setting Up Your Contracts - Coach Ammar — "Anything the AI flags gets clarified with the brand over email before you sign. Verbal confirmation isn't enough."

### BRD-CONTRACT-10 — Terms nobody discussed: ask the creator, then one reply to the brand

Rule (should): Brands often add terms to the contract that were never discussed: usage rights (often lifetime), a view guarantee, performance clauses, longer exclusivity. Don't sign around them and don't answer piecemeal. Mirror the contract to the creator first and collect their answer (INF-CLOSE-06), then send the brand every requested change at once, saying plainly that these weren't part of what we agreed: each comes out, or stays at a higher rate the creator accepts. The usual 30-day category exclusivity is standard; on a first deal it can be granted at no extra charge (BRD-NEG-26).
Source: coaching call "October 9th: Brand Detective Hour - Michael" (2025-10-09) — "It was not discussed at all before. They send me the contract. I have to come back and be like, hey, we didn't discuss usage rights. Is this something that is necessary for you guys? Because the creator is charging this much for it. And then they're like, we'll take it out."
Source: coaching call "November 24th 2025: Brand Response Rescue" (2025-11-24) — "And then you would send it back to them, say, hey, here's a redline of some of the things that we haven't discussed before. Let me know if this looks good to you guys and we are more than willing to move forward."

### BRD-CONTRACT-11 — Asked for company or personal details: answer plainly, never the creator's

Rule (must): The contract is with the agency, so the agency's details go in it, never the creator's passport, tax number or bank details: the brand pays the agency, and the agency answers for the creator's delivery. Some brands work only with registered companies. If the agency isn't one, say so plainly, offer what a self-employed person can (the sender's legal name and address) and ask whether that is enough; never dodge the question or claim a registration that doesn't exist. These details come from the person approving; the draft never fills them in.
Source: coaching call "November 17th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-11-17) — "Because I kind of, honestly, I kind of need to see the contract, I need to understand why they need the passport number and the tax number and whatever. If it is for payment, because they want to pay you, then they have no business having his tax number, if that makes sense."
Source: coaching call "July 23rd: Review My Negotiations - Kamil" (2026-07-23) — "there are brands that require to deal with companies, that's it, they will ask you all these legal stuff to be able to draft contracts and to be able to work with you"

### BRD-CONTRACT-12 — Price agreed: ask for the contract right away, with a true reason

Rule (should): As soon as the brand agrees the price, thank them and ask for the contract, the brief and the payment terms, with a true reason: we want to book the creator's slot and plan the video well. Aim to have it signed within days, even for a campaign months away; a brand given too long may reconsider. Never press with claims that aren't known to be true ('the creator will be booked out').
Source: coaching call "20/5" (2025-05-20) — "you can ask them, hey, we need to book the slots for our influencers. So they have the schedule up date. Can you please send us any details such as contracts, terms of agreement, net terms, and stuff like that."
Source: coaching call "October 1st: Deal Breakdown & Strategy - Kushtrim" (2025-10-01) — "Try to lock in, like, send the contract, be very quick, because sometimes if you give them too much time, maybe they're going to overthink it. And they might say, oh, actually, we're not interested anymore."
