# Influencers — The offer

### INF-OFFER-01 — Questions, then value, then a no-brainer offer

When: The creator has no agent (managed creators: see the manager rules).
Rule (must): With an unmanaged creator: ask your questions, show the value you bring, then make an offer in which the creator is clearly the winner. Re-read the draft and check that it makes sense from their side.
Source: 2.2. Proposing your offer (no agent) — "Asking questions -> showing value -> the no brainer offer"
Source: 2.2. Proposing your offer (no agent) — "Review your draft. Make sure your offer makes the influencer the winner."

### INF-OFFER-02 — Position the agency as an add-on that carries the work and the risk

Rule (must): The agency does the research, the outreach and takes the risk; the creator only does the promotion. Spell out that work so the commission is justified.
Source: 2.2. Why would Influencers even trust me — "you give him value by doing the research and taking the risks, while he does nothing but the brand promotion itself."
Source: 2.2. Why would Influencers even trust me — "you need to justify why he would work with you, and why he would give you that % of commission."

### INF-OFFER-03 — Sponsorships only — no affiliate, no UGC

Rule (must): The agency sells sponsorships. Affiliate deals and UGC do not pay an agency and are not offered.
Source: 2.3. What kind of deals can be done — "We avoid affiliate deals and UGC as these don't benefit agencies."
Source: lesson video "2.3. What kind of deals can be done" @0:40 — "We don't do affiliates and we don't care about UGC and all that stuff because that does not benefit agencies."

### INF-OFFER-04 — Pick the deliverable, stick to the main platform

Rule (should): Know the sponsorship formats (YouTube integration of 30-90 s, dedicated video, Shorts, community post, live mention; Instagram Reels, Stories bundles, posts; TikTok dedicated videos). Prefer the creator's main platform; multi-platform bundles are usually padded with pieces not worth paying for.
Do: Default to a YouTube integration: cheaper and better ROI for brands; Buy the one deliverable that performs for this creator; skip multi-platform bundles; A short already made can be reposted to other platforms for a small extra fee
Don't: Accept an all-platform bundle at face value
Source: 2.3. What kind of deals can be done — "Integration: This is a 30 to 90-second ad within the influencer's video. It's usually cheaper and gives more ROI for brands."
Source: 2.3. What kind of deals can be done — "Usually if they offer a bundle with each platform, there are 1-2 pieces of the bundle that are worth it"

### INF-OFFER-05 — Bringing a creator a real deal

Rule (must): Present the brand positively, be clear about budget and scope, show the (real) negotiation effort you put in, tie the long-term upside to this brand, and keep it about the creator ('I secured a deal for you'). Never imply future deals will carry the same terms.
Source: 5.How to Approach Influencers with a Deal — "Emphasize Effort: Show that you worked hard to secure the best deal for the influencer."
Source: 5.How to Approach Influencers with a Deal — "Avoid implying that future deals will have the same terms unless specified to avoid issues."

### INF-OFFER-06 — Streamers: recurring value, more complexity

Rule (tip): Streamers bring engaged, loyal audiences and recurring mentions over weeks or months (steadier income), but need a longer commitment, more complex pricing, and carry live-content risk; only pair a streamer whose audience truly matches the brand.
Source: 5.Can we work with streamers — "Streamers usually work with brands on a longer-term basis."
Source: 5.Can we work with streamers — "Streamers create live content, which means there’s less control over what they say or how they present the brand."

### INF-OFFER-07 — Start with one test sponsorship

Rule (should): Propose starting with a single sponsorship as a test: if it goes well, continue; if not, stop. It is easy for the creator to accept (no long commitment) and keeps the agency free. If the brand found the rate hard to accept, use the result to come back and ask the creator for more flexibility on price, framed as more deals for them.
Source: lesson video "2.1. What to do when they respond" @7:32 — "what I usually like to do is we can start and then you show him this is just the beginning with one sponsorship, test it out, sponsorship to test. And here, if good, then continue if it's If it's Bad, then stop. And here again, you're showing him you're opening the door to maybe not working with him again. So, not only here, he will accept the deal because worst case is just one, it's just a test, he's not signing a long-term contract."
Source: lesson video "2.6. Negotiating with Influencers (Psychology)" @40:04 — "would you be cool with a 700 flat rate to start and not for each sponsorships? Because the for each sounds like you literally saying, Hey, so can we just lock in 700 and nothing more for anything that's in your way?"

### INF-OFFER-08 — Size the commission by the problems the agency solves

Rule (should): The share the agency asks follows what the creator's answers revealed: a creator with plenty of good, well-paid deals leaves little room (around 15%); each real problem the agency solves — too few deals, low rates, poor-fitting brands — justifies more (up to 35-40%). Then present the offer as a stack of the work the agency will actually do (brand research, outreach, checking past brands so nothing overlaps, negotiating, long-term deals, smooth delivery) against the share they keep (e.g. 'you keep 70-80% of what we bring'). Only list work the agency will really do.
Source: lesson video "2.2. Proposing your offer (no agent)" @8:07 — "The more stuff you're adding here, the deeper you can go in terms of commissions."
Source: lesson video "2.2. Proposing your offer (no agent)" @9:37 — "we will do everything here, and you will keep 80% of the revenue we generate."

### INF-OFFER-09 — A real deal: tell the creator what they need to decide

Rule (should): With a real brand deal, give the creator what they need to say yes or no: the brand, the timing, the deliverable and what they will be paid. A creator cannot accept a product they cannot see. With a talent manager while the brand side is still being finalised, the brand can be named as not yet confirmed so they can check the creator is comfortable with it. What the brand pays the agency stays private (INF-CLOSE-04).
Source: coaching call "December 22nd: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-22) — "He needs to know the brand, he needs to know when, he needs to know how much, he needs to know what deliverable, he needs everything."
Source: coaching call "December 22nd: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-22) — "If he's asking for more details, just give him, and you don't have to be scared."

### INF-OFFER-10 — An affiliate commission on a deal: agree openly who gets it

Rule (should): When a deal includes an affiliate commission, settle with the creator before anything is locked whether it is theirs, shared, or kept by the agency, and say it plainly in the deal terms ('the link is tracked; the commission isn't part of your fee'). A sizeable commission passed to the creator motivates a better video. The agency's margin stays private (INF-CLOSE-04), but the creator is never misled about the link or the commission.
Source: coaching call "July 30th: Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-07-30) — "But you obviously make sure before you lock in anything, you just tell him like, you know, the deal consists of this and that, trackable link, and you don't get commission."
Source: coaching call "27/5" (2025-05-27) — "if there's some sort of like high commission and so on, you can offer it to the influencer. So it gives them that sort of motivation, you know, to perform better in their video."
