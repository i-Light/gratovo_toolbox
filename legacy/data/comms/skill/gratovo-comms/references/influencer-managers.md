# Influencers — Managers and agencies

### INF-MGR-01 — Managed creators: win on price, not on management

Rule (should): When a creator has an agent, the play is nearly always a better price for this deal that you resell to the brand. Exclusivity is only for an unmanaged creator with exceptional potential, and only after one discounted deal together.
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "99% of the time, you'll go the discount way. Negotiate a better price and go upsell it to the brand."
Source: 2.1. Setting expectations — "If They Don't Have a Manager and Have Crazy Potential (Very Rare): - Do one deal with a discount first. - Then consider exclusivity and manage them. - Don't jump into exclusivity right away-you never know what might happen. Better safe than sorry."

### INF-MGR-02 — Managed creator: justify a lower price, gently

Rule (must): A creator with an agency will not pay two commissions, so the job is to bring their price down with real reasons (audience countries, ages, engagement). Say hard things softly, itemize the services you add (brand research, scripting) to justify your share, ask from their side ('we want to help you get more partnerships — open to it?'), and show you want a long-term relationship.
Do: Give a concrete reason for every price cut you ask for; List the work you do for them; Frame questions around their benefit
Don't: Say you want the deal for your commission; Lie to look bigger — it is against Islam and wrecks your reputation; Act arrogant
Source: 2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency — "Your focus here will be lowering his offer with any justification. It could be because of his main countries, age, etc."
Source: 2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency — "Avoid saying “we want to work with you, and get more commissions” and that kind of speech."

### INF-MGR-03 — An arrogant creator: show you have options

Rule (tip): If a creator plays hard to get, let them see — truthfully — that they are one of many on your list and you will not chase. Never bluff about deals you do not have.
Source: 2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency — "just make him figure out that he’s another one on a huge spreadsheet, and that you're not going to waste your time if he does not accept."
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @37:45 — "If you don't want a deal, plenty of fish in the sea. We have currently 20 influencers ready to unboard. You don't have to lie. If that's not the case, just create a sheet, get 20 influencers, and say, look, bro, you were the 90th if our shit, you know, shit, no sheet. You were the 19th in our sheet."

### INF-MGR-04 — Straight cash offers are for established agencies only

When: Only when the agency already makes $10K+ a month and can absorb a deal that falls through.
Rule (tip): Offering a creator a fixed sum for videos and then finding brands to fill it is a risky shortcut; the workflow never proposes it unless the owner has marked the agency as ready for it.
Source: 2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency — "DON'T DO THE FOLLOW WHEN STARTING OUT! (Advisable Only When Making +10K/m) They're risky tactics to move faster, but doing this could lead to losing money or to not close any deal and losing the influencer's confidence, just apply it when you know you're financially ready and have the experience for it."
Source: 2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency — "Hook him with straight offers: Offer him an amount of money for a video or more."

### INF-MGR-05 — Two paths once a creator replies: discount or exclusive management

Rule (must): If the creator has an agent (or writes from an agency address), take the discount path: buy below their ask, sell at it. If they have no agent and real potential, the option is exclusive management: a 20-40% commission on every deal, including brands that come to them directly. Start with discounted deals; after 2-3 deals that prove you deliver, choose between locking exclusivity and staying non-exclusive at the discounted rate.
Do: Detect an agent from the signature or the email domain (e.g. @agency.com); Default to the discount path for the first deals
Don't: Mention a commission to a creator unless proposing exclusive management
Source: 2.9. The 2 different paths you can take — "If an influencer already has an agent or an email like "@agency.com" representing them, your goal is to negotiate their price down."
Source: 2.9. The 2 different paths you can take — "A good scenario is to always start with a discount when starting to work with an influencer."

### INF-MGR-06 — Why exclusivity can be worth a smaller cut

Rule (tip): Exclusive creators give the agency status, make brand pitches easier, and bring inbound leads: when a brand hand-picks your creator, you can offer it your other creators too.
Source: 2.9. The 2 different paths you can take — "Easier to Pitch Brands: Brands like it when you have exclusivity. - More Leads: Some brands only do outbound and handpick their influencers. So if they handpick your guy and email him, you can upsell them your other guys."
Source: lesson video "2.9. The 2 different paths you can take" @12:28 — "get only a couple, like two or three guys exclusively, and then you can get like 50 guys on discounts."

### INF-MGR-07 — Spot a managed creator from the reply address

Rule (should): When the creator's business email is under another person's name (a personal Gmail with a different name) or on an agency's domain, treat it as an agent. Asking a managed creator for a commission fails: the agent already takes one, so they either inflate the price or refuse. Take the discount path instead (INF-MGR-05).
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @0:31 — "if you look at the influencer's email and it's something like a name completely different at gmail or at an agency that is completely different from the influencer then there's a high likelihood like this person is his agent"
Source: lesson video "2.3. Closing an influencer that has an agency" @0:49 — "but if imagine you try asking 20 percent and then the the the Agent makes his money and then the influencer it becomes too many layers. Either they will top off their existing price and give you a bumped-up price and then you're just screwed, or they will not accept, right?"

### INF-MGR-08 — A student's experience: one manager can bring several channels

Rule (tip): A creator's manager or network often runs several channels; when one replies, ask about the others — one conversation can onboard several creators.
Source: lesson video "6.How Suleyman Made 9.5k in 5 days" @29:30 — "These guys' brand had a few YouTube channels under their name. So I closed one, two, three, four, five, six, seven creators with this one message, alhamdulillah."

### INF-MGR-09 — Before offering brands to an agency, check its roster

Rule (should): Talent agencies already work with brands. Before proposing brands to an agency for its creator, look at three or four similar creators on its roster (its website, or ask for the roster) and make sure those brands have not already sponsored them — otherwise the pitch only shows you did not check.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @17:40 — "Agencies already work with brands. So pick three or four influences that they have within the same niche and see whether that brand Sponsored them or not, right?"
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @18:37 — "You need to firstly check if that influencer is working with the brands that you're planning to propose to them, right? Which is easy, you can just do it on YouTube quickly. And you need to go to other influencers within travel niche that are represented by this agency, which you can find on their website most of the time. If not, you can just request the roster and they'll give it to you with no issues, right?"

### INF-MGR-10 — A manager who takes us for the brand: say we are the agency

Rule (must): If a talent manager's reply shows they think we are the brand (asking for our budget, brief and timeline as if from the advertiser), say plainly that we are an agency working with brands and creators. One coach suggests letting them believe it; house reading, ethics first: we never let anyone keep a false picture of who we are.
Source: coaching call "November 12th: Review/ Roast my email- Ammar" (2025-11-12) — "If you think they think your brand, clarify that one. You know, just to clarify, we are an agency working with brands and influencers."

### INF-MGR-11 — At an agency address, still write to the creator by name

Rule (should): When the only address is an agency's or a manager's, send the same personalised email the creator would get and greet the creator by name ('Hi Ahmed', not the agency's name): the agency answers for them or passes it on. Keep the whole email addressed to that one reader; don't speak to the creator in one paragraph and to 'your team' in the next.
Source: coaching call "August 31st - Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-08-31) — "Whether it's an agency or like a manager, I would still say the same thing, I would say the same thing, like I would also speak to them as if I'm speaking to the influencer, like influencer, let's say his name is Ahmed or something, Ahmed, okay, I would say like hey Ahmed, I wouldn't say like the agency name, would say hey Ahmed"
Source: coaching call "August 22nd- Roast my email- Ammar" (2026-08-22) — "If you're addressing a team, you're addressing a team. If you're addressing an influencer, you're addressing an influencer. And also, even if you reach out to an agency, you can still do prioritization, you can still do everything exactly the same way"

### INF-MGR-12 — Talk to talent agencies about brands, not about getting their creator sponsorships

Rule (should): Finding sponsorships is a talent agency's own job, so an offer to 'help your creator get more sponsorships' or 'get noticed by brands' competes with them and goes unanswered. Write to them about a specific brand campaign we have, or about the named brands we are about to approach for this creator. Never describe a campaign we don't have (INF-FIRST-04).
Source: coaching call "August 31st - Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-08-31) — "if you're reaching out to them, you're telling them, we want to help you get more sponsorships, this, this and that, we want to help you, you know, get noticed by brands, this and this and that, it might not align with what they're looking for."

### INF-MGR-13 — An agency sends its roster: price two to four creators, not all of them

Rule (should): When an agency sends a list of its creators, don't ask for every rate. Pick the two to four who best fit the niche and the brands we work with (engagement, recent views, audience countries), ask their rates for the usual integration and negotiate those. The discount we get on them is a fair guide to what the rest of the roster would accept; keep the others on file for a brand that wants someone different.
Source: coaching call "December 25th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-12-25) — "The trick is, let's say, if they give you a roster, let's say 10 influencers, or maybe more, only pick out like three at most, three of the best ones you think is best, and negotiate, ask for prices and mitigate and negotiate those only, even though the rest might be good, and you might use them, only pick out a few."
Source: coaching call "23/6" (2025-06-23) — "But you can get a sense, OK, if I took this guy for $1,000, I took him down to $600, then the rest are probably able to go down 20% to 40% as well."

### INF-MGR-14 — Never go around a creator's agent

Rule (must): Once a creator has an agent, every deal goes through the agent: their contracts usually say so, and going around them burns the relationship with both. If the agency stops answering on every channel while a brand is waiting, write to the creator openly: say we have tried to reach their agency, that a brand is waiting, and ask them to help move it forward. If a deal later seems to have gone around us, don't assume bad faith: the brand may have contacted the agency on its own.
Source: coaching call "July 15th: Brands Q&A with Himzo" (2026-07-15) — "Yeah, I definitely recommend not going, not trying to go behind the agent's back. Most of these guys, if they've got an agent, they've got contracts with them. And in those contracts, they'll have specific terms and clauses around they can't do deals without it going through the agency."
Source: coaching call "September 4: Q&A - Younes" (2025-09-04) — "What I would do is go back to the influencer, if you haven't, and, like, ask him or tell him, listen, I've tried to get in contact with the agency."

### INF-MGR-15 — An agent wants a formal offer first: say honestly what we have

Rule (must): Agents often ask for the brand, budget and brief before giving a rate. When no deal is in place, say so plainly: name the brands we will approach for this creator (ones they haven't worked with), say no budget is fixed yet, and ask for their usual rate for a 60-second integration without exclusivity or usage rights, explaining that without it we can't tell which brands fit and would waste their time and the brand's. Never invent a budget or a brief.
Source: coaching call "October 2nd: Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-02) — "So you need to clarify, you don't have anything at the moment, but you will be reaching out to brands like X, Y, and Z. And then you mentioned good brands that he hasn't worked with before, the influencer."
Source: coaching call "November 20th: Influencers Q&A - Younes" (2025-11-20) — "tell him, in order for me to not waste your time, and also not waste the brand's time, you kind of need a ballpark or a pricing range that you usually do, that you're comfortable for a 60 second integration, so that we know if these brands are a good fit or not."

### INF-MGR-16 — Product before a deal: tell the agent why, and what follows

Rule (should): When a brand wants to send a product before a deal, don't just ask the agent for the creator's address: agents earn nothing from gifts, and an unexplained address request from someone they barely know looks suspicious. First agree with the brand roughly what a paid deal would pay. Then tell the agent what the package is for (to see whether the creator likes the product), that no free video is expected, and that a paid deal at around that rate follows if it suits them.
Source: coaching call "October 3rd: Q&A (Advanced Brands) - Osama" (2025-10-03) — "But you didn't justify, yeah, so this sounds like a bit, uh, for the agent, and the influencer, it's not gonna look good, because this sounds like, okay, this guy just wrote my address, that's what it looks like."
Source: coaching call "October 3rd: Q&A (Advanced Brands) - Osama" (2025-10-03) — "hey agent, we want to send this package of goodies just to see if this would actually suit this influencer, if he likes them. And then if he likes them, can proceed with the deal at around this rate."
