# Influencers — Qualifying

### INF-QUAL-01 — Judge creators by recent views; without a brand, stay mid-size

Rule (should): Judge a creator by recent average views, not subscribers: aim for about 50k; a strong fit at 35-40k is fine, never a whole list of them. Without a brand in hand, stay around 50k-200k views: bigger creators are now mostly managed and ghost unless there is a concrete offer, while mid-size creators need an agency (the lesson's 50k-500k range is out of date, FND-MIND-22; the cap is house choice HC-18). In narrow sub-niches (gardening, AI tools, camera gear) smaller channels with loyal, specific audiences can convert well for the right brand.
Source: 2.1. How to find influencers — "Your target influencers should have around 50,000 - 500,000 subscribers/followers, and more important: around 50,000 views on average."
Source: lesson video "2.1. How to find influencers" @5:53 — "like followers doesn't mean views because you can just see here 800,000 followers, but some videos get 20,000 views. If those videos get sponsored by a company and they get 20,000 views, these guys wasting money never working with you anymore."

### INF-QUAL-02 — Vet a creator before pitching anyone with them

Rule (must): When a creator replies, check four things before you pitch them to a brand, and rate them 1-5: engagement (3-8% on smaller channels, ~3% is healthy above 1M views, real comments; INF-QUAL-08), posting consistency (at least monthly, predictable), audience concentration (at least 35% from one country), and how fast they answer. The 35% threshold is the default of house choice HC-27; the alternative is INF-QUAL-12.
Do: Record a 1-5 rating per creator; Treat slow replies now as slow replies when a brand is waiting
Don't: Pitch a creator whose audience is spread so thin that no country reaches 35%
Source: 2.How to Qualify Influencers - Coach Ammar @0:50 — "When an influencer replies, evaluate before you pitch."
Source: 2.How to Qualify Influencers - Coach Ammar — "You need at least 35% of the audience from one country."

### INF-QUAL-03 — Find smaller niche creators with long-tail searches

Rule (tip): Broad searches surface the same big channels. Use specific long-tail searches, re-search the titles of small creators' videos, exact-phrase and title operators, and recent-upload filters to reach the smaller, better-fitting creators.
Source: 2.2. The best way finding influencers organically — "YouTube's algorithm tends to push the most popular channels, making it harder to find smaller, niched-down influencers."
Source: 2.2. The best way finding influencers organically — "Instead of using generic keywords like "tech review" or "fitness vlog," get very specific with your searches."

### INF-QUAL-04 — Skip creators too small to carry a real deal — after pricing them

Rule (should): Aim for about $500-1,000 profit on a first deal. A creator whose correctly priced integration cannot reach roughly $1,000-1,500 is not worth the work. Price them first, though: a creator charging little while getting strong views is underpriced, not small, and is a margin opportunity (INF-NEG-16).
Source: lesson video "1.6. IMA roadmap & navigating the course" @9:46 — "aim to make 500 to 1000 bucks on your first deal."
Source: lesson video "1.6. IMA roadmap & navigating the course" @10:03 — "if guys are too cheap and it's impossible for you to make money, then try to stay away from it."

### INF-QUAL-05 — Know which kind of creator it is before choosing the approach

Rule (should): Three kinds behave differently. Creators who get few deals answer fast and accept fair offers — the place to start and build case studies. Creators managed by an agency: speak business directly, expect a thinner margin (the agent takes a share), and check the agency's roster first. High-demand creators (the last 10-30 videos all sponsored): only a clear, strong offer gets through, often only with a brand in hand, prices are higher, and they may need many follow-ups.
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @20:38 — "What what what does a high demand influencer look like? If if you open their channel, you're gonna see that the past ten, twenty, thirty videos they're all sponsored back to back."
Source: lesson video "2.SECRET SAUCE Influencer Negotiation - Coach Osama" @21:41 — "some influences, when you reach out to them, you need to reach out to them with a brand at hand."

### INF-QUAL-06 — Only niches where brands really pay

Rule (should): Some niches attract brands that only offer product exchange or affiliate deals (the coach's example: fragrances). Work niches where several creators clearly have paid brand deals; a great creator in a niche without paying brands is wasted effort.
Source: lesson video "2.How to Qualify Influencers - Coach Ammar" @29:08 — "One of what I know is fragrances. Brands are not spending money there. Basically, what they do, they want to do some kind of exchange of value, they want to do affiliates. So, inshallah, you have a good guy, everything, but you're in that space. Basically, brands are not spending money and you're wasting your time. What do you should do? You should go for a niche with proven brand partnership."

Rule (tip): Coach Younes ignores subscriber counts and caps: anyone with about 50k average views goes on the list, bigger creators included, since they are the ones you want most once you have some experience. The other side of house choice HC-18 (INF-QUAL-01).

### INF-QUAL-08 — Check for bought views before taking a creator on

Rule (must): Before pitching a creator, look for signs of bought views or engagement: likes far below what the views suggest, or almost no comments on a big video; an engagement rate unnaturally flat across videos (the same 10% everywhere) or far above the usual 1-4%; videos that all land on nearly the same view count; comments that are generic praise, all about the sponsor's product, or all posted at the same moment; and a creator who won't share audience analytics. A botted channel costs the brand money and loses the brand for good.
Source: coaching call "July 23rd: Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-07-23) — "First of all, if the likes, like, are very low compared to the views, then that's a red flag."
Source: coaching call "July 23rd: Influencers Q&A with Izak" (2026-07-23) — "sometimes the engagement rate is very steady, like flat 10% on all of the videos, then it's a red flag."

### INF-QUAL-09 — AI products split audiences: read the comments before pairing

Rule (should): Some audiences dislike AI products and say so in the comments, which the brand will read. Before pairing a creator with an AI brand, check how their viewers react to AI in the comments of past videos and pick creators whose audience welcomes it. Creators who teach or review software tools are the natural fit for software sponsors: their viewers are the people looking for those tools.
Source: coaching call "May 06: Student Success Breakdown - Abu Numaan" (2026-05-06) — "I would be very careful, like, with these other brands, like, the influences that you pair them with, because also, like, like, they read the comments as they're going to check and they'll be like, we're getting flamed"
Source: coaching call "May 06: Student Success Breakdown - Abu Numaan" (2026-05-06) — "try to find creators that, you the audience wouldn't mind, especially with AI stuff, like, certain audiences, yeah, in certain niches, hate AI, right"

### INF-QUAL-10 — Faceless channels: a real voice, and a product that fits

Rule (should): Brands want a person the audience trusts: a face, or at least a real voice and personality. A faceless channel with a real voice can carry software and digital products (a spoken mention and a link) but not much physical showcasing; one already doing paid integrations has shown its audience converts. Channels where no one speaks at all (music only, not even subtitles) are very hard to sell; stay away from such sub-niches.
Source: coaching call "October 27th: Influencer Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-27) — "if it's a faceless channel, at least he should have a real voice and there should be some personality to it. That makes sense. So, that doesn't mean you can't close deals with faceless channels."
Source: coaching call "9/7" (2025-07-09) — "yeah they cannot for the physical product i think but if it's like i don't know those let's say instantly link in the description they're okay to work with it"

### INF-QUAL-11 — English-speaking creators first

Rule (should): Build the roster from English-speaking creators first: most brands ask for them. Creators in other languages come in when a brand we already work with wants that market (for example a German-speaking campaign), not before. Where the creator lives matters less than where their audience is.
Source: coaching call "29/4 - RME" (2026-04-29) — "when it comes to influencers, we are preferring English-speaking influencers, right? But Insha Allah, you will see down the line, once you have a good brand, you're working with them for a couple of months, they like to explore different marketplaces."
Source: coaching call "November 13th 2025: Brand Detective Hour" (2025-11-13) — "Me, personally, like, uh, the only time I dove into that is when the brand was asking that of me. For example, I worked with the brand, and they wanted to target, uh, like, the UK and the, uh, D-A-C-H"

### INF-QUAL-12 — Coaches Younes and Izak: about 30% in the brand's countries is enough, less in tech

Rule (tip): Coach Younes treats about 30% of the audience in the brand's countries (US, UK and Canada together, or the US alone when that is the target) as good, and less as still workable; in tech, under 50% US is normal because the content draws a global audience, and only under about 20% is a problem. Coach Izak: around 20% US can still sell to a brand buying awareness when the views are big. Either way, name the top one or two countries separately: brands target countries, not 'Western audiences' (Coach Kamil). One of the options of house choice HC-27; the default is the lesson's 35% from one country (INF-QUAL-02).
Source: coaching call "October 6th: Q&A - Younes" (2025-10-06) — "Then most would like minimum 30% combined, or sometimes maybe only U.S. because they want to target U.S. So if you had 30%, I would say that's good. Less than that, it's not super good, but it's still doable."
